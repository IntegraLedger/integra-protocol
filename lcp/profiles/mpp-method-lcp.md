**LCP profile `mpp/method/lcp`: the `lcp` MPP payment method, a ledger transfer the buyer signs whole, which carries H in a field the payer signs.** It serves the `charge` intent on two rails, each a pairing: `mpp/charge/lcp/hedera` and `mpp/charge/lcp/stellar`. Clauses 1 to 3 and 5 of `mpp/charge` apply to its challenge, and clause 10 of `mpp/charge` is the order of its server's checks.

**The method.**

1. **The name.** The method identifier is `lcp`, lowercase ASCII letters as MPP's method identifier is written (draft-httpauth-payment-01, *Method Identifier Format*), with the `charge` intent alone. The rail is named in the request's `methodDetails.rail`, `hedera` or `stellar`, and each rail is its own pairing, whose id's third segment is the method and fourth the rail. The method has no `methodDetails.version`: absent, it is version 1 (draft-httpauth-payment-01, *Versioning*). The method exists for rails whose stock MPP client would itself broadcast a payment that carries no H; no stock client implements it, so a stock client shown its challenge does not pay it. Its specification is this profile, which holds what MPP asks of a payment method's specification: the identifier, the request schema (rules 3 and 4), the payload schema (rule 5), the verification procedure (rule 7), the settlement procedure (rule 7, step 4) and the security considerations (rules 9 and 11).
2. **The challenge** is an MPP challenge with `method="lcp"` and `intent="charge"`. Its `id` is the base64url of H's 32 bytes, `.`, and the challenge's position; its `opaque` holds the seller's own members with `legalContext`, `legalContextUrl` and, where the sale has one, `legalContextAgreementUrl`; its `request` is the request below, written as its RFC 8785 form in base64url without padding; and its `expires`, an RFC 3339 date-time, is required. No request member carries H: H rides in the `id` and the `opaque`, and in what the payer signs. The challenge is tied to the agreement by its `id`, its `opaque` and the ATR's `mpp` slot, as every challenge `mpp/charge` places is, and no attribution memo and no server secret takes part.
3. **The request** is the `charge` intent's (draft-payment-intent-charge-00, *Request Schema*), so a client that does not know the method can still show what it asks: `amount`, `currency` and `recipient`, with `description` and `externalId` where given, which nothing writes to the ledger; and `methodDetails`, which holds `rail`, `network`, `supportedModes` and, on Hedera, `feePayer` and `feePayerAccountId`. The schema is closed: a request member, or a `methodDetails` member, that this rule does not list is refused, `splits` among them, since a seller that declared a split would see it bound and never paid.

   | Member | Hedera | Stellar |
   |---|---|---|
   | `amount` | a decimal string of base units, at least 1 and below 2^63, with no sign and no leading zero | a decimal string of base units, zero admitted, with no sign and no leading zero |
   | `currency` | an HTS token id, `shard.realm.num`, or HBAR as `0.0.0` | the asset contract's address, `C…` |
   | `recipient` | the payee's account id, `shard.realm.num` | the payee's plain account, `G…`; a muxed address, `M…`, is refused |
   | `description`, `externalId` | optional strings | optional strings |
   | `methodDetails.rail` | `hedera` | `stellar` |
   | `methodDetails.network` | `hedera:mainnet`, `hedera:testnet`, `hedera:previewnet` or `hedera:devnet` | `stellar:pubnet` or `stellar:testnet` |
   | `methodDetails.supportedModes` | exactly `["pull"]` | exactly `["pull"]` |
   | `methodDetails.feePayer`, `methodDetails.feePayerAccountId` | `feePayer` `true` with `feePayerAccountId`, the account id the transaction id must name, where the party that submits pays the fee; both absent, or `feePayer` `false` alone, where the buyer pays it | `feePayer` absent or `false`; `feePayerAccountId` absent |

   `supportedModes` is the stock Tempo method's member, and `pull` its value for a server that takes the signed bytes and submits them (draft-tempo-charge-00). `feePayer` and `feePayerAccountId` are the names MPP's Hedera charge foresees for a server that pays the fee (draft-hedera-charge-00, *Fee Delegation (Future)*); on Stellar a server that pays the fee rebuilds the transaction, and the rebuilt one carries no H, so `feePayer` `true` is refused there.
4. **The filter.** `pairingsOf` names `mpp/charge/lcp/<rail>` for a challenge that meets rules 2 and 3, and refuses any other, before any challenge is sent, with the first of these codes that holds: `mpp/not-this-pairing` where the method or intent is not `lcp` and `charge` or `rail` is neither `hedera` nor `stellar`; `mpp/expires-required` where the challenge has no `expires`, or one that is not a date-time; `mpp/modes-not-pull` where `supportedModes` is absent or not exactly `["pull"]`; `<rail>/network-malformed` where `network` is not one of the rail's; and `<rail>/option-malformed` for any other fault of rule 3. `bound`, `reference`, `fetchPresented` and the client hold the challenge they are given to the same filter, with the same code, so these refusals stand for an echoed challenge a client altered.
5. **The credential** is MPP's: the echoed challenge, an optional `source`, a DID of the payer on which no check rests, and the payload. The method offers one payload type, `{"type":"transaction","transaction"}`, `transaction` being the base64 of the bytes the payer signed, which the party that submits sends unchanged. A payment its payer broadcast all the same carries all of H and has moved, so a credential that names it is not refused for its type: its payload is the stock one for a landed payment, `{"type":"hash","transactionId"}` on Hedera (draft-hedera-charge-00) and `{"type":"hash","hash"}` on Stellar (draft-stellar-charge-00), the pairing's `fetchPresented` reads the landed transaction over a reader the caller supplies, `landedTx` names it, every check of rule 7 is made on what landed, and nothing is sent. So each rail's `broadcaster` answers `payee` for a `transaction` credential and `payer` for a `hash` one, and a resource server that must offer no form its payer broadcasts is served by `supportedModes`, which names pull alone. A payload of another type is refused `mpp/credential-type`. A credential over 64 KiB as JSON, or nested more than 16 arrays and objects deep, is refused `mpp/credential-malformed`.
6. **The client.** lcp's `build` holds no key and calls no network. It takes the chosen challenge and H, holds the challenge as `bound` holds an echoed one, with `bound`'s code for each fault, refuses one whose `id` does not derive from that H (`mpp/id-not-ours`) and one the filter refuses, for the filter's reason, reads `rail` and builds that rail's act (rule 12) from the request and the buyer's inputs, and returns what is to be signed. Its `complete` takes the signed bytes, writes the credential with the payload type `transaction` and the challenge as held, and measures it against the credential bound before it returns it (`mpp/credential-malformed`). It never broadcasts and never writes a `hash` credential. Before anything is signed, the buyer's software fetches the record at `legalContextUrl`, hashes the bytes received and compares them with H; on a mismatch it does not sign.
7. **What a server checks, and in what order**, every check before step 4 moving nothing:
   1. lcp's two checks. The challenge belongs to the agreement: the credential's shape; its `id` derives from H and equals, whole, the id the resource server recorded when it issued the challenge; its `opaque` names that H; its `request` decodes; its method, intent and `rail` are the pairing's and it passes the filter (rule 4); and its bound members equal those issued for H, against the ATR's `mpp` slot for a charge and against the digest the resource server recorded for its agreement option for an agreement challenge. Then the payment carries H, the pairing's `bound`. For a `hash` credential `fetchPresented` first reads the landed transaction, and `bound` reads H there.
   2. Validation that moves nothing, lcp's own, since no stock server accepts either form: the pairing's `validate`, on the bytes the payer signed, or on what landed for a `hash` credential, the challenge and a clock reading the caller gives, in Unix seconds, since lcp reads no clock. It checks that the transaction is the rail's act and no other; that its asset, payee and amount are the request's, which the `charge` intent asks a server to verify (draft-payment-intent-charge-00, *Server Responsibilities*); and the rail's own checks (rule 15). Where a rail has a check that reads the ledger, it is made over a reader the caller supplies; without a reader it is not made, and validation never answers that it passed: its answer names each check it did not make, and a party submits only on an answer that names none. A reader that fails leaves the check not made, never a pass and never a refusal of the payment.
   3. The resource server's own record that this H's act, its agreement or its payment, is now taken, made once for each H and act. It stands until the instrument is shown consumed or lapsed, so a submission the network refuses before its ledger does not strand the buyer.
   4. Only then the submission, by the party that broadcasts, a settler or the seller's own server, which sends the buyer's bytes unchanged and keys its record by the instrument the rail names (rule 17). A `hash` credential has no step 4: it takes step 1's first check, then `fetchPresented`, then step 1's second check, then step 2 on what landed, then step 3.

   A payment that fails a check of a `hash` credential has still moved: the refusal states which check its landed transaction failed. After a submission, what the ledger shows is read by the pairing's `status`, whose answers and reasons are `read-status`'s; a settler's answer names a transaction, and it is the read that shows it succeeded.
8. **Problem types.** A refusal is given MPP's problem type by lcp's one mapping, `problem`: `malformed-credential` for `mpp/credential-malformed`, `mpp/request-malformed` and `mpp/opaque-malformed`; `invalid-challenge` for `mpp/id-not-ours`; 500 `internal-payment-error` for a read to repeat (`<rail>/unreadable`, `<rail>/not-found`), a reader that is not for the challenge's network (`<rail>/wrong-reader`) and a `hash` credential whose landed transaction has not been read (`<rail>/read-first`); and `verification-failed` for every other code, an expired authorization among them. The method adds no problem type.
9. **What is not a refusal of the method.** That the echoed challenge's bound members differ from what was issued for H is the resource server's finding, since it holds the record. A challenge whose `expires` has passed is the resource server's to answer, by MPP's status table. A second presentation for an H whose act is taken is the resource server's record, step 3. A submission the network refuses before its ledger, and a transaction that failed on the ledger, are no refusals: the party that submits answers what the chain shows, by the reasons of `read-status`, and `status` reports the same.
10. **The receipt** is MPP's `Payment-Receipt`, issued on success alone and written by the resource server: `status` `success`, `method` `lcp`, `timestamp` (RFC 3339), `reference`, the rail's (rules 19 and the Stellar rail's), and `externalId`, the request's, where given. It carries no H: the reference names the transaction, and the transaction carries H.
11. **What the method does not carry.** No intent but `charge`, and no session or subscription; no rail but Hedera and Stellar; no offer of push, a mode other than `pull` or a client that broadcasts; no attribution memo, the carrier being H itself; no server secret and no keyed challenge id; no partial H; no splits; no `version` member; no key, signature or broadcast in lcp.

**The Hedera rail, `mpp/charge/lcp/hedera`.**

12. **What the buyer signs.** One `TransactionBody` whose `transactionID` names the account rule 13 names and a valid start the buyer chooses, with nonce 0 and not scheduled; whose `nodeAccountID` is the node the buyer chooses; whose `transactionFee` is the most the fee payer pays; whose `transactionValidDuration` is at most 180 seconds, lcp's client writing 120; whose `memo` is H's LCP string, `lcp:sha256:0x` and the 64 lowercase hex digits of H, 77 bytes of the 100 a memo holds; and whose `cryptoTransfer` is one debit of the buyer and one credit of the request's `recipient` by the request's `amount`, in HBAR where `currency` is `0.0.0` and otherwise in the token `currency` names, with no allowance spent. Where the buyer signed the same body for several nodes, the payload is the SDK's `TransactionList` of them, taken only where every body equals the first in every field but `nodeAccountID`; otherwise it is refused `hedera/list-inconsistent`. A transaction id is written `shard.realm.num@seconds.nanos`, as the SDK spells it, and `shard.realm.num-seconds-nanos`, the nanoseconds in nine digits, as the Mirror Node spells it, in whose spelling every read key is.
13. **Who pays the fee, and who is the buyer.** The account the transaction id names pays the fee. Where `feePayer` is `true` it is `feePayerAccountId`, the submitter's account: the buyer signs the body, the submitter checks the debited account and adds its own signature over the same body, and submits. Where `feePayer` is absent or `false` it is the buyer's account, the body is complete with the buyer's signature alone, and the submitter needs no key. The debited account is the buyer's in both: where `feePayer` is `true` it is never the account the transaction id names, and where it is not it is that account. On Hedera one signature by an account's key authorises both its fee and any debit of it, so a body that debited the fee payer's own account would be paid by the fee payer's signature with nothing of the buyer's; such a body is refused `hedera/payer-not-buyer` before the submitter signs, by `validate` and again by the submitter.
14. **`bound`**, the check that the payment carries H, reads the memo of the body the payer signed and compares it byte for byte with H's LCP string in lowercase hex, H being the hash the echoed challenge's `id` derives from: an empty memo is refused `hedera/no-memo`, and any other memo `hedera/memo-not-lcp`, one with an uppercase hex digit or another H among them, since the read settles only on that exact string. Bytes that are not the base64 of a wire `Transaction` or `TransactionList` whose signed body decodes, a crypto transfer of HBAR and token rows alone where it has a transfer, are refused `hedera/tx-malformed`; bytes over 8,192 bytes `hedera/tx-too-large`; a `Transaction` that uses the deprecated body fields `hedera/deprecated-fields`; and a body whose valid duration is above 180 seconds `hedera/valid-duration`. For a `hash` credential `bound` reads the memo of the landed entry `fetchPresented` wrote, and before that read it is refused `hedera/read-first`. `bound` reads no transfer, amount, payer or signature.
15. **`validate`**, on a `transaction` credential, after `bound`'s checks, with the first of these codes that holds: `hedera/transfer-mismatch` where the body is no crypto transfer, or its rows are not exactly one debit and one credit in one asset, or the credit is not of the request's `recipient`, the asset not the request's `currency` or the amount not the request's `amount`, or a row spends an allowance (`is_approval`), or the debited account is the `recipient`; `hedera/payer-not-buyer` where the debited account does not stand to the transaction id's account as rule 13 states; `hedera/transaction-id-mismatch` where `feePayer` is `true` and the transaction id does not name `feePayerAccountId`, or the id is scheduled or has a nonce other than 0; `hedera/valid-start-future` where the valid start is later than the clock reading, which the chain refuses, since it takes no valid start later than its consensus time; and `hedera/expired` where the valid start plus the valid duration is earlier than the clock reading. A clock reading that is not a non-negative safe integer is refused `mpp/input-malformed`. Nothing is read from the ledger: the stock Hedera charge reads nothing before it executes (draft-hedera-charge-00, *Pull Mode Verification*), and a Mirror Node's accounts and token associations trail consensus, so a check on them could refuse a body that would land; a body that fails when the network handles it is the read's `transaction-failed`, and the payee's account and its association are the payee's preconditions (rule 23). So `validate` answers no check not made. A body names no network: the network is the one whose submitter sends it, and for a `hash` credential the reader's. On a `hash` credential `validate` makes the first three checks on the landed entry, its HBAR rows read as rule 20 reads them, the node's and the fee accounts' rows set aside, and the id read from its `transaction_id`, `nonce` and `scheduled`; the two clock checks are not made, the payment having landed.
16. **`fetchPresented` and `landedTx`.** For a `hash` credential, `fetchPresented` holds the echoed challenge to the filter, reads the credential's `transactionId`, which must be `shard.realm.num@seconds.nanos` (`hedera/tx-malformed`), through a reader for the challenge's `network` (`hedera/wrong-reader`), by `GET /api/v1/transactions/{id}`: a read that fails or answers out of its form is `hedera/unreadable` and an id with no entry that decides `hedera/not-found`, each a read to repeat; entries that decide with no `SUCCESS` among them are `hedera/not-success`, the transaction having failed. It writes the `SUCCESS` entry onto the credential under `FETCHED` and returns it; a `transaction` credential comes back unchanged. `landedTx` gives a `hash` credential's transaction id in the Mirror Node's spelling.
17. **The instrument** a settler's record is keyed by is the network, the debited account, the transaction id, and the SHA-256 digest of the signed body's bytes (`bodyBytes`), so that two bodies under one transaction id are two instruments. The single-use value is the transaction id: the chain takes one transaction under it. Who can land a second body under it turns on who pays the fee: where the submitter pays, the id names its account, and only its key can; where the buyer pays, the buyer can broadcast the same bytes itself, or land another body under the id before the submitter submits. A second body under a used id is another instrument, and its answer is the read's `instrument-consumed`. A signed payment stays payable for its valid duration, counted from its valid start, which the chain holds to its `transaction.maxValidDuration`, 180 seconds.
18. **The reference** a read takes holds, from the body presented: the network, the challenge's; the transaction id in the Mirror Node's spelling; the valid start, to the nanosecond, and the valid duration; the memo; the SHA-256 digest of the signed body's bytes, `bodyDigest`, which with the network, the debited account and the transaction id is the instrument of rule 17; the debited account, the payer, which is the signer the read names and the search's key; and the node of each body the list holds, the maximum fee and the transfer rows, the rest of the payment presented. From the challenge the payment answers it takes the `recipient`, the payee, the key of the read's two party tests. `reference` refuses a credential whose echoed challenge is not the challenge it is given, by its `id`, whole, or by the members fixed at issue, `mpp/challenge-not-option`; a body whose rows debit no account or more than one, `hedera/transfer-mismatch`; and one whose debited account does not stand to the transaction id's account as rule 13 states, `hedera/payer-not-buyer`. For a `hash` credential it takes the same members, but the digest, which the Mirror Node does not serve, from the landed entry `fetchPresented` wrote, its rows read as rule 20 reads them, and before that read refuses `hedera/read-first`. The reference holds no member for comparing an amount or an asset with the challenge.
19. **The receipt's `reference`** is the transaction id as the SDK spells it, `shard.realm.num@seconds.nanos`, the nanoseconds in nine digits, as MPP's Hedera charge spells a receipt's reference (draft-hedera-charge-00, *Receipt Generation*); the Mirror Node's spelling is derived from it.
20. **Succeeded** means that the Mirror Node's entry for the transaction id with `nonce` 0 and `scheduled` false has `result` `SUCCESS`; its `memo_base64` decodes to exactly H's LCP string in lowercase hex; and its `transfers` or `token_transfers` debit the buyer with `is_approval` false, the buyer being the one account the signed body debits, what they credit recorded as observed. HBAR rows are read with each account's `staking_reward_transfers` row taken out and `charged_tx_fee` added back to the account the transaction id names, since `transfers` holds each account's total change, fee and reward included.
21. **The read** (`status`, under the profile `read-status`) is the read of the profile `x402/exact/hedera`, rules 11 to 14, on the same signed body and the same Mirror Node entries: one mark, `consensus`, which is final; found by the transaction id; the mirror's latest consensus timestamp read first; an entry under the id decides unless it is a `DUPLICATE_TRANSACTION`, an `INVALID_NODE_ACCOUNT` or an `INVALID_PAYER_SIGNATURE`; the lapse, the valid start plus the valid duration strictly before that timestamp; the search for H over the buyer's transactions before every `failed` but `before-position`; and, until a run shows the three facts that search rests on, no `failed` answer but `before-position`, the read answering `pending`, `search-incomplete`. A `settled` answer states `presented`: for a `transaction` credential, by the members of the body as that profile's rule 12 compares them; for a `hash` credential, which presents the transaction its payer broadcast and nothing beside it, `yes` where the act is the transaction the credential names and `no` where it is another. An act whose debited accounts include the option's payee is the payee's own signature and no act of the payer's. A transfer of zero debits no account and needs no signature of the buyer's.
22. **The agreement.** A payment of this pairing can itself be the agreement, held by its binding alone: its smallest amount is 1 tinybar, or 1 unit of an HTS token, since a transfer of zero debits no account and needs no signature of the buyer's; its fee is paid by the account the transaction id names, the submitter where `feePayer` is `true`, the default, or the buyer; it must succeed, as rule 20 states, and no failed result under the id is an agreement; and a body the payee's own account signed is no agreement, whatever it moved. An agreement is held to no account.
23. **The payee's preconditions,** which no validation reads: the account exists; for an HTS asset it is associated with the token or holds a free automatic association; it does not require a receiver's signature, since a credited account with `receiverSigRequired` set must sign the transaction; and for an HTS token it is granted KYC where the token has a KYC key, and is not frozen for the token. A payment that fails for want of one consumed its transaction id and is answered `transaction-failed`.
24. **The read kit.** The cases of `read-status.json` run through this pairing's read; a case is skipped only where this rule states that it cannot arise, and these are its statements: every case that answers `below-mark`, since every record the mirror serves is final; every case that answers `not-found`, since the read holds the single-use value, the transaction id, and reads the entries under it, an id with no entry that decides being `pending`, `instrument-live`, until the lapse; *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, *a value unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head*, *a value whose validity passed, unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the mirror's one mark is its latest consensus timestamp, read first, at which the id is read; *a value read consumed whose transaction the search has not found*, since the id is read consumed only by an entry under it, which names the transaction; *a read with no means to read the single-use value*, since the read holds the id; *a payment act carrying H that transferred zero* and *an act carrying H that transferred zero*, since a transfer of zero debits no account and the buyer is the account the body debits; *the settled act's answer names every foreign item*, *instrument-live is answered before search-incomplete* and *instrument-live is answered before an item the search could not decode*, since the search is made only before a `failed`; *an act the read cannot place against the position given*, its three cases, and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since every entry carries its consensus timestamp; and, while the search proves no absence (rule 21), every case at coverage `full` that answers `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`, which the search's gate holds, the vectors holding the read's answer, `search-incomplete`. *search-incomplete is answered before not-found* is run: the read holds the id, so the case is the id read with no entry under it that decides, past its lapse, while the search for H has a page left to read. A second act of the payer is run, made under its own transaction id.
25. **What a record of this pairing proves:** the payer's own signed act carries this ATR's hash, a Hedera crypto transfer whose memo is the hash's LCP string reached consensus with `SUCCESS`, debiting the payer, the one account its rows debit, and paid the account it credits, recorded with the asset and amount it moved. It compares no amount, asset or time with the ATR's terms.

**The vectors.** Each file names its oracle in its `about`.

26. `mpp-method-lcp.json` holds the method's challenges, as `mppx` 0.17.6's `Challenge` serializer writes them and its parser reads them back, their requests' bytes in the RFC 8785 form `canonicalize` 5.1.0 writes; the filter's rows, each a good challenge with one member changed and the code rule 4 gives, which no party outside this profile can decide, so each is this profile's schema applied to a challenge whose encoding `mppx` and `canonicalize` fixed; and a credential of each payload type and a receipt, as `mppx` 0.17.6's `Credential` and `Receipt` codecs write them.
27. `mpp-method-lcp-hedera.json` holds the Hedera rail's bodies and signed transactions, each built, frozen and signed by hiero-sdk-python 0.2.10's `TransferTransaction` outside this package, the command in the file's `about`: the bodies `build` writes, the submitter paying the fee, the buyer paying it, and an HTS token with and without its decimals; the payments `validate` takes; a refusal of each code of rules 14 and 15, each a good transaction with one member changed, built the same way, or, where no SDK writes the bytes, stated as made by hand; the credential's own refusals of rules 4, 5 and 16; the order of rule 7, in which a presentation that fails several checks is refused at the first; a `hash` credential with the Mirror Node's recorded testnet entry of `0.0.3229-1791145265-469000038` (2026-10-04), whose memo is no LCP string, and that entry with its memo H's LCP string; the receipt; and the buyer gate's rows. `broadcaster.json` holds the pairing's two kinds of credential.

**What this costs, stated.**

28. Where the buyer pays the fee, it can replace its payment after the server answered, by another body under the same transaction id: the read answers `settled` on what that body moved to the payee's account, or, on a charge, where it credits no account the challenge names as payee, `failed`, `instrument-consumed`. It is the cost of settling with an instrument the payer controls, and a seller avoids it by paying the fee, the default.
29. `validate` reads nothing, so a body whose payee does not meet the preconditions of rule 23 passes it and fails when the network handles it, consuming its transaction id: `transaction-failed`.
30. What the Mirror Node does not show of a body is not compared, so a landed body that differs from the presented one only there reads `presented` `yes`.
31. Until a run shows the three facts the search for H rests on, the read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, where it would answer `failed`, but for `before-position`.
32. A `hash` credential's `transactionId` is read only in the spelling with nine digits of nanoseconds; another spelling is refused `hedera/tx-malformed`.
33. The pairing's `confirmed` is `no`: nothing outside lcp has yet shown a body of this form landing with H in its memo. A recorded run through a submitter, or a sample the method's own publication shows, would show it.
