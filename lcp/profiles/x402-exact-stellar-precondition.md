**LCP profile `x402/exact/stellar/precondition`: the agreement on Stellar under x402, the ATR hash in a signed-payload precondition.**

The form is an agreement and never a charge: a buyer's own act over H that a public ledger shows, made before the sale's charge is paid. It is offered as an x402 `exact` option on a Stellar network and is told from every other Stellar option by `extra.assetTransferMethod`.

**The option**

1. The option is `scheme` `exact`, `network` `stellar:pubnet` or `stellar:testnet`, `asset` the contract strkey (`C…`) of a Stellar Asset Contract, `payTo` the payee's account strkey (`G…`), `maxTimeoutSeconds` a positive integer whose fifth, rounded up, is at most 2^32 − 1, and `extra` an object holding `assetTransferMethod` `"lcp-precondition"` and `areFeesSponsored` `true`. `amount` is a decimal integer below 2^127 with no sign and no leading zero: `"0"` where the settler declares that it takes zero, and otherwise the token's smallest unit, which the resource server sets and nothing checks. An option whose `extra.assetTransferMethod` is absent or another value is not this form's. An option of this form that fails another member is refused `x402/option-malformed`, and one whose `payTo` is a muxed account (`M…`) `stellar/option-malformed`.
2. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, H being the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes as `0x` and lowercase hex and L an `https` URL that serves those bytes. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received and signs nothing unless it equals H.

**Where H rides, and what the buyer signs**

3. The payment is one Stellar transaction whose one operation is `invokeHostFunction` calling `transfer(buyer, payTo, amount)` on `asset`, authorised by the buyer's Soroban authorization entry with address credentials (`SOROBAN_CREDENTIALS_ADDRESS` or `SOROBAN_CREDENTIALS_ADDRESS_V2`), whose root invocation is that same call with no sub-invocations. The buyer is an account (`G…`); any other credential arm, and a contract buyer, is refused before anything is signed.
4. The transaction's preconditions are `PRECOND_V2` holding exactly one signer of type `SIGNER_KEY_TYPE_ED25519_SIGNED_PAYLOAD` (CAP-40) in `extraSigners`: the buyer's Ed25519 key, with the **payload** of exactly 40 bytes: H's 32 bytes, then the authorization entry's `nonce` as its XDR `int64`, 8 bytes big-endian two's complement. H rides whole in what the buyer signs; the payload is never 32 bytes, and binds the buyer's own single-use value beside H. `PRECOND_V2` keeps the time and ledger bounds the transaction already carried.
5. The buyer's key signs two things: SHA-256 of the entry's `HashIDPreimage` (`ENVELOPE_TYPE_SOROBAN_AUTHORIZATION`, or its V2 form with the address) for the network's id, the entry's nonce, its expiration ledger and its invocation; and the 40-byte payload itself, which the signed-payload signer verifies (CAP-40). The entry's signature is written as a Stellar account's is, a vector of one map `{public_key, signature}`; the payload's rides in the presented envelope's `signatures` as a decorated signature whose hint is the key's last 4 bytes XOR the payload's last 4 bytes. The entry's expiration ledger is the current ledger plus `ceil(maxTimeoutSeconds / 5)`.
6. The client builds from the transaction the buyer's wallet simulated, writes the entry's expiration and the precondition, and hands both requests to the buyer's own signer; it signs nothing and calls no network. The payment is x402's `PaymentPayload` with `payload` `{"transaction": <the base64 envelope>}` and `extensions` echoed unchanged.

**What the client and the resource server refuse, before anything moves**

7. The envelope is the strict base64 of a `TransactionEnvelope` v1, or a fee bump of one, of at most 8,192 characters (`stellar/tx-too-large`), read whole with no trailing byte (`stellar/tx-malformed`); its operation and the buyer's entry nest ScVal vectors and maps, and authorized invocations, at most 64 levels (`stellar/tx-malformed`). An envelope whose operation is not exactly the call the buyer's entry signs, or is not one `transfer`, is `stellar/not-one-transfer`; one in which the buyer's entry cannot be identified, or is not an address entry of an account, `stellar/no-address-auth`.
8. The client refuses, before the buyer signs: a simulated transfer whose contract is not the option's `asset` (`stellar/asset-mismatch`), whose amount is not the option's `amount` (`stellar/amount-mismatch`), whose `from` is not the buyer the caller names (`stellar/payer-mismatch`), whose `to` is not the option's plain `payTo`, or whose `from` is the option's `payTo` (`stellar/payee-mismatch`); and a simulated transaction that is a fee bump, already holds an extra signer, or whose current ledger or expiration is not a ledger sequence (`stellar/tx-malformed`).
9. The resource server's check of a presented payment (`bound`) refuses: preconditions holding no signed-payload signer, or more than one (`stellar/no-carrier`); a payload that is not exactly 40 bytes, whose last 8 bytes are not the buyer's entry's nonce, or whose key is not a key whose signature the buyer's entry carries (`stellar/carrier-mismatch`); and a transfer whose `from` is the option's `payTo` (`stellar/payee-mismatch`). It gives H, the payload's first 32 bytes, which the resource server compares with the H it issued. It checks how the payment carries H, never what it pays.
10. The read keys (`reference`) refuse a presented payment whose `accepted` is not the option given, compared as RFC 8785 JSON (`x402/accepted-not-option`).

**What a settler checks and does**

11. A settler builds the transaction it submits around the buyer's operation and entry, as its source, paying the fee, with the buyer's precondition unchanged, and carries the buyer's payload signature into the envelope's signatures beside its own. It holds the transfer's `to` to the option's `payTo`. A transaction submitted without the payload signature, or with a wrong one, is refused at submission (`txBAD_AUTH`); the signed-payload check reads no ledger state.

**The single-use value, the instrument and the lapse**

12. The single-use value is the buyer's entry's nonce, which only a successful landing of the entry consumes. A transaction included with a failed result consumes no nonce and emits no event; the entry can then land again, with the precondition or without. The entry lapses after its expiration ledger: a ledger above it closed with the nonce unconsumed.

**The agreement's rules**

13. Floor: zero. The Stellar Asset Contract refuses only a negative amount, still requires the buyer's authorization, and emits its event at zero. The fee is the settler's, as the transaction's source; the buyer needs a key, and no funds where the amount is zero. The agreement holds only where the transaction succeeded (`getTransaction` answers `SUCCESS`, or the inner success under a fee bump, with `INVOKE_HOST_FUNCTION_SUCCESS`) and the five checks below hold. A transaction the option's payee signed is no agreement. An entry can land in an envelope with no precondition, since its signature covers the network, the nonce, the expiration ledger and the invocation, never the envelope: such a landing carries no H and consumes the entry. At zero it moves nothing; at the smallest unit it moves that one unit with no H, a cost stated to sellers.

**The read**

14. The five checks, made on the landed transaction, all required: (1) it succeeded; (2) its preconditions hold exactly one signed-payload signer, whose payload is exactly 40 bytes, H then the nonce of the authorization entry the transaction consumed; (3) the payload's key is a key whose signature that entry carries; (4) the transfer's `from` is not the option's `payTo`; (5) the buyer is an account whose entry's credentials are `ADDRESS` or `ADDRESS_V2`. A transaction that passes them for H and the buyer is the agreement, whatever amount or asset it moved and whichever of the buyer's entries it consumed; an agreement is held to no account.
15. The read goes through a Stellar RPC that holds history back to the ledger the search starts from: `getNetwork`, `getLatestLedger`, `getEvents` and `getTransaction`. It finds the buyer's acts with `getEvents` on every contract, by a filter that names none: `transfer` events whose `from` topic is the buyer and `burn` events whose `from` topic is the buyer, from the ledger the caller gives as the search's start to the latest closed ledger at the read. No filter names the payee or the asset. Each candidate's transaction is read whole by `getTransaction`. A stranger can emit events naming the buyer, so at most 50 candidates are read in one call; past them the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`, and the next call resumes at the position returned. A throttled or failed page is never read as absence.
16. The mark is one, `finalized`: a transaction in a closed ledger, which is final; the latest closed ledger is the latest head. Positions are written as the position P of the transaction (below); the position the caller gives as the one the transaction must come after is a ledger sequence, and a transaction in that ledger or below answers `failed`, `before-position`.
17. A transaction found that consumed the entry's nonce and is not the agreement, one that carries no H among them, is the transaction of `failed`, `instrument-consumed`. With the search read through to the latest closed ledger and the nonce unconsumed, the answer is `pending`, `instrument-live` before the expiration ledger has passed, and `failed`, `instrument-unused` after it. A search left with range to read, or that met an item it cannot decode, given by the caller or not, reads the nonce neither consumed nor unconsumed: `pending`, `search-incomplete`. Until a run shows that the event search with no contract named holds every act of the buyer, the read proves no absence, and every answer the search decides, each `failed` reason but `before-position`, is `pending`, `search-incomplete`.
18. On `settled` the answer states `presented`: `yes` where the landed entry's `HashIDPreimage` digest and the landed payload are those presented, `no` where either differs. Each transfer is named by its transaction and by the event's id as the RPC gives it; what moved is the event's contract, `to` (its base account, with `to_muxed_id` where the event carries one) and `amount`, recorded as observed.
19. The position P of a transaction is `ledger` × 2^32 + `order` × 2^12, an unsigned 64-bit integer whose low 12 bits are zero: `ledger` the sequence of the closed ledger that holds it, `order` its application order in that ledger, counted from 1, at most 1,048,575; written as a decimal string with no leading zero. A reader given P refuses nonzero low bits and an order of 0 (`stellar/position-malformed`).

**What a record proves**

20. "Signed: the buyer's account signed a Soroban authorization of a transfer and the 40-byte payload of this ATR's hash and that authorization's nonce, carried as a signed-payload precondition of the transaction that consumed it, so the buyer's own signed act carries the hash. The transaction succeeded on the asset contract its event names." Where the transfer moved a nonzero amount, "paid" in place of "signed", with the account the value reached. It states nothing of the amount, nor that the asset, the account reached or the time meet a term of the ATR.

**The read kit**

21. The read runs the cases of `read-status.json` on the act `agreement` alone, the form being offered as an agreement only. These cases cannot arise on this form, and its vectors skip them: every case answering `below-mark`, and the cases opening *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, *a value unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head*, *a value whose validity passed, unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the one mark, `finalized`, is the latest closed ledger, the latest head, at which the nonce is read (rule 16); every case answering `transaction-failed`, and those opening *a failed result that consumed the value*, since a `FAILED` result consumes no nonce (rule 12); every case answering `not-found`, and those opening *a read with no means to read the single-use value*, since every read holds the nonce (rule 17); those opening *an act the read cannot place against the position given* and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since every transaction the read meets is in a closed ledger, at its position P (rule 19); those opening *a value read consumed whose transaction the search has not found*, *instrument-live is answered before search-incomplete*, *instrument-live is answered before an item the search could not decode* and *an item the caller gave by its transaction holds no search though the read cannot decode it*, since the nonce is read only by the search read through (rule 17); and, until a run shows that the event search with no contract named holds every act of the buyer, each case whose answer is `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`, which this read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete` (rule 17). These arise and run: an act that transferred zero, since the Stellar Asset Contract transfers zero and emits its event; *the settled act's answer names every foreign item*, since every read makes its search; and *search-incomplete is answered before not-found*.

**Vectors**

22. `x402-exact-stellar-precondition.json` holds the payload, the signed-payload signer, the build of the transaction the buyer signs byte for byte, bound, reference, and one refusal vector for each refusal of rules 1, 8, 9 and 10, each a good input with one member changed; `stellar-position.json` holds the position's compositions, Horizon's `paging_token` examples parsed to their ledgers, its refusals and `M(payee, P)`. Their oracle is stellar-sdk 13.0.0 for Python, run outside this package, and each file's `about` names the command; the refusal codes are this profile's.
