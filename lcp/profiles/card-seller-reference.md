**LCP profile `card/seller-reference`: the ATR hash in the seller's own processor metadata, the payment named by the seller's reference for the charge.** This profile defines the card checkout in which nothing the payer signs carries H, Amex's included: the seller writes the link into its own processor's record and names the charge by its own reference for it.

1. Before payment the seller shows `{"legalContext":{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}}`, where H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes (`0x`, lowercase hex) and L an `https` URL serving them, with `legalContextAgreementUrl` after L where an agreement URL is given. The card option offered is `{"scheme":"seller-reference","checkout":C}`, C the seller's own checkout id, 1 to 128 visible ASCII characters.
2. The buyer's agent fetches L, hashes what it receives and compares with H as 32 decoded bytes; on a mismatch it does not pay.
3. Nothing the payer signs carries H: the card authorization does not, and no card network carries H in anything that reaches the network or the statement, so `build` and `bound` refuse `card/no-signed-place`. The pairing is at coverage `none`: an agreement carrying H, a payment of an x402 or MPP pairing whose payment is itself a public proof, is made first, and the seller charges only once that agreement has landed.
4. The seller writes `lcp:sha256:` + H in its processor's own metadata under the key `legal_context`: Stripe's `metadata`, Adyen's `metadata`, a Braintree custom field, Cybersource's `merchantDefinedInformation`. That value is a link, written by the payee, and never proof.
5. The seller names the charge its processor made by its own reference for it, the charge reference: the processor's identifier of the payment, such as a Stripe PaymentIntent's `id` (`pi_3MtwBwLkdIwHu7ix28a3tqPa` in Stripe's published PaymentIntent object) or an Adyen `pspReference`. What the seller presents of the charge is `{"charge":R}`, R being that reference, 1 to 255 visible ASCII characters (`MAX_CHARGE_REFERENCE`).
6. `reference` takes that presentation with the card option the charge answers and gives `{"checkout":C,"charge":R}`. A presentation that is not an object whose `charge` has rule 5's form is `card/charge-reference-malformed`; then an option that is not a card option of the `seller-reference` scheme is `card/option-malformed`. It compares nothing the charge moved with the option, and reads no payment: the payment is known from the seller's report alone, which names it by R.
7. The payee broadcasts: the seller submits the charge to its processor. That the processor returns the metadata unchanged has not been shown by a run, so the form is not confirmed.
