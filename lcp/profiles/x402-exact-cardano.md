**LCP profile `x402/exact/cardano` and `x402/exact/cardano/lock`: the ATR hash as a CIP-20 message.**

Two pairings carry H the same way. `x402/exact/cardano` is x402's address-to-address method: the option's `extra.assetTransferMethod` is absent or `"default"`. `x402/exact/cardano/lock` is x402's two lock methods, `"masumi"` (Masumi's `vested_pay` escrow) and `"script"` (a contract the resource server defines): a deposit into a contract, which is a charge and never an agreement. The member tells the two apart; an option naming another method is neither's.

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`.
2. The client fetches L, hashes the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client builds the `exact` payment with a TTL, attaches metadata label 674 = `{"msg": ["lcp:sha256:0x", <H's 64 lowercase hex digits>]}` with its `auxiliary_data_hash`, signs, does not broadcast, and echoes `extensions` unchanged. This client always writes a TTL.
4. The server refuses a payment whose signed body does not commit to exactly one such message carrying an H it issued for that request and has not seen claimed, before it calls a settler. A settler then applies the published verifier's rules, which are x402's Cardano scheme's (*Facilitator Verification Rules*), and submits.
5. H is on chain in the transaction's metadata, readable by transaction id.
6. A client that does not implement this profile writes no such message, and rule 4 refuses its payment before it is verified or settled.

**The option and the payment**

7. The option's `extra.areFeesSponsored`, where present, is `false`: the published scheme sponsors no fee, and an option that says otherwise is refused at the filter. `extra.paymentFlow` is absent or `"authorization"`. The networks are `cardano:mainnet`, `cardano:preprod` and `cardano:preview`, and the three CIP-34 forms are read as the same networks.
8. The payment is `{transaction, nonce}`: the signed, unbroadcast transaction in base64, and the input `nonce` names, `<transaction id>#<index>`, which must be one of the inputs the body spends. That input is the payment's single-use value: once a transaction spends it, this body can never land.
9. The payment's reference names the network, the transaction id (the Blake2b-256 of the body as received), the input, the method, H, the body's TTL slot where it has one, and the option's payee: its `payTo`, or on a Masumi lock the seller its `extra.terms.sellerAddress` names, `payTo` there being the escrow every seller of a deployment shares, which the reference keeps beside the seller. A Masumi seller is the address of a public-key payment credential, as the scheme's verification rules require, and its escrow an address with a payment credential; an option otherwise is `x402/option-malformed`. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not the option it is given. On the address method a body with no TTL is admitted, as the published verifier admits it (`@x402/cardano` 2.30.0 checks a TTL only where the body has one), and its reference names none; a lock refuses it, since the scheme requires a TTL of a lock.
10. The payment presented is the body the payer signed, its witnesses apart. A landed transaction is the payment presented exactly where its id is the reference's: a `settled` answer states `presented` `yes` where the act's id is the reference's and `no` where it is another, and never `unshown`, the id being the body's digest.

**The agreement (`x402/exact/cardano` only)**

11. A payment of the address method can be the sale's agreement: the same signed transaction, carrying H in label 674, paying the offer's payee directly. Its floor is a rule, the ledger's smallest output to the payee: `(160 + |serialized output|) × coinsPerUtxoByte`, about 1 ADA, the output being `{0: the payee's address, 1: value}` in the post-Alonzo map form, its value the coin alone for a lovelace option, or the coin and one unit of the option's token, and its coin the least that covers its own encoding. The resource server evaluates it when it issues the agreement's offer, from `coinsPerUtxoByte` read from its own Cardano endpoint, and never stores it as a number; where that read fails, the option is left out of that offer. `advertise` on the address method takes `coinsPerUtxoByte` after the offer, before the agreement URL, evaluates the floor as `cardanoFloor` does, and refuses a lovelace offer whose amount is below it (`cardano/amount-below-floor`) and a parameter, `payTo` or asset the rule cannot read (`cardano/floor-input`). An amount below the floor cannot be built. The ADA such an output holds travels to the seller with a token amount.
12. The buyer pays the fee, a field of the body balanced against its own inputs (about 0.17 ADA); the published scheme sponsors none.
13. The agreement succeeded where the transaction whose id is the Blake2b-256 of the signed body is in a block at the mark read with `valid_contract` true, and its label 674 holds H. What its outputs pay is recorded as observed and compared with nothing; an agreement is a proof and is held to no account. A transaction whose payer is the option's payee is no agreement.

**The read**

14. `status` reads through an indexer the caller supplies (Blockfrost or Koios), holding the chain from genesis. Marks are counts of confirmations, the blocks after the transaction's own, from 0, the transaction included at the tip, to the ledger's security parameter read from the endpoint's genesis (2160 on mainnet and Preprod, 432 on Preview), which is the final mark. Positions are block heights. The position given is read first: an endpoint that answers no read of the block at it is `unreadable`, `history-gap`; where none is given, the genesis block shows the history.
15. What identifies the act: H in label 674, by this package's decoder; `valid_contract` true; the payer, the payment credential that holds the reference's input, among the payment credentials of the transaction's inputs, and the payee's not among them (one that is, is `payee-signed`); the act's kind, a deposit into a contract (an output to a script credential with an inline datum) being the lock's and no deposit the address method's; and, on a payment, an output whose address has the payee's payment credential, whatever its stake part, or on a Masumi lock a deposit into the reference's escrow whose inline datum, the scheme's `masumi.vested_pay.v2` (Plutus `Constr 0` over 19 fields), names in field 2, `seller`, an address with the payee's payment credential; a datum that does not decode names no account. Only a deposit into that escrow names a seller: any other output, on every method, names its own payment credential, so a datum naming the seller on a deposit into another script, or on an output to a key, names no seller, and an output to the seller's own credential is a transfer to the payee. On a `script` lock an output pays where its address has the payment credential of `payTo`.
16. What moved is each output to another credential than the payer's, its asset (`lovelace`, or `<policy id>.<asset name>` in hex) and amount, the payment credential it reached (on a Masumi lock, the beneficiary the datum names, also recorded as its `beneficiary`), and the transaction's id with the output's index among the body's outputs as its place, recorded as observed. The read compares no amount, asset or time with the option.
17. The single-use value is the reference's input, read through the indexer: unspent, or spent and by which transaction. An unspent input is read at the latest head only where the indexer that answered held the tip read first; otherwise the read never answers `instrument-live`. The input is consumed at the final mark where the transaction that spent it has the security parameter's confirmations. A transaction consumed it where it is valid and spends it, or is invalid and took it as collateral. A phase-2-invalid transaction that left the input unspent consumed nothing: the answer is `pending`, `instrument-live` until the TTL passes, and then the lapse's.
18. The lapse: the slot of the block the security parameter below the tip is at or past the body's TTL, the transaction absent from a lookup that covers the tip read first, and the input unspent. Below the final mark (the tip's own slot at or past the TTL, that block's not) the answer is `pending`, `below-mark`. A body with no TTL never lapses: it is `pending`, `instrument-live` while its input is unspent.
19. The search for H, before every `failed` but `before-position`: an act of the payer spends an input its payment credential holds, so the search lists that credential's transactions from the block after the position given (or after the position an earlier answer returned) to the tip read first, ordered by height and then by id, and reads each whole; no endpoint finds label 674 by its content, and the payee is never a key. A listed transaction that is the act settles the read, or, above the final mark, answers `pending`, `below-mark`. One that carries H and is not the act is a foreign item. A listed transaction that does not decode is named, and the search reads on: where nothing else decides, the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`, naming it, its position the height before it. The search is complete where every page to the tip was read, the endpoint named a height at or past it before each page, every listed transaction was read whole, and the listing is one rule 20 admits. Every page is read to the last; where the endpoint named a height below the tip before a page, the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`, with the lowest height it named, through which every listed transaction was read, and where the bound or a listed transaction the endpoint does not hold stopped it, with the height before the first it did not read; the next call takes that height. An item the search could not decode is held no later than that height. One call reads at most 1,000 listed transactions whole. A payer whose address holds no payment credential gives no search, and the read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete` where it would answer `failed`.
20. The search proves absence only through a listing shown to hold every transaction that spends an output of the credential, a page with no more being the last. Koios's `POST /credential_txs` (with `POST /tx_cbor` and `POST /tx_info`) is such a listing by its published document (v1.4.2), not yet shown by a run; Blockfrost's `GET /addresses/{address}/transactions` lists one address of the credential only. Until a run shows a listing whole, the search is never complete: the read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete` where it would answer `failed`, and a search read to the tip gives no position. No reader can state a listing shown whole; that comes with the run.
21. `failed`, `instrument-consumed`: at the final mark, a transaction that is not the act spent the input (an invalid one, one that carries no H, an act the caller gives, or on a payment one that names no payee's account, `other-account`), and the search is complete and found no act. A valid transaction that spent the input and is the act settles the read, the body the payer rebuilt from the same input among them, with `presented` `no`. Where the endpoint cannot name the transaction that spent the input, or does not hold it, the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`. `failed`, `transaction-failed`: the transaction the reference names, included with `valid_contract` false at the final mark, took the input as collateral, and the search is complete and found no act.
22. A buyer's wallet that pays again from another payment credential is another payer, whose act is no act of this read.

**The read kit.** Every case of `read-status.json` runs through both pairings' reads but those the reads cannot meet, on both pairings, which the forms in `test/read-kit/forms/cardano.ts` skip, each with its reason: *a payment act carrying H that transferred zero* and *an act carrying H that transferred zero*, since every output carries at least the ledger's smallest (the agreement's floor); *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since the credential listing names each transaction it lists with its block's height (rule 19); and, while no run has shown a listing whole (rule 20: Koios's listing until its run, Blockfrost's until a run shows the credential whole), each case whose answer on the act is `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`. Through the read each of those answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`. The lock pairing's form runs each case on the Masumi lock and, where every act the case holds is of its kind, on the `script` lock as well, whose every act is of its kind (rule 15), each answer held to the case's.

**What a record proves.** On the address method: the payer's own signed transaction carrying H landed valid at the mark read, and paid, as observed, the outputs the read names. On a lock: it deposited into a contract, with the beneficiary a Masumi datum names. A transaction with fewer confirmations than the security parameter can be rolled back.
