**LCP profile `mpp/charge/tempo/split-memo`: the ATR hash as a split's memo in an MPP Tempo charge.**

Two pairings: `mpp/charge/tempo/split-memo`, the pull form, whose signed transaction the resource server's settler broadcasts, and `mpp/charge/tempo/split-memo/push`, the push form, whose transaction the payer broadcasts and names by its hash. The challenge carries H as `mpp/charge` rules 1, 2, 3 and 5 say. The retired ids `mpp/charge/tempo/memo` and `mpp/charge/tempo/push`, which carried seven bytes of H in the attribution memo, are never reused: no form carries part of H.

**The challenge**

1. The resource server places one split that carries H: `methodDetails.splits` holds `{recipient, amount: "1", memo: H}`, `recipient` the request's own `recipient`, `amount` one base unit of `currency`, and `memo` H's 32 bytes as `0x` and 64 hex digits. The seller's own splits number at most nine beside it, so that the challenge holds at most the ten splits MPP's Tempo charge admits, and the primary transfer is `amount` less every split. A charge's `amount` is therefore above 1 plus the seller's own splits. The split carrying H is the challenge's carrier: the ATR's `mpp` slot and the issued digest read the challenge without it (`mpp/charge` rule 5).
2. The challenge names `methodDetails.chainId` always, as an integer: `4217` on Tempo mainnet (`eip155:4217`), `42431` on Moderato (`eip155:42431`); a challenge without one, or with one that is not a positive integer, is refused `mpp/chain-id-required`, since MPP's Tempo charge draft reads an absent one as Moderato. It names `feePayer`, `true` where the resource server's settler pays the fee and `false` where the payer pays it in the fee token: the payer signs with the fee payer's slot open, so who pays is fixed in the challenge.
3. The pull form's challenge names `supportedModes: ["pull"]`, which stock `mppx` honours from 0.5.13; a list holding `push` names the push form, and a challenge without `supportedModes` names both. Which form a challenge offers is read from `supportedModes` alone. A payment that already moved under a challenge naming pull alone is read as the push form: its `hash` credential is read by the push form's codec.
4. A challenge is refused, and none is placed, where it carries `methodDetails.zones` (no form defines where a Tempo Zone payment's H rides), where it sets `machineTokenEnabled` (the stock client's swap route can move the memo out of the split), where it carries `methodDetails.memo` (`mpp/carrier-taken`: the carrier is the split), or where its amount is zero (the stock client then signs an off-chain proof and moves nothing).

**The payment**

5. The stock client (`mppx` 0.17.6, pympp 0.20.0) builds one Tempo transaction from the challenge: the primary `transferWithMemo` to `recipient`, whose memo is MPP's attribution memo, and one call for each split, each split's `memo` copied into its transfer. So the payer's own signed transaction carries all of H, in the split's `transferWithMemo` call. lcp's client builds nothing for this form.
6. Where the challenge has the fee paid for the payer (`feePayer: true`), a stock signer sends the transaction as a `0x78` envelope: the `0x76` envelope's list with the sender's 20-byte address in the fee payer's slot, item 11. The sender signed the `0x76` form with the fee token empty and that slot `0x00`. lcp reads both envelopes, recovers the sender from the `0x76` form, and refuses a `0x78` envelope whose item 11 names another account (`tempo/tx-malformed`).
7. `bound` requires exactly one `transferWithMemo` call to `currency` whose memo is H, in a transaction of at most 65,536 bytes signed for the challenge's `chainId`; none is `tempo/memo-not-bound` and more than one `tempo/ambiguous`. A `source` the credential carries names the transaction's sender. `bound` checks how the payment carries H, never what it pays: the split's recipient and amount, like every split's, are held to the request by the stock `tempo` validation the settler runs, which also checks the attribution memo. On the push form the server first reads the named transaction's receipt, a read that moves nothing, and `bound` reads H there; a missing receipt or a failed read is a read to repeat, never a refusal.
8. The single-use value is the Tempo transaction's own nonce, fixed by its nonce key (TIP-1009): key 2^256 − 1 is the expiring nonce, whose value is the hash over the transaction's sender-signed fields followed by the sender's address (`expiringNonceHash`), which no fee payer's signature or fee token changes, marked in the nonce precompile `0x4e4F4E4345000000000000000000000000000000` at slot `keccak256(hash ‖ uint256(1))` under the transaction's `validBefore` and cleared only after it; key 0 is the protocol nonce, read by `eth_getTransactionCount`; any other key is a 2D nonce lane, read by the precompile's `getNonce(sender, nonceKey)`. The protocol nonce and a lane are consumed where they answer above the signed nonce. The chain takes the value before the transaction's calls run, outside their checkpoint, so a transaction a block includes has consumed its value whatever their result: the protocol nonce of one whose first call is a call, a lane's nonce and the expiring nonce alike (tempoxyz/tempo@c8b89f9b `crates/evm/src/handler.rs:1116-1257`, `apply_nonce`). The stock client signs the expiring nonce, with `validBefore` at most 25 seconds ahead. The signed `validBefore` bounds the value, and a transaction that signs none has no bound: it is searched until its hold ends and is never `failed`. The challenge's `expires` is not signed and bounds nothing.

**The read** (`read-status`)

9. The reference names the network, the payer (the sender: recovered from the signed bytes on pull, read from the landed transaction on push), the signed `validBefore`, the payment presented (on pull, the hash over the fields its sender signed, in the `0x76` form whatever envelope carried it, as `expiringNonceHash` computes it; on push, the transaction the credential names), and the option's payee, the request's `recipient`.
10. The search for H reads, from the position the caller gives to the latest head the endpoint serves, in pages of at most 10,000 blocks, halved for an endpoint that refuses a page's range (a range refused at one block is `unreadable`, `transport`), the logs of every contract with topics `[TransferWithMemo, payer, any, H]`, by a filter that names no address, and the receive-policy guard `0xB10C000000000000000000000000000000000000`'s `TransferBlocked` logs by that event alone, each receipt decoded (`ClaimReceiptV1`, version 1) and kept where its originator is the payer and its memo is H, since a transfer a receive policy held emits no `TransferWithMemo`; a receipt the read cannot decode holds the search at its block (`read-status`, *Absence is proven, never inferred*). Each hit is read whole and tested against the act asked about: on pull, the landed transaction's sender-signed fields equal the presented transaction's; on push, the transaction is the one the credential names. A value read consumed whose transaction the search has not found is found in the first block at which the value reads consumed, by halving the blocks from the position given to the block it was read consumed at (from block 0 where it is already consumed at the position given, so that the answer is `before-position`), and in that block's transactions as the payer's transaction over that value, on the protocol nonce a transaction of another type than Tempo's among them, whose signed bytes carry the nonce and whose sender the node names the payer; its receipt decides. An agreement and a charge of this form both carry H as the split's memo, each in its own transaction: an act the caller gives under the same H is never settled on (`read-status` rule 11).
11. Success is the receipt's `status` 1 for the act's own transaction, whose sender is the payer, and in it a token's `TransferWithMemo` from the payer with memo H, or, where the payee's receive policy held that transfer, the guard's `TransferBlocked` whose receipt names the payer as originator and H as memo. What moved is every token transfer the transaction's calls make from the payer, the primary and every split, each with its token, its recipient, its amount and its log's position among the receipt's logs, a held transfer recorded as moved to the guard; the one-unit split alone is never the payment. On a charge the act pays where one of those transfers goes to the request's `recipient`, whatever its token and amount, zero included (`read-status` rule 9); a transaction of the payer carrying H with no transfer to the `recipient`, one whose every transfer to it a receive policy held among them, pays nothing (`other-account`) and, having consumed the value, answers `failed`, `instrument-consumed`, at the final mark, as the stock server finds no transfer to the recipient in it either. An agreement is held to no account, so one held this way counts. A `TransferWithMemo` with memo H in a transaction another account sent, a spender's `transferFromWithMemo` among them, is a foreign item, `other-signer`, and one the payee's own account sent is `payee-signed`; a transaction of another type than Tempo's that carries H is of another kind (`other-kind`).
12. A reverted transaction of the payer's own is included and its nonce consumed: the chain consumes a transaction's value before its calls run, and their revert does not restore it. It moved nothing; the account it names is the recipient of the transfers it signed. Read at the final mark, one that carries H and, on a charge, names the request's `recipient` answers `failed`, `transaction-failed`, and any other answers `failed`, `instrument-consumed`; the payer signs again.
13. Marks: one, `finalized`, a state and not a count, the default and the final mark; `status` takes no other finality (`unreadable`, `unparseable`). A follower node's head is final; a validator node's head can be a notarized block above `finalized`, and a transaction found there is `pending`, `below-mark`, named at `latest`, never `settled`. The position recorded at issue is the `finalized` block's number. The value is read at the `finalized` block and at `latest`, each at the last block whose timestamp is below `validBefore` where that head's timestamp is at or past it, since a later read shows nothing: unconsumed at both, its bound not passed by the `finalized` block's timestamp, `instrument-live`; the `finalized` block's timestamp at or past `validBefore`, the value unconsumed at the last block below it and the search read whole, `instrument-unused`; a bound passed at `latest` alone is `below-mark`. On pull the reference holds the value; on push it is read from the signed bytes the endpoint returns for the named transaction (`eth_getRawTransactionByHash`), which must hash to that name, and where the endpoint returns none the read holds no value. A read of the value below the latest head needs the endpoint to hold that block's state, which a header it serves does not show: such a read that fails answers `unreadable`, `history-gap`, unless the reader names the failure another reason. The cost: a value read consumed whose transaction the search did not find costs one read of the value for each halving and one block's transactions.
14. The read kit's cases (`read-status` rule 28) that cannot arise on its ledger: on both forms, *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, since no finality below `finalized`, Tempo's one mark, is asked; by the case's words, *an act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since every log and transaction the read meets is in a block, which places it; and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since each reads the value at `latest` whenever it reads it at `finalized`. On the pull form also `not-found`, since the read holds what the payer signed, its transaction, and reads that value, and so *a read with no means to read the single-use value*. On the push form also *a read with no means to read the single-use value that meets a failed result of the payer's*, since the read meets a failed result of the payer's only as the transaction the credential names, which consumed its value whatever its result (rule 8).
15. One transaction can pay two sales of this form: each sale's read names as what moved every transfer the transaction makes from the payer, the other sale's included. Where the two options name two accounts, each read settles on the transaction by its own transfer to its own payee. Where they name one account, both reads settle on the same transaction and name the same transfers, so a caller that counts each transfer for one sale alone settles only one of them: a buyer that pays two such sales of one seller in one transaction has paid the second without its settlement, and its recourse is that seller.

**The agreement**

16. The pull form's payment can itself be an agreement. Its floor is 2 base units: the one-unit split carrying H and a primary of at least one unit, both to the payee. Under `feePayer: true` the resource server's settler pays the fee, and under `false` the payer, in the fee token. Success is rule 11's. A reverted transaction is no agreement (rule 12), nor is a `TransferWithMemo` with memo H in a transaction another account sent, nor a transaction the payee's own account sent. The push form is not offered as an agreement.

**What a record proves**

17. "paid" where value moved, with the account it reached, and "signed" where nothing moved: the payer's own signed Tempo transaction carries all of H as the memo of a `transferWithMemo` call, and the chain verified the payer's signature when it executed the call. This does not show that the amount, the payee, the asset or the timing match the ATR's content.
