**LCP profile `x402/exact/casper`: the ATR hash as the CEP-3009 nonce.**

**The form**

1. The `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, H the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes as `0x` lowercase hex and L an `https` URL serving them.
2. The client fetches L, hashes the bytes received, and compares with H as 32 bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match it signs CEP-3009 `TransferWithAuthorization` (`from`, `to`, `value`, `validAfter`, `validBefore`, `nonce`, under the domain `name`, `version`, `chain_name`, `contract_package_hash`) with `to` the option's `payTo`, `value` its `amount`, `validAfter` 0, `validBefore` the client's clock plus the option's `maxTimeoutSeconds` in Unix seconds, `chain_name` the option's `network`, `contract_package_hash` its `asset`, and `nonce` = H as 64 hex digits; and it echoes `extensions` unchanged. A presented nonce is read with or without `0x`, and is 32 bytes.
4. **The agreement's own nonce.** Where the payment is offered as an agreement, the signed `nonce` is the agreement's value in place of H: SHA-256 over the 16 ASCII bytes `lcp-agreement-v1` followed by H's 32 bytes (the profile `read-status`, rule 1), 64 hex digits; its vectors are `core-vectors.json`'s `agreementValue` and this pairing's. The token keys a used nonce by `(from, nonce)`, so an agreement and a payment under one H from one account never take one item. `build` writes it for the act `agreement`; `bound`, given the act `agreement` and H, refuses a payment whose nonce is not that value, `casper/nonce-not-bound`, and gives H where it is; `reference`, given the same, names that value as the signed nonce.
5. The server refuses a payment whose nonce is not an H it issued for this request, or that H's agreement value where it offered the option as an agreement, and has not seen claimed. It refuses a payment whose `from` is the option's `payTo`: an act the payee signed is no payment and no agreement.
6. A settler relays the authorization to the token's `transfer_with_authorization` and pays the gas; nothing in the authorization names it. It checks the authorization by x402's Casper scheme before anything is sent, and the server checks H first (rule 5).

**What "succeeded" means, and what fails**

7. Succeeded: the transaction executed with no `error_message`, calling a CEP-3009 package's `transfer_with_authorization`, or `receive_with_authorization`, with the authorization's `from` and the signed nonce. The call sets that package's `used_nonces[(from, nonce)]`. Its package, `to` and `value` are what moved, recorded as observed; the package is never a reason to pass a call over (rule 16).
8. Executed with an `error_message`: contract state rolls back and the nonce stays unset. No payment and no agreement, and nothing is consumed: the authorization can still land until `validBefore`.
9. A cancellation: an executed `cancel_authorization` for the nonce is the payer's signature over it. It sets the item with no transfer, so the nonce can never land on this token, and it is no payment and no agreement.

**The asset's requirements**

10. The asset's CEP-3009 refuses a nonce that is not 32 bytes; names the amount `value`, as x402's published Casper settler sends it; refuses an authorization at or past `validBefore`; and holds a used nonce in the dictionary `used_nonces` under the item the reader reads for `(from, nonce)`. The dictionary's name and the item key are the token's own; a reader reads an asset only where a run has shown them. On any other asset the payment is not found from what the payer signed.

**The read** (the profile `read-status`)

11. The reference names the network, the asset package, `from`, the signed nonce (H, or the agreement's value), the option's `payTo` as the payee, `validBefore` (as `settleBy`, capped at 2^53 − 1), and the authorization as presented. `reference` refuses a presented `accepted` that is not the option it is given, `x402/accepted-not-option`. With nothing given, which means the act `payment`, it refuses a payment that echoes a legal context whose H is not its nonce, `casper/nonce-not-bound`; a payment that echoes none gives its nonce as H, as `bound` does. The caller adds H for `status`, by which the read tells H's other value (the agreement's value on a payment, H on an agreement) from the act's own. The reference's nonce must be the value the act asked about carries, H on a payment and the agreement's value on an agreement, and its authorization as presented, where it holds one, must be of the form rule 3 states; otherwise `status` answers `unreadable`, `unparseable`, with nothing read.
12. The ledger has one mark, `finalized`: a node serves only executed transactions of final blocks, so the newest block it has executed is both the final mark and the latest head, and one read of the nonce there is its read at both. A position is a block height.
13. The read is of the nonce. It reads `used_nonces[(from, nonce)]` with `state_get_dictionary_item` at the state root of the newest block the node has executed. Unset, in the node's documented form for a missing item: the payment has not landed on this package. Where that block's own timestamp is before `validBefore` the answer is `pending`, `instrument-live`. At or past it, the authorization can no longer land on this package, but an authorization the payer signed under the same nonce on another token package is a payment carrying H that no read of this item shows, so the read of the item is no search for H (the profile `read-status`, rules 24 to 26: each token package keeps its own nonces) and the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`, never `failed`; its search position is the height read unset, as it is wherever the caller gave a search position.
14. Set: the read finds the block that set it. Where the caller gave a position and the item is already set at it, the block is found below it, from the lowest height the node serves; otherwise above the position given, or above the lowest height the node serves. It bisects block heights, one read of the item at one block's state root for each probe (at most 64 a call), to the first block where the item is set. A probe the node does not serve is `unreadable`, `history-gap`; an item set at the lowest height served is `unreadable`, `history-gap`; a search position naming a transaction that set the item, which the node no longer knows, is `unreadable`, `history-gap`. The node's `available_block_range` chooses the endpoint and is never the verdict.
15. It reads that block's transactions, in the block's order, a page at a time (at most 8 pages a call, and at most 1024 calls a page, a page that gives more being `unreadable`, `too-large`), for the one transaction that executed with no `error_message` on the asset package, under the nonce, for the reference's `from`. One act sets the item, so exactly one such transaction exists. It reads the whole block, so that every transaction in it that carries H, on whatever package it calls, is named. Where pages remain, the answer carries the search position, the block and the next page, from which the next call resumes. A CEP-3009 call there whose package or 32-byte nonce the node does not show, and a call that carries the nonce or H's other value and whose `from`, or on a transfer `to` or `value`, the node does not show, are calls the node does not give whole: each is an item the search could not decode, which holds the search at its page, and the answer names it (the profile `read-status`, rule 19).
16. The one that set the item, by `transfer_with_authorization` or `receive_with_authorization` on the asset package, is the act asked about, the call on another package setting another package's item: on the act `agreement` it is the act whatever its `to`, an agreement being held to no account; on the act `payment` it is the act where its `to` is the option's `payTo`, whatever its `value`, zero included. A payment whose `to` is another account consumed the nonce and names no payee's account: it is not the act, and since the read of the item is no search for H the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`, naming it with what it moved and the reason `other-account`, never `failed`. A `cancel_authorization` is answered the same way, naming it with the reason `other-kind`.
17. The item set and the block read whole with no such transaction found, as where an authorization was executed from session code or through another contract, whose arguments no transaction's own call shows: `pending`, `search-incomplete`, never `not-found` or `failed`.
18. Every other transaction in the block that carries the nonce or H's other value and executed is a foreign item, named with the first reason of the closed list that holds: an authorization the payee signed, `payee-signed`; another account's, `other-signer`; one the caller gave, by its reference or by its transaction in either case of its hex digits, `given`; the payer's act under H's other value, or on a package other than the asset's, its package recorded with what it moved, `other-kind`. One that executed with an `error_message` decides nothing and is no foreign item. A transaction at or before the position given that set the item answers `failed`, `before-position`, naming it.
19. What moved names the one transfer by the transaction that set the item and, as its place, that transaction's own call to the package, by its entry point, from whose arguments it is read: the package, `to` and `value`. `to` alone is compared, with `payTo`, on the act `payment`; the package and `value` are compared with nothing. The signer is the authorization's `from`.
20. `presented`, on `settled`: `yes` where the executed call's package, `from`, `to`, `value`, `validAfter`, `validBefore` and nonce equal the authorization as presented; `no` where one differs; `unshown` where the read does not show every one. A reference given to `status` without the authorization as presented is compared with nothing, and the answer states no `presented`; its `settleBy` is then the lapse. It decides no answer. An authorization as presented with a member out of its form (rule 11) is no reference.
21. No transaction hash is needed for any answer: a lost settle answer is decided by the read of the nonce and the search.
22. `failed` is answered only as `before-position`. The read kit's cases this ledger cannot give are declared inapplicable to it: `below-mark`, since the family has one mark; `transaction-failed` and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*, since a failed execution leaves the nonce unset; `instrument-unused` and `instrument-consumed`, since no `failed` but `before-position` is answered (rule 13, rule 16); and `not-found`, since the read always holds the nonce. Declared inapplicable also, by the cases' words: *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, *a value unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a value whose validity passed, unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the one mark is the latest head and the read holds the nonce (rule 12); *a read with no means to read the single-use value* and *search-incomplete is answered before not-found*, since the read always holds the nonce; *an act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since the read takes each transaction from a block at its height (rule 15); *each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place*, since the one CEP-3009 call the act makes moves one transfer (rule 19); *a payment act carrying H that moved another asset* and *a second act of the payer that meets every test*, since the act asked about is the one call under the signed nonce on the option's asset package, which one act sets, and no search reaches the payer's acts in another package (rules 13 and 16); and *instrument-live is answered before an item the search could not decode* and *an item the search could not decode holds a failed answer*, since the read reads a block's transactions only once the item is set, so a nonce read unset, lapsed or not, meets no item (rules 13 and 15).

**Recovery**

23. `recover` takes `{network, tx, option}`, the option's `asset` naming the package, and returns the `nonce` argument of the transaction's executed authorization call on that package: H from a payment's transaction, and from an agreement's the agreement's value, which a holder of the ATR computes from its own H and compares.

**The agreement**

24. The payment can be an agreement: one authorization the payer signs, paying the payee directly a fixed amount, with no hold, landing whole on one network. The floor is 0 by the token; x402's published Casper settler refuses a zero amount, so a settler that registers it declares no zero, and the agreement is then 1 base unit. The settler pays the fee, always. The transaction must succeed (rule 7). An authorization the payee signed is no agreement.

**What a record proves**

25. A settled read shows that the payer's own signed authorization carries H as its nonce (on an agreement, the agreement's value), which the token contract verified with the payer's key when it executed; and that it paid the account `to` names the amount `value`, zero included, which on a payment is the option's `payTo`. It does not show that the amount, the asset or the timing match the ATR's content.
