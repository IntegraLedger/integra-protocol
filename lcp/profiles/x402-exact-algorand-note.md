**LCP profile `x402/exact/algorand/note`: the ATR hash as the Algorand payment's note.** This profile defines the pairing `x402/exact/algorand`.

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`. H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes, as `0x` lowercase hex, and L is an `https` URL that serves those bytes.
2. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client builds the `exact` payment as x402's Algorand scheme defines it. It sets the `note` of `paymentGroup[paymentIndex]` to exactly the 77 ASCII bytes `lcp:sha256:` followed by H ([LCP §8.1](https://github.com/legal-context-protocol/legal-context-protocol/blob/main/spec/legal-context-protocol-v1.md#81-naming-conventions)), and echoes `extensions` unchanged. The option's `network` is `algorand:` and the first 32 characters of the network's genesis hash in base64url (CAIP-2); a payment whose genesis hash does not match it is refused `avm/network-mismatch`. Its `amount` may be `"0"`: the scheme sets no floor.
4. The server refuses a payment whose payment-transaction note does not decode to an H that it issued for that request and has not seen claimed. It also refuses, before anything moves, a payment entry that a logic signature authorises (`avm/logic-signature`) and one that names an asset sender, `asnd` (`avm/clawback`): under a logic signature no key signs the transaction, and a transfer that names `asnd` moves that account's holding by the act of the asset's clawback address, so neither is the payer's signed act. A logic signature a Falcon key delegated (`lsig.pqsig`) is refused `avm/txn-malformed` first: the x402 facilitator's decoder reads no `pqsig` there either, so it and algod read two transactions (rule 8).
5. H is on chain in the confirmed transaction's note. An Indexer search with `note-prefix` equal to the base64 of that string returns it, and also returns any other transaction that wrote the same note. The read of rule 9 does not use that parameter, which the Indexer's default configuration disables.
6. x402's Algorand scheme leaves the note to the client, and the steward's client writes its own (`x402-payment-v2-<milliseconds>`). A client that does not implement this profile is refused by rule 4.

**The settler, the signer and the agreement**

7. The settler is the resource server's x402 facilitator. It checks the group (at most 16 entries, the scheme's bound), the payment entry's `aamt`, `arcv` and `xaid` against the requirements, the amount by equality, and that the entry is signed; where the option names `extra.feePayer` it signs its own zero-ALGO payment, which carries the group's fee, and the buyer's transfer carries a zero fee bound to the group; with no fee payer the buyer pays its own transfer's fee. The choice is fixed in what the buyer signs. The settler submits the group, and a confirmation it reports is a hint: the read of rule 9 decides.
8. The signer is `sgnr` where the payer's account is rekeyed and `snd` otherwise. Its authority is a key's signature over the transaction: a plain signature, a multisignature, or a Falcon signature (`pqsig`), by the account's own key or by the key it is rekeyed to. The x402 facilitator's decoder reads no `pqsig`, so an entry that sets it has one reader, algod's, as lcp reads it: lcp takes it where `pqsig` is its one signature, the scheme is Falcon-1024's, `f1`, and the key and signature are within 1,793 and 1,538 bytes, under the transaction's own id, and a settler other than the facilitator submits it as lcp read it, the node being its second reader. lcp's client writes no Falcon signature: `complete` takes a 64-byte Ed25519 signature alone, so a payer whose key is Falcon-1024's has its own wallet write the `pqsig` envelope over the transaction lcp built, under that transaction's own id. A logic signature, whether a key delegated it or it is a contract account's, is no such authority, and a transfer that names `asnd` is not the holder's act: one that lands is read and described, and is never the payer's act. Such a transaction is reported as a foreign item with its signer and its authority, named by the Indexer's signature member that is set (`sig`, `multisig`, `logicsig` or `pqsig`), and an inner transaction, which no key signed, with the authority `none`.
9. The pairing's payment can itself be an agreement. Its floor is zero; the fee is the settler's where the option names `extra.feePayer`, else the buyer's; the transaction must be confirmed, which on Algorand is final and means it succeeded, since a group applies whole or not at all and no failed transaction is included; and an act whose signer is the option's payee, or whose `snd` is the payee, is no agreement and no payment.

**The read** (the profile `read-status`)

10. `reference` gives the network, the transaction id (the base32 of SHA-512/256 of `"TX"` and the transaction's canonical encoding, which covers the note and not the signature), the last valid round as a decimal string, H, the payer `snd`, the signer, and the option's payee, `payTo`, for the two party tests. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not the option it is given (`x402/accepted-not-option`).
11. The single-use value is the transaction id within its validity rounds. The read holds it: `GET /v2/transactions?txid=` on an Indexer. The one mark is the confirmed round, which is final. The position is the confirmed round. A signed payment is payable through its `lastValid` round, at most 1,000 rounds after its `firstValid`; the bound is in rounds.
12. **Succeeded** means the transaction is confirmed under its id in a round after the position the caller gives. It is the act asked about where it is a top-level asset transfer, its note is exactly H's LCP string, its `snd` is the reference's, it names no `asnd`, its authority is a key's signature (rule 8), its signer is not the payee, and, on the act `payment`, its `arcv` is the payee; `xaid`, `aamt` and, where set, `aclose` with the close amount are recorded as observed, whatever asset or amount moved, zero included. A transaction confirmed under the claimed id that fails any of these consumed the id and is not the act asked about.
13. **The history.** Absence is answered only where the Indexer holds the rounds from the position the caller gives: the reader states that its Indexer is declared archival, or the Indexer answers `/v2/blocks/{round}?header-only=true` at that position. Otherwise an id not found is `unreadable`, `history-gap`.
14. **The lapse.** An id not found, in an answer whose `current-round` is not past `lastValid`, is `pending`, `instrument-live`. Past `lastValid`, or where the id landed as a transaction that is not the act asked about, the answer needs the search for H (rule 15) read whole and finding no act asked about: then `failed`, `instrument-unused` or `instrument-consumed`; an act the search finds is `settled`, its `presented` `no`.
15. **The search for H.** The payer's transactions, `GET /v2/accounts/{snd}/transactions`, from `min-round` one past the position the caller gives, returned newest first and paged by the `next` token; the call takes only `min-round`, `max-round`, `limit` and `next`. The read matches H on each transaction's note, and on each of its inner transactions' notes, byte for byte. The range is read in sub-ranges: a sub-range's top is the `current-round` of its first page; each later page is asked with `max-round` at that top and must answer a `current-round` at or past it; a sub-range ends at the first page that holds no transaction, a short page not being its end. The search is complete when it is read whole from the position given to the top of a sub-range at or past the round of the id's lookup. Its position is the round through which it is read whole, with the top and the `next` token of a sub-range under way; at most 32 pages are read in one call. A page answering a `current-round` below its top leaves the search incomplete, its position held at that page. An item the reader cannot parse is named by its id, the rest of the range is read, and the search is held at that item's page, which the next call reads again, unless the caller gave that transaction as another act under the same H. A page that fails is `unreadable`, `transport`. Where the read runs no search, a search position the caller gives is carried back unchanged. A top-level asset transfer it finds that is the act asked about (rule 12) settles; any other transaction carrying H is a foreign item. The search reads every transaction of the payer's account, sent or received, so a busy or flooded account lengthens it.
16. **Not yet shown.** That the Indexer holds every transaction of the rounds after a header it answers, and the Indexer's paging as rule 15 reads it, are not yet shown by a run; nor is a payment whose entry a Falcon signature authorises (rule 8) taken by the node under the id lcp computes. Until a run shows it, no server or settler states a Falcon-authorised payer as one it takes. Until a run shows them, a search read whole proves no absence: where the read would answer `failed` for any reason but `before-position`, it answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`.
17. **The payment presented.** A `settled` answer states `presented`: `yes` where the act is the transaction the reference was built from, whose id covers the whole transaction the payer signed, its signature apart; `no` where it is another. Each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction id and its place, the member naming the account it reached: `arcv`, or `aclose` for a holding closed to it, after the path of `inner-txns` indexes that leads to an inner transaction (`inner-txns/0/arcv`).
18. **The read kit.** These cases of `read-status.json` cannot arise on this read, and the read kit skips them. By their reason: `below-mark`, since the Indexer serves confirmed rounds alone; `not-found`, since the read holds the single-use value, the transaction id within its validity rounds, and answers its absence `pending`, `instrument-live`, or `unreadable`, `history-gap` (rules 13 and 14); and `transaction-failed`, since Algorand includes no failed transaction. By the case's words: *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, *a value unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a value whose validity passed, unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head*, since the Indexer's one round is the final mark and the latest head; *transactions carrying H with a failed result*, *a failed result that left the value unconsumed* (its two cases), *a failed result that consumed the value* (its two cases) and *a read with no means to read the single-use value*, since no failed transaction is included; *a value read consumed whose transaction the search has not found* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the id is read consumed only by finding its transaction, at that one round; and *an act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since every transaction is in a round. And, while the search proves no absence (rule 16), the cases its gate holds: each case at coverage `full` whose answer on the act is `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`, which the read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, as the pairing's vectors hold; that declaration goes when a run shows the facts of rule 16.
19. **What a record proves.** That the payer's account authorized, with a key's signature (rule 8), an asset transfer whose note is H, confirmed and final. Where it transferred, one of zero included, that the payer paid the account `arcv` names; it shows nothing of the amount, asset or timing the ATR states. As an agreement, that this account signed the transfer carrying H.
