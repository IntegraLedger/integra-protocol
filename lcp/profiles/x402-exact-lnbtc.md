**LCP profile `x402/exact/lnbtc/payment-hash`: an invoice for a payment hash the ATR commits, issued after the agreement.**

This pairing is x402's `exact` scheme on Bitcoin Lightning (`lnbtc`), with the `bolt11` asset transfer method and the `upfront` payment flow, as x402 specifies it (`specs/schemes/exact/scheme_exact_lnbtc.md` in `x402-foundation/x402` at `88b66dd4`, cited below as *the scheme*), on `lnbtc:000000000019d6689c085ae165831e93` (mainnet) and `lnbtc:000000000933ea01ad0ee984209779ba` (testnet). The buyer signs nothing: its node pays an invoice the seller's node signed, and no public ledger shows the payment. So the pairing is at coverage `none`, with no link (`link` `none`): the ATR commits the invoice's payment hash, and the buyer makes the sale's agreement on another network first, by an agreement option the resource server offers. The ids `x402/exact/lnbtc` and `x402/exact/lnbtc/invoice-named` are retired and never reused.

**The offer, and what the ATR commits**

1. **The secret.** For each sale the seller's own software draws a 32-byte secret with cryptographically secure randomness, once, so that issuing the same sale again finds the same hash, and keeps it. The payment hash is SHA-256 of the secret. The software never logs the secret and never puts it in the ATR, a response or anything it sends to another party; it gives it to its own node only when the invoice is issued.
2. **The offer at issue** is an x402 v2 option with the scheme's members (`scheme` `exact`, the network, `amount` in millisatoshis, `asset` `BTC`, `payTo` the seller node's compressed secp256k1 key as 66 lowercase hex digits, `maxTimeoutSeconds`, and in `extra` `paymentFlow` `upfront`, `assetTransferMethod` absent or `bolt11`, `requestHash`, `requestBindingProfile` and `requestBindingParams`) and one more `extra` member, `paymentHash`: the payment hash as 64 lowercase hex digits. It carries no `extra.invoice`. `advertiseBeforeCarrier` checks it and places the legal context: it refuses an offer that carries `extra.invoice` (`ln/invoice-in-offer`) or lacks a `paymentHash` of that form (`ln/payment-hash-required`), and makes rule 6's checks of the option's own members.
3. **What H commits.** The ATR's `x402` slot holds the option as `unplaced` gives it, without `extra.invoice`, so H commits `extra.paymentHash`, and with it the one invoice whose `p` field is that hash. `paymentHash` is a member the resource server declares, so the scheme's own rule holds it equal between the option a payment accepted and the requirements the resource server checks it against (the scheme, Facilitator Validation, check 3).
4. **The first response** carries H, the ATR's link, the agreement's URL and the sale's return value (`legal-context-return.md`, rule 5), and no option of this pairing: a stock client finds nothing to pay.

**The invoice, after the agreement**

5. **One invoice, sent to the sale's own client.** Only once the sale's agreement is recorded does the seller's node issue one invoice for the committed hash, from the kept secret: its `p` the payment hash, its `h` the request hash, no `d`, its amount the option's `amount`, its expiry the option's `maxTimeoutSeconds`, signed by the key in `payTo`. The resource server sends the option with that invoice in `extra.invoice` only in answer to a return whose `Legal-Context` header names the sale's H and carries the sale's return value, and to such a return again the same invoice; it sends the invoice to no other request. `advertise` checks the option with its invoice by rule 6 and places the legal context. Every node on the payment's route learns the preimage, and H is public once the agreement lands; the return value, which no route node holds, is what keeps a route node from obtaining the sale's invoice.
6. **The checks of the option and its invoice** that the option filter makes, and so `read`, `advertise`, `build`, `bound` and `reference`, in this order:
   a. the option's own members: `scheme` `exact` on one of the two networks, `asset` `BTC`, `amount` a positive integer in canonical decimal of at most 78 digits, `maxTimeoutSeconds` a positive safe integer, `payTo` a valid compressed secp256k1 public key as 66 lowercase hex digits, `extra.paymentFlow` `upfront`, `extra.assetTransferMethod` absent or `bolt11`, else `x402/option-malformed`;
   b. `extra.paymentHash` 64 lowercase hex digits, else `ln/payment-hash-required`;
   c. `extra.requestHash` 64 lowercase hex digits, else `ln/request-hash-mismatch`; `extra.requestBindingProfile` `http:1` or `mcp:1` and `extra.requestBindingParams` exactly the parameters the scheme's Request Binding gives that profile, in their stated form, else `ln/request-binding-malformed`;
   d. `extra.invoice` a string that decodes strictly by BOLT 11 (`11-payment-encoding.md` at `19146282`): a valid checksum, a prefix the decoder knows, an integral millisatoshi amount, exactly one `p` and exactly one `s`, each 52 words, an `n` where present of 53 words, and a signature that verifies, by the `n` key where the invoice has one, a low `s` required, and otherwise by public-key recovery, either `s` accepted; else `ln/invoice-malformed` (`ln/invoice-too-large` past 8,192 characters or 64 tagged fields);
   e. exactly one `h` field and no `d` field: `ln/no-description-hash`, `ln/field-repeated` for a second `h`, `ln/description-inline` for a `d`;
   f. the `h` field equal to `extra.requestHash`, else `ln/request-hash-mismatch`;
   g. the invoice's signing key equal to `payTo`, else `ln/signer-not-payee`;
   h. the invoice's currency the network's (`bc` on mainnet, `tb` on testnet), else `ln/currency-network`;
   i. the invoice's amount equal to `amount`, else `ln/amount-mismatch`;
   j. the invoice's expiry equal to `maxTimeoutSeconds`, else `ln/expiry-mismatch`;
   k. added by this form: the invoice's `p` equal to `extra.paymentHash`, else `ln/payment-hash-mismatch`.
   These checks read no clock and no request. An option on an `lnbtc` network that fails one is refused with its code, so the pairing offers no option whose invoice it would not pay. The scheme's two checks of the invoice's time, its creation time no later than the validation time plus the clock-skew allowance and the invoice unexpired, are the resource server's with its own clock before it sends the option.
7. **The client's checks, before it pays** (`build`), are the scheme's (Client Payment Construction, checks 1 to 9) in the scheme's order, with check k added: rule 6's checks; then, from the buyer's own intended request, which `build` is given, the request hash computed by the scheme's Request Binding for the option's profile (the profile must be the request's own transport, and on `http:1` the document's `resource.url` the request's URL, on `mcp:1` the option's `server` the server the buyer means to call), which must equal `extra.requestHash` and the invoice's `h` (`ln/request-hash-mismatch`; `ln/request-binding-malformed` for a request the profile cannot bind); then, at the validation time `build` is given, the invoice's creation time no later than that time plus 60 s (`ln/created-in-future`) and the invoice unexpired at that time, its creation time plus its expiry later than it (`ln/invoice-expired`). Only then does `build` return the invoice to pay. After the payment, `complete` takes the preimage the buyer's node returned and refuses one that is not 64 lowercase hex digits (`ln/preimage-malformed`) or whose SHA-256 is not the invoice's `p` (`ln/preimage-mismatch`), as the scheme asks of a paid result; it builds `{x402Version: 2, resource, accepted, payload: {preimage}, extensions}`, `accepted` being the option with the invoice paid.
8. **One invoice for a sale.** A sale has one hash and one invoice. An invoice that expires unpaid, where the seller's node will issue no second invoice for the hash, leaves the sale to a new ATR and a new agreement. `maxTimeoutSeconds`, which the ATR commits before the invoice exists, is the invoice's expiry, so the resource server sets it to at least the sale's lifetime.

**The payment presented, and what the resource server checks before anything is taken**

9. **`bound`** takes the payment presented: an x402 v2 payment whose `accepted` passes rule 6 (so the invoice's `p` is the echoed `extra.paymentHash`), whose `payload.preimage` is 64 lowercase hex digits (`ln/preimage-malformed`) and hashes by SHA-256 to the invoice's `p` (`ln/preimage-mismatch`), and whose echoed `extensions.legalContext` names an H; it returns that H. The echoed H only locates the sale. The resource server holds the echoed option, without its invoice, to the option the ATR's slot holds for that H, which ties the echoed payment hash to the ATR. `reference` gives the network, the payment hash and the time by which the payment can be proven (the invoice's creation time plus its expiry plus the scheme's 60 s), and refuses a payment whose `accepted`, without its invoice, is not the option it is given without its invoice (`x402/accepted-not-option`).
10. **Before anything is taken,** the resource server, in this order: answers no request of the sale that lacks the sale's return value; holds the presented `accepted.extra.invoice` byte-equal to the invoice its node issued for the sale; builds the requirements from its own record of the sale (the option the ATR's slot holds, the invoice issued, and the request hash it computes from the request that will execute), never from `accepted`; and runs `validateExactLnbtc` over the payment and those requirements, which moves nothing. Only then does it send the payment to a settler's `/settle`, never `/verify`, which the scheme's protocol sequence forbids for this flow, or make its own record that the payment hash is used, and serve. So a presentation with an invoice another node signed, or past the scheme's window, is refused before anything another party can take, with the scheme's reason.
11. **`validateExactLnbtc`** is the scheme's Facilitator Validation, checks 1 to 7 and the Paid-but-expired Policy, in the scheme's order, each failure answered with the scheme's `errorReason`, with this form's one check added at the end of check 5: the accepted invoice's `p` equal to `requirements.extra.paymentHash`, else `invalid_exact_lnbtc_invoice_payment_hash_mismatch`. It takes the validation time and the clock-skew allowance, 60 s by default, and answers the network and the payment hash, from which a settler forms the scheme's consumption key, `network + ":" + payment_hash`. A settler's `/settle` for this form runs the same export, then records that key once.
12. **`checkPreimage(preimage, paymentHash)`** is the preimage check alone: `true` where the preimage is 64 lowercase hex digits whose SHA-256 is the payment hash, given as 64 lowercase hex digits; `ln/preimage-malformed`, `ln/payment-hash-required` or `ln/preimage-mismatch` otherwise.

**What a preimage shows, and what a record proves**

13. The preimage is a bearer value. Every node on the payment's route learns it (BOLT 2 at `19146282`, `02-peer-protocol.md`, `update_fulfill_htlc`), and the seller's software holds the secret from the sale's issue. So a preimage that hashes to the committed hash shows that the secret left the seller's custody, as its node releases it when the invoice is paid, and never who paid; to anyone but the seller's node it does not show that the invoice was paid. The invoice's signer is held to `payTo`; no payer is named; the amount is the invoice's, as the seller reports it.
14. A record of a payment through this pairing proves: that an account signed an agreement transaction carrying the ATR's hash, on its own network, before this payment was taken or reported, which shows neither that the account is the payer nor when the payment was taken, which nothing reads; that the ATR commits the payment hash of the invoice paid; and that settlement is the seller's report.

**Where this form departs from the scheme's text**

15. One invoice for each sale, repeated to the sale's own client, where the scheme asks for a fresh invoice for each challenge and forbids reuse across challenges (the scheme, `PaymentRequirements`); the form keeps the rule against reuse across clients.
16. A proof accepted only under its own sale's option, where the scheme accepts an unused proof for another challenge with the same request and terms (the scheme, Request Binding): another sale's option names another `extra.paymentHash`, which `validateExactLnbtc` refuses `invalid_exact_lnbtc_extra_mismatch`.
17. One reason outside the scheme's closed list (the scheme, Error Vocabulary): `invalid_exact_lnbtc_invoice_payment_hash_mismatch`, for an invoice whose `p` is not `requirements.extra.paymentHash`.
18. **The record a resource server keeps of a sale** that offered this pairing outlives the sale: an invoice issued late in the sale's life ends `maxTimeoutSeconds` after its issue, and the scheme accepts its proof until 60 s after that end, so the record is kept until the sale's expiry plus `maxTimeoutSeconds` plus 60 s, and a proof the scheme still accepts finds its sale.

**The registry entry**

19. Coverage `none`, `link` `none`, `nonce` `single-use` (the payment hash), `broadcasts` `payer`, `agreementOn` `any`, no `agreement` (no Lightning act is signed by the buyer, so this pairing is never an agreement), no `read`. `confirmed` is `yes`: a recorded run on Bitcoin testnet3 showed a payment of this form.

**The read**

20. None. No public ledger shows a Lightning payment and no node is read, so the pairing has no `status` and no `recover`, and no read kit form runs through it: every case of the read kit reads a ledger. A sale paid through it is settled by the seller's report.

**The vectors**

21. `x402-exact-lnbtc.json`, whose `about` names each oracle: the scheme's own example option, invoice, payment, preimage, payment hash, request binding digests, validation time and reasons; invoices written and signed by `bolt11` 1.4.1 and read back by `light-bolt11-decoder` 3.2.0, one for each field rule a refused invoice breaks (a `d` field, two `h` fields, a `p` that is not the committed hash, a signer that is not `payTo`, no `s` field) and for the `n` field, an amount and an expiry the census tests use; and BOLT 11's published examples for the decoder. Its refusal rows each change one member of a good input: the offer (`offers`), the client's checks and `complete` (`build`), `bound`, `reference`, the scheme's validation (`validate`), the preimage check (`checkPreimage`) and the option filter (`boundAccepted`, each row an option the pairing's `read` does not offer, refused by `bound` and `advertise` with its code).
