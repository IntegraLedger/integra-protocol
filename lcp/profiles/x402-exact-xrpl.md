**LCP profile `x402/exact/xrpl`: the ATR hash as the digest the Payment signs in its `InvoiceID`.**

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, as in the profile `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009`, and the chosen option's `extra.invoiceId` is H's LCP string, `lcp:sha256:0x` and 64 lowercase hex digits; an option already holding another `invoiceId` is refused `xrpl/carrier-occupied`. The option is an `exact` option on an `xrpl:` network whose `extra.areFeesSponsored` is present and `false` (the payer pays the fee), and whose `extra.assetTransferMethod` is absent, `sequence` or `ticketSequence`.
2. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares it with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client signs one `Payment`, as the stock `@x402/xrpl` 2.30.0 client writes it from the option: `InvoiceID` the SHA-256 of `extra.invoiceId`'s UTF-8, 64 hex digits; `Destination` the option's `payTo`; `Amount` drops of XRP, or the issued currency's `{currency, issuer, value}`; the account's `Sequence`, or `Sequence` 0 with a `TicketSequence`; a `LastLedgerSequence`; and no `NetworkID` on a network whose id is at most 1024, the network's own id above that, as rippled reads it. It may be single-signed or multisigned.
4. The server refuses a payment whose signed `InvoiceID` is not the one the option names (`xrpl/carrier-mismatch`), whose option's `invoiceId` is not an LCP string (`xrpl/carrier-not-lcp`), whose blob carries a `NetworkID` a node of the option's network does not take (`xrpl/network-mismatch`), or whose blob is over 16,384 bytes (`xrpl/blob-too-large`), before it is verified or settled. A settler's `/verify` also refuses `Delegate` and `Memos`, as x402 requires of every facilitator.
5. H is on chain only as its digest: the `InvoiceID` can be confirmed from the ATR's bytes, and H cannot be recovered from the ledger alone.
6. A client that does not implement this profile writes another `InvoiceID`, or none, and rule 4 refuses its payment.

**The reference.**

7. `reference` gives the network, the presented transaction's hash, H, the option's payee (the `payTo` of the option it is given, the key of the two party tests and no search key), and the payer's instrument as signed: its `Account`, its `Sequence` or ticket, its `LastLedgerSequence`, and its signed body, every field but `TxnSignature`, `SigningPubKey` and `Signers` in the codec's canonical encoding. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not that option, compared by RFC 8785 form without `extra.invoiceId` (`x402/accepted-not-option`).

**The agreement.**

8. A payment of this pairing can itself be an agreement. The floor is 1 drop of XRP, a `Payment` of zero being malformed; an agreement option names XRP. The buyer pays the fee, in the `Payment` it signs. It succeeded where the transaction is in a validated ledger with `meta.TransactionResult` `tesSUCCESS`; a validated `tec` result destroys the fee, delivers nothing and consumes the sequence number or ticket, and is no agreement. The payee's own signature is no agreement: a `Payment` whose `Account` or `Delegate` is the option's payee is never the act, and one that carries H is a foreign item, `payee-signed`.

**The read.** `status` reads a rippled or Clio server through the caller's reader (`server_info`, `tx`, `account_tx`, `account_info`, `ledger_entry`), each `account_info` and `ledger_entry` call naming its ledger, `validated` or `current`, never the server's default; it compares no amount, asset or time with the option.

9. **Marks.** One mark a query can ask for, `validated`: the final mark. A transaction the server answers in a ledger it has not validated was read at `current`, which is no mark: an act there is `pending`, `below-mark`, never `settled`. The position is the ledger index.
10. **Found by the instrument.** The payment is found by the payer's `Account` with the signed `Sequence` or `TicketSequence`, never by a hash, which only helps to look: a re-signed or differently multisigned copy has another hash and is the same payment. The sequence number is read at the validated ledger and at the current one (`account_info`), a ticket by its `ledger_entry`. A `settled` answer states `presented`: `yes` where the act's signed body equals the reference's, its signatures apart, and `no` otherwise.
11. **What moved, and who signed.** What moved is `meta.delivered_amount`, never `Amount`: drops of XRP, an issued currency as `CUR:issuer`, or an MPT by its id, recorded with the `Destination` it reached, observed and not judged. A `Payment` delivers one amount to its one `Destination`, so each transaction the read takes holds one transfer, named by its transaction alone, its place being the whole transaction; an inner `Batch` payment is named by its own inner transaction. The signer is the `Account`, or the `Delegate` where one acts; a multisigned transaction's `Signers`, a `Delegate`, or an inner `Batch` transaction's parent is recorded as the authority it acted under. A payment names the payee's account where its `Destination` is the option's payee; an agreement is held to no account.
12. **The lapse.** The payment can no longer land once a validated ledger is past its signed `LastLedgerSequence`; one that signs none has no lapse. `instrument-unused`: past that ledger, the account's `Sequence` not past the signed one or the ticket still present. `instrument-consumed`: another transaction took the sequence number or ticket, a `Payment` to another account among them, named. `transaction-failed`: the payment, or a copy of it under another hash, validated with a `tec` result. Each is answered only once the search for H (rule 13), read whole, holds no act of the payer that is the act asked about, and, until the run rule 15 names, not at all.
13. **The search for H.** The payer's `account_tx` from the issue ledger, or the search position an earlier answer returned, to the latest ledger the server serves at the read, every transaction of the payer's account whatever it moved, never narrowed to a destination or an asset, at most 16 pages a call. Each transaction is classified by H in any of the XRPL forms, by the instrument, and by the hash presented; one found above the validated ledger is `pending`, `below-mark`. A transaction whose blob does not decode, whose answer is out of the reader's form, or whose result is no ledger's, is named among the items the search could not decode and holds the search's position at its ledger (read-status rule 19).
14. **History, and an account not shown.** An absence counts only where `complete_ledgers` holds the ledgers from the issue ledger to the validated ledger and the search began at its start; otherwise the answer is `unreadable`, `history-gap`. An account the validated ledger does not show, `account_info` answering that it is not found, deleted or never funded, gives no read of the value as unconsumed, since a deleted account may have taken it: the search decides, and with no act found the answer is `pending`, `search-incomplete`, never `not-found`.
15. **Not yet shown.** That `account_tx` lists, for every ledger it answers for, each transaction of the payer's account, a delegated payment and an inner `Batch` payment among them, has not been shown by a run. Until a run shows it, a search read whole proves no absence (read-status rule 25): where the read would answer `failed`, `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`, it answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, the search's position the ledger after the last it read. The cost: until that run a payment that lapsed, or whose sequence number or ticket another transaction took, stays `pending`.

**The read kit.**

16. Declared inapplicable to the read kit: `not-found`, since the payment carries the signed transaction, so the read holds its sequence number or ticket (`account_info`, `ledger_entry`); *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, since a seller names `validated` alone (rule 9); *each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place in it*, since each transaction the read takes holds one transfer, a `Payment` delivering one amount to its one `Destination` (rule 11); *a payment act carrying H that transferred zero* and *an act carrying H that transferred zero*, since a `Payment` of zero is malformed and a partial payment that delivers nothing does not succeed (rule 8); and, while the search proves no absence (rule 15), each case at coverage `full` whose answer on an act is `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`, which the search's gate holds: the vectors hold the read's answer to such a case, `pending`, `search-incomplete`. A case is named by its reason, or by the opening words of its text in `read-status.json`. Where XRPL cannot hold a case's fact as the case states it, the case is made in the ledger's own spelling where the rule that decides it treats the two alike: an act that names no account is made as one naming another account than the payee's (read-status rule 9), and a transaction that took the instrument is the payer's, read-status rule 16 deciding `before-position` before anything asks who signed it.
