**LCP profile `x402/exact/tron/lcp-trc20-memo`: the ATR hash in a payer-signed TRC-20 transfer's memo.**

*Scope.* x402 v2, `scheme` `"exact"`, `network` `tron:<chain id in decimal>` (CAIP-2), `extra.assetTransferMethod` `"lcp-trc20-memo"` (required). It defines this transfer method until x402 or MPP defines Tron. The default flow is `authorization`, and `upfront` is allowed.

1. The resource server's option gives `amount` in the token's atomic units as the canonical decimal of a positive integer (digits only, the first not `0`), or `"0"` for a contract on the list of contracts a run has shown to succeed and log `Transfer` on a `transfer(to, 0)`, by network and address, which is empty until a run shows one, `asset` (the TRC-20 contract) and `payTo` in base58check, and `maxTimeoutSeconds` as an integer from 1 to 86,340. `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, where H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes as `0x` lowercase hex and L is an `https` URL that serves them.
2. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client signs one transaction and sends it as `payload.transaction`, the serialized `Transaction` in lowercase hex. It echoes `extensions` unchanged. The transaction's `raw_data` holds:
   - exactly one `TriggerSmartContract`, from the payer to `asset`, calling `transfer(payTo, amount)` with no call value;
   - `data` = the 77 ASCII bytes `lcp:sha256:` followed by H;
   - `timestamp` = now and `expiration` = now + `maxTimeoutSeconds` × 1000, in milliseconds;
   - a recent reference block, and the client's own `fee_limit`.

   The signature is the payer's secp256k1 signature over SHA-256 of the serialized `raw_data`. `build`'s `tron-txid` request carries, beside `txid`, `rawBytes`: the serialized `raw_data` whose SHA-256 is `txid`, which `tronRequest` reads back to that id and to every field the payer signs.
4. The payer pays every fee: energy, bandwidth and the network's memo fee. Nothing in this profile sponsors them.
5. A facilitator's `/verify`:
   - decodes the transaction, and requires rule 3's shape on `asset`, for `transfer(payTo, amount)` exactly;
   - requires `data` to be an LCP `sha256` string;
   - requires signatures that the owner's permission accepts;
   - requires `expiration` to be in the future and no later than now + `maxTimeoutSeconds`.

   It should also simulate with `/wallet/triggerconstantcontract`, a recommendation and not a requirement. Its `/settle`:
   - repeats `/verify`;
   - broadcasts with `/wallet/broadcasthex`;
   - deduplicates settlements by transaction id, atomically, until `expiration` passes;
   - answers `success: true`, with `transaction` = the id in hex and `payer` = the owner, only when a node reports the transaction in a block with receipt result `SUCCESS` and H in `raw_data.data`, the owner being the payer. The `Transfer` logs the receipt holds whose sender is the owner are what the transaction moved, recorded as observed; a transaction whose receipt holds no `Transfer` log moved nothing, save a signed `transfer(to, 0)` of a token that logs nothing for zero, a transfer of zero to its `to`. A transaction is the payment only where one of those names `payTo`; one that names no account pays no one.
6. The resource server refuses a payment whose `data` does not decode to an H that it issued for that request and has not seen claimed.
7. Declarations for x402's facilitator-submitted family:
   - fee payer: the payer, self-funded;
   - replay primitive: the transaction id, exclusive to this payment;
   - validity window: bounded by `expiration`, at most 24 hours by the node;
   - duplicate submission: a node rejects a duplicate, and settlements are still deduplicated as rule 5 requires.
8. H is on chain as `raw_data.data`, inside the bytes whose SHA-256 is the transaction id the payer signed. The payment is read by the transaction id, the single-use value, which the chain derives from the signed `raw_data` alone, through a FullNode's `/walletsolidity` API for solidified data (the final mark), then its `/wallet` API at the head; H is read back by the same id through its `/walletsolidity` API. No index searches memos. The read is the profile `read-status`'s, with these facts:
   - **The marks** are `head` and `solidified`, the final mark; a position is a block number. The reference holds the `raw_data` the owner signed, the payment presented, its id, its owner as the payer, and the option's payee.
   - **Succeeded** means the receipt's result is `SUCCESS`, with H in `raw_data.data` and the reference's payer, the owner read from the signed bytes, as the owner. A result other than `SUCCESS` (`REVERT`, `OUT_OF_ENERGY`) is included with a failed result, which consumed the id: it moved nothing, and the account it names is its signed call's `to`.
   - **What moved** is each `Transfer` log of the receipt whose sender, the address its second topic names (`0x41` and the topic's last 20 bytes, TRC-20's `Transfer(address indexed _from, address indexed _to, uint256 _value)`), is the transaction's owner: its emitter, the recipient its third topic names and the amount in its data, recorded as observed and compared with no term, no sender recorded, named by the transaction id and the log's place among the receipt's logs. A receipt that holds no `Transfer` log, whose signed call is a `transfer(to, 0)`, names that call's `to`, recorded as a transfer of zero to it, named by the place `call`. On the act `payment` the transaction is the payment only where one of those names `payTo`, whatever contract emitted it; one that reaches only other accounts, and one that holds neither, names no payee's account. An agreement is held to no account. A transaction whose owner is the option's payee is never the act asked about, on either act.
   - **Who signed.** An answer that names a transaction names its owner, in base58check, as the signer, and its contract's `Permission_id`, field 5 of `Transaction.Contract` in `Tron.proto`, as the authority it acted under: `Permission_id 0`, the owner's permission, where the transaction names none, as a transaction of this form does (rule 5).
   - **The payment presented.** On `settled`, `presented` is `yes` where the settled transaction's id is the presented one's, the id being the SHA-256 of the `raw_data` the owner signed, and `no` otherwise.
   - **Absence and the history.** The id's validity has passed once the solidified head, read first, is two slots (6,000 ms) or more past `expiration` and neither lookup finds the transaction, each naming a latest block at its level at or past that head's time; a lookup whose node lags that head, or names no time, proves no absence: `pending`, `search-incomplete`. Absence also needs the history: the node's answer to `/walletsolidity/getblockbynum` at the position the caller gives; `{}` there from a node whose solidified head is at or past it is `unreadable`, `history-gap`, and from one below it proves no absence. With no position given, absence is `history-gap` unless the caller declares its node archival (`archival` on the reader). Absent, with the history shown, before the lapse: `pending`, `instrument-live`.
   - **The search for H.** The id is derived from all of `raw_data`, not from H alone, so a transaction the payer signs again over H has another id, and the read by id is no such search. Only a `failed` answer needs the search, which the read makes only then: it reads every block after the position the caller gives, or after the search position an earlier answer returned, up to the solidified head read first by `/walletsolidity/getblockbylimitnext`, and on to the full node's head by the same call under `/wallet`, at most 100 blocks a call and `TRON_BOUNDS.searchBlocks` blocks a read; a block missing from an answer is read alone by `getblockbynum`, and an answer over the reader's byte bound is read again in halves down to one block. Its key is a transaction's `data`, field 10 of its `raw_data_hex`, equal to H's LCP string, its hex digits in either case, as rules 5 and 6 read H there. Each transaction found is read by its id at its block's level: one of the form, its owner the payer and not the payee, that succeeded and, on a payment, names `payTo` is the act asked about, `settled` at `solidified` or, above the solidified head, `pending`, `below-mark`; any other is a foreign item where a reason holds, an act of another contract with H in its `data` being of another kind, and one with a failed result decides nothing. The search is complete when each block's `number` is the one asked, its `parentHash` the `blockID` of the block before it from the block at the position given, and the last block of each part that part's head. Its position is the last block linked, by number and `blockID`, at or below the solidified head; a link that fails starts it again at the position given; a block missing from the node below it, a found transaction whose receipt its lookup does not return, and a block whose transactions are not hex hold it at the last block linked; a transaction that is no protobuf message is an item it could not decode, held there and named by its id; `{}` for a block from a node at or past it is `history-gap`, and one block over the byte bound `too-large`.
   - **The gate.** The link shows the chain of headers, not that a block's answer lists every transaction of its block. Until a run on a test network shows that, at each level, a complete search proves no absence: a failed receipt, a receipt that names no payee's account, a transaction the payee owns and a lapse each answer `pending`, `search-incomplete`, with the search's position, and no `failed` but `before-position` is answered.
   - **Cases that cannot arise.** The read kit's cases that cannot arise on this ledger, each named by its reason or the opening words of its text: `not-found`, since the read always holds the value; *a value read consumed whose transaction the search has not found*, *a value unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a value whose validity passed, unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head*, since the value is the transaction id, read consumed only by the lookup that returns its transaction; *a read with no means to read the single-use value*, *search-incomplete is answered before not-found* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the read holds the id and looks it up at the solidified level and at the head; *the settled act's answer names every foreign item*, *instrument-live is answered before search-incomplete* and *instrument-live is answered before an item the search could not decode*, since the search is made only before a `failed`; *an act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since the search reads blocks by number; *the validity passed with the value unconsumed, read below the final mark*, since `expiration` is compared with the solidified head alone; and, until the run the gate names, the cases at coverage `full` whose answer is `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`.
9. **The agreement.** The payment form is also offered as an agreement, at the floor: 1 base unit, held per network and contract; rule 1 admits `"0"` only once a run on a network and contract shows that its `transfer(to, 0)` succeeds and logs a `Transfer`, and no contract is so shown yet. The buyer pays every fee (rule 4); another party can only delegate resources to the payer's account in advance, which pays no one sale's fee. The transaction must succeed (rule 8); a transaction the option's payee owns is no agreement and no proof, on either act. A transaction included with a failed result is no agreement; it consumed the id, and the buyer signs again.
10. **What a record proves**, as each pairing's `data.proves` states it:
    - `x402/exact/tron/lcp-trc20-memo`: The payer, the transaction's owner, signed a Tron transaction whose memo is this ATR's hash in LCP string form, and whose one contract calls transfer on the token. The network verified the signature, which covers the memo through the transaction id, and the receipt's result is SUCCESS, at the finality recorded. Where the receipt holds Transfer logs from the owner, the transaction paid, with each recipient and what moved; where it holds no Transfer log and the signed call is a transfer of zero, it paid zero to that call's recipient; where it holds neither, the payer signed, and no account is named. The owner is named as the signer, and its Permission_id as the authority. This does not show that amount, token or timing match the ATR's content.
