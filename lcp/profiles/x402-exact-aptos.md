**LCP profile `x402/exact/aptos`: x402's stock Aptos payment, paid after its agreement.**

This pairing serves x402's `exact` scheme on Aptos as its stock client writes it. No field of that payment carries H, so the pairing is at coverage `none`: the payment is taken only after an agreement carrying H is recorded, by any agreement option the resource server declares (`agreementOn` `any`), and the read finds the payment by what the payer signed, never by H.

**The payment**

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, as in the profile `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009`, and an `exact` option on an `aptos:` network whose chain id is 1 to 255, with no `extra.assetTransferMethod`, `extra.paymentFlow` absent or `"authorization"`, `asset` and `payTo` as `0x` and 64 hex digits, and an amount below 2^64.
2. The payer signs one Aptos transaction, the scheme's own: an entry function call of `0x1::primary_fungible_store::transfer`, or `0x1::fungible_asset::transfer`, with the type argument `0x1::fungible_asset::Metadata` and three arguments, the asset, the payee and the amount. An entry function's arguments are fixed, so none holds H. With the option's `extra.feePayer` the payer signs the fee-payer form, and the settler adds the fee payer's signature. `payload.transaction` is the base64 of the BCS bytes, or of x402's reference form, `{"transaction": [bytes], "senderAuthenticator": [bytes]}`; the transaction is at most 64 KiB, the chain's `max_transaction_size_in_bytes`.
3. The payment echoes `extensions` unchanged; `bound` returns the echoed H, which only locates the sale and is no proof.
4. A settler checks the payment by the scheme's own verification (x402's `scheme_exact_aptos.md`), which `@x402/aptos` implements: the network and chain id, the sender's signature, the fee payer where it sponsors, the transfer's asset, payee and amount, the expiry, the balance, and a simulation.
5. The resource server takes the payment only after the agreement for this H is recorded, and an agreement whose signer is the option's payee is no agreement.

**The reference**

6. `reference` gives the network, the sender, the signed sequence number, the signed expiry, a digest of the signed transfer (SHA-256 over the RFC 8785 form of the sender, the sequence number, the function, its type arguments and its arguments), the signed `max_gas_amount` and `gas_unit_price`, and the option's payee, the `payTo` of the option it is given, the key of the two party tests and no search key. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not that option, compared by RFC 8785 form (`x402/accepted-not-option`). A transaction hash a settler names is a hint, since a fee payer's signature changes it.

**The read**

`status` reads through an Aptos node's REST API the caller supplies. It compares no amount, asset or time with the option.

7. **Found by.** The payment is the sender's committed transaction at the signed sequence number, `GET /v1/accounts/{sender}/transactions?start=n&limit=1`, a named hash read first as a hint. It is the act asked about where it succeeded, its sender is not the option's payee, it is no act the caller gave (by its reference's sender and sequence number, or by its hash), and it names the payee's account, whatever it moved. Whether it is the payment presented is `presented` on `settled`, which decides no answer: `yes` where every signed member the reference holds equals the REST answer's (the transfer's digest, the expiry, and the gas members), `no` where one differs, `unshown` where the answer does not show one. Neither the fee payer's address nor the chain id is compared: x402's Aptos scheme states that the sender's signature does not cover the fee payer's address (`scheme_exact_aptos.md`), which a run is yet to show, and the REST answer carries no chain id, the read holding the ledger's own (`GET /v1`) to the network instead. A transaction at the number with another payload than an entry function (a script, a multisig) is read for its deposits as rule 8 states, and is not the payment presented. A named hash is passed over where it names no user transaction, and a transaction the node holds uncommitted ends nothing; the lookup by sequence number is still made.
8. **The account an act names.** Each fungible-asset deposit the transaction made, by its `0x1::fungible_asset::Deposit` event, `{store, amount}`: the store's owner, from the transaction's `0x1::object::ObjectCore` change at the store's address, is the account it reached, and its asset is the `metadata` of the `0x1::fungible_asset::FungibleStore` change there. What moved records each deposit with its asset, amount and owner, named by the committed transaction and its place, the index of its `Deposit` event among the transaction's `events`. A payment names the payee's account where one deposit's owner is the option's payee. The reader returns each committed transaction with its `events` and its `changes` whole; an answer without either, or with a deposit whose owner it does not show, is `unreadable`, `unparseable`, and is never read as a transaction that deposited nothing.
9. **The single-use value** is the sender's sequence number. The prologue takes a sequence-number transaction only at the account's current number and the epilogue raises it by one whether it succeeded or aborted (aptos-core `aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/transaction_validation.move`), so a transaction at the number consumed it. Where the lookup returns nothing at the number, the read takes the account's sequence number, `GET /v1/accounts/{sender}`, with that answer's ledger time (`X-Aptos-Ledger-TimestampUsec`) and history floor (`X-Aptos-Ledger-Oldest-Version`). A number the account has passed, whose transaction the lookup did not return, is `pending`, `search-incomplete`. One it has not passed can still land while the ledger's time in whole seconds is below the signed expiry: `pending`, `instrument-live`; the prologue takes a transaction only while `timestamp::now_seconds() < txn_expiration_time`, so from that second it is `failed`, `instrument-unused`.
10. **History.** A number passed with nothing returned at it, and an expiry passed, are decided only where the history floor of each answer is at or below the position given (the issue position); otherwise, or with no position given, the answer is `unreadable`, `history-gap`, decided before `instrument-unused`, and the caller reads its declared archive endpoint.
11. **Answers.** Committed with `success` false at the number, it consumed the number and moved nothing: the account it names is the destination the payer signed, `arguments[1]` of `0x1::primary_fungible_store::transfer` (another payload names none the read can take), and it is `failed`, `transaction-failed` where that is the option's payee, else `failed`, `instrument-consumed` (`read-status.md` rule 20). Committed and not the act asked about, signed by the payee, given by the caller, or naming no account of the payee's, is `failed`, `instrument-consumed`, naming it with what it moved. A transaction at or before the position given is `failed`, `before-position`. One mark: a committed transaction is final, and the position is its ledger version. Coverage `none` takes no search for H.

**What a record proves**

12. That an account signed an agreement transaction carrying H before this payment was taken, which shows neither that the account is the payer nor what the agreement moved; that the payer's signed transaction does not carry H; and that the read found the payment by its sender and sequence number, signed by an account other than the option's payee and depositing to a store the payee owns, what it moved recorded as observed.

**The read kit**

13. Declared inapplicable to the read kit: `below-mark`, since a committed transaction is final and there is one mark; `not-found`, since the read reads the account's sequence number; and a lookup beside an item a search could not decode, since the read takes no search.
