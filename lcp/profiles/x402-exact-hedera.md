**LCP profile `x402/exact/hedera`: the ATR hash as the transaction memo.**

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, H being the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes as `0x` lowercase hex, and L an `https` URL serving those bytes. The option's `network` is `hedera:mainnet`, `hedera:testnet`, `hedera:previewnet` or `hedera:devnet`.
2. The client fetches L, hashes the bytes received, and compares with H as 32 decoded bytes; on a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client signs one `TransactionBody` whose `memo` is H's LCP string, `lcp:sha256:0x` and the 64 lowercase hex digits of H, 77 bytes of the 100 a memo holds, and echoes `extensions` unchanged. The body's `transactionID` names the option's `extra.feePayer` and a valid start the buyer chooses; its `cryptoTransfer` debits the buyer and credits the option's `payTo` by the option's `amount`, in HBAR where the option's `asset` is `0.0.0` and otherwise in the token the `asset` names. The stock x402 client writes no memo, and its payment is refused by rule 4.
4. The server refuses, before anything is sent, a payment whose memo is not byte for byte H's LCP string: an empty memo `hedera/no-memo`, and any other, one with an uppercase hex digit among them, `hedera/memo-not-lcp`, since the read of rule 11 settles only on that exact string; and an H it issued for that request and has not seen claimed. It refuses also a body whose valid start is later than the time of the check, `hedera/valid-start-future` (rule 7).
5. H is public in the transaction record's memo, readable by transaction id from any Mirror Node.
6. A `TransactionList`, the bodies the SDK freezes for several nodes, is taken only where every body equals the first in every field but `nodeAccountID`; otherwise it is refused `hedera/list-inconsistent`. Every body of a list taken is the same payment, and a settler submits any one of them to the node it names.
7. **Life.** The chain holds a body's `transactionValidDuration` to its `transaction.maxValidDuration`, 180 seconds, and takes no valid start later than its consensus time. A body whose valid duration is above 180 seconds is refused `hedera/valid-duration`, and one whose valid start is later than the time of the check `hedera/valid-start-future`: by the client's `build` at the time of the call, and by the server's `bound` and `reference` at the time each runs. A signed payment stays payable for its valid duration, counted from its valid start.
8. **Who pays the fee, and who is the buyer.** The account the transaction id names, the option's `extra.feePayer`, is the fee payer: the seller's settler, which adds its signature and submits the buyer's bytes unchanged. The buyer is the one account the signed body debits, and never the fee payer: a body that debits the fee payer is refused `hedera/payer-not-buyer`, and one that is no crypto transfer, or debits no account or more than one, `hedera/tx-malformed`. The body lists the buyer's debit and the payee's credit alone, so the form has no split. A transaction id is written `shard.realm.num@seconds.nanos`, as the SDK spells it, and `shard.realm.num-seconds-nanos`, the nanoseconds in nine digits, as the Mirror Node spells it, in whose spelling every read key is.
9. **The reference** a read takes holds, from the body presented: the network; the transaction id, the payment's single-use value, since the chain takes one transaction under an id and only the fee payer's key can land another body under it; the valid start, to the nanosecond, and the valid duration; the memo; the debited account, the payer, which is the signer the read names and the search's key; and the node of each body the list holds, the maximum fee and the transfer rows, the rest of the payment presented (rule 12). From the option the payment answers it takes the `payTo`, the payee, the key of the read's two party tests. A payment whose `accepted` is not the option the reference is given, compared by its RFC 8785 form, is refused `x402/accepted-not-option`. The reference holds no member for comparing an amount or an asset with the option.
10. **Succeeded** means that the Mirror Node's entry for the transaction id with `nonce` 0 and `scheduled` false has `result` `SUCCESS`; its `memo_base64` decodes to exactly H's LCP string; and its `transfers` or `token_transfers` debit the buyer with `is_approval` false, what they credit recorded as observed. HBAR rows are read with each account's `staking_reward_transfers` row taken out and `charged_tx_fee` added back to the account the transaction id names, since `transfers` holds each account's total change, fee and reward included.
11. **The read** (`status`, under the profile `read-status`) goes through the Mirror Node's REST interface. Its one mark is `consensus`, a consensus result, which is final; positions are consensus timestamps. It reads the mirror's latest consensus timestamp first, the end of its newest block, and then the entries under the transaction id, so that an entry the mirror ingests between the two reads is seen. An entry under the id decides unless it is a `DUPLICATE_TRANSACTION`, a copy sent through another node, or an `INVALID_NODE_ACCOUNT` or `INVALID_PAYER_SIGNATURE`, a node's failure after which a valid transaction under the id is still handled. A deciding entry consumed the id: where it succeeded, carries H and is the buyer's own act (rule 14), it is the act asked about, for the act `payment` only where it also credits the payee; any other result under the id is the act's own failed result, which moved nothing and names the account the body presented credits, by this rule and not by a read, since its rows show the fee alone and the id names the fee payer, whose key could land another body under it, `transaction-failed` where that account is the payee; and a `SUCCESS` that is not the act is `instrument-consumed`, with its reason where it carries H. With no deciding entry, and the mirror holding records back to the valid start, the id is unconsumed: `pending`, `instrument-live`, until the valid start plus the valid duration is strictly before the timestamp read first, and `instrument-unused` after it. A precheck refusal reaches no record and consumes nothing. Every `failed` but `before-position` waits for the search of rule 13, read whole. Records that begin after the valid start, or after the position the search starts from, answer `unreadable`, `history-gap`.
12. **The payment presented.** A `settled` answer states `presented`: `yes` where every member the mirror shows of the body that landed equals the body presented, `no` where one differs, `unshown` where the mirror does not show one. The mirror's `bytes`, which would carry the signed transaction whole, is nullable, so the members are compared one by one: the transaction id with its account and valid start (`transaction_id`, `valid_start_timestamp`); the memo (`memo_base64`); the valid duration (`valid_duration_seconds`); the maximum fee (`max_fee`); the node (`node`), any the presented list names; and the transfer rows, each the body lists equal to a row of the entry and no other row left: the HBAR rows read as rule 10 reads them, then the rows of the node the landed entry names, of the network's fee account `0.0.98`, of the staking and node reward accounts `0.0.800` and `0.0.801` and of the fee collection account `0.0.802`, whatever their sign, and every row that then nets to zero, set aside, since the charged fee is spread over those accounts; and the token rows. What the mirror does not show of a body is not compared, so a landed body that differs from the presented one only there reads `yes`; a body that itself lists the node or one of those accounts, or a token transfer that assessed a custom fee (`assessed_custom_fees`), reads `unshown`. An act settled under another transaction id reads `no`. The comparison decides no answer.
13. **The search for H.** A buyer that signs again under another transaction id can land a body that carries H while the presented one lapses or fails, so every `failed` but `before-position` comes only once a search for H is read whole and finds no act asked about. It is made once the validity has passed with the id unconsumed, or once a deciding entry under the id has consumed it, and reads the Mirror Node's list of the payer's transactions, `GET /api/v1/transactions` with `account.id` the debited account the reference names, `timestamp` `gt:` the position the transaction must come after, or the search position an earlier answer returned, and `lte:` the timestamp read first, `order` `asc` and `limit` 100, page by page, at most 8 pages a read, each next page asked `gt:` the consensus timestamp of the last entry read, which is the page the answer's `links.next` names, no two transactions sharing a consensus timestamp. No query selects by memo, so every entry is read and its `memo_base64` compared with H's string byte for byte. An entry that decides as an entry under the id does and is the act asked about is answered `settled`, under whatever transaction id; any other entry that carries H decides nothing and stops no search. The search is read whole where every page up to that timestamp was read, the last naming no `links.next`, and the mirror holds records back to its start. A page left when the read's bound ends, or a page the mirror refuses, leaves it not whole: `pending`, `search-incomplete`, with the search's position, the consensus timestamp of the last entry read, from which the next read resumes. An entry the read cannot decode is named by its transaction id and holds the search one nanosecond before its consensus timestamp, the read going on through the rest of its range. Three facts the search rests on are not yet shown by a run: that `account.id` selects a transaction in which the account appears only in `token_transfers`, as the buyer does in an HTS transfer whose fee the settler paid; that a mirror balanced over several replicas answers every page of one search from one view; and that a mirror holding a block holds every transaction in it. Until a run shows the three, the search proves no absence, read to its last page or not: no reader and no caller states them shown, and the read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, at the search's position, where it would answer `failed`; the pairing's vectors hold that answer.
14. **The parties, and what moved.** An entry's signer is the one account its rows debit, rule 10's adjustments made, or, where they debit none, the account its transaction id names. It is the buyer's own act where that one account is the debited account the reference names and its debit spends no allowance (`is_approval` false); an act whose debited accounts include the option's payee is the payee's own signature and no act of the payer's, a foreign item `payee-signed` on either act. It names the payee's account where its rows credit the payee, in any asset and any amount; it names another account where they credit another, and none where they credit only the node and the fee accounts. An entry of another transaction type than `CRYPTOTRANSFER` that carries H is an act of another kind. What moved names each credit by the transaction id and its place, `<asset>/<account>`, the asset, `0.0.0` or the token id, and the credited account, since the mirror holds each account's total change in an asset as one row, the node's and the fee accounts' rows apart; the asset and the amount are recorded and compared with nothing. An entry that carries H, succeeded and is not the act asked about is a foreign item, with the first reason of `read-status`'s list that holds; an entry that carries H and failed is none.
15. **The transfer-executor**, `x402/exact/hedera/transfer-executor`, is at coverage `none`: nothing the payer signs carries H, which reaches the payer only in the option's extension. Its read takes the transaction the facilitator names as the act, in its merged consensus record, the user entry and its child entries, read as rule 10 reads them, and makes no search. Its signer is the one account the record debits: the payer where its digest is the one `reference` recorded, the payee where it is the option's payee. It pays where it credits the payee. Its `reference` refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not the option it is given, `x402/accepted-not-option`, as rule 9 states. The named transaction id is single-use: a deciding entry under it consumed it, a failed result, the act's own, answering `transaction-failed` and naming the payee by this rule and not by a read, since the read does not decode the authorization the payer signed, and a success that is not the payer's payment to the payee `instrument-consumed`; with none, it is `instrument-live` until its valid start plus the chain's `transaction.maxValidDuration`, 180 seconds, is strictly before the mirror's latest consensus timestamp, and `instrument-unused` after it, the mirror holding records back to that valid start. A `settled` answer states `presented` `unshown`: the read decodes no authorization the payer signed, so it shows no member of the payment presented. The form waits on an executor contract a payer uses with published source and a client that builds the form, and is not offered until then.
16. **The agreement.** A payment of this pairing can itself be the agreement, held by its binding alone: its smallest amount is 1 tinybar, or 1 unit of an HTS token, since a transfer of zero debits no account and needs no signature of the buyer's; its fee is paid by the seller's settler, the account the transaction id names; it must succeed, as rule 10 states, and no failed result of rule 11 is an agreement; and a body the payee's own account signed is no agreement, whatever it moved. An agreement is held to no account.
17. **The read kit.** The cases of `read-status.json` run through this pairing's read and the transfer-executor's; a case is skipped only where this profile states that it cannot arise, and these are its statements. On both: every case that answers `below-mark`, since every record the mirror serves is final. On this pairing: every case that answers `not-found`, since the read holds the single-use value, the transaction id, and reads the entries under it, an id with no entry that decides being `pending`, `instrument-live`, until the lapse; *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, *a value unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head*, *a value whose validity passed, unconsumed at the final mark and consumed at the latest head* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the mirror's one mark is its latest consensus timestamp, read first, at which the id is read; *a value read consumed whose transaction the search has not found*, since the id is read consumed only by an entry under it, which names the transaction; *a read with no means to read the single-use value*, since the read holds the id; *a payment act carrying H that transferred zero* and *an act carrying H that transferred zero*, since a transfer of zero debits no account and the buyer is the account the body debits; *the settled act's answer names every foreign item*, *instrument-live is answered before search-incomplete* and *instrument-live is answered before an item the search could not decode*, since the search is made only before a `failed`; *an act the read cannot place against the position given*, its three cases, and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since every entry carries its consensus timestamp; and, while the search proves no absence (rule 13), every case at coverage `full` that answers `instrument-unused`, `instrument-consumed` or `transaction-failed`, which the search's gate holds, the vectors holding the read's answer, `search-incomplete`. A second act of the payer is run, made under its own transaction id. On the transfer-executor, whose read takes the transaction the settler names by its id and makes no search: every case that answers `not-found`, and *at coverage none, a lookup read whole beside an item the search could not decode*.
18. **The payee's preconditions,** which no validation reads: the account exists; for an HTS asset it is associated with the token or holds a free automatic association; it does not require a receiver's signature, since a credited account with `receiverSigRequired` set must sign the transaction; and for an HTS token it is granted KYC where the token has a KYC key, and is not frozen for the token. A payment that fails for want of one consumed its transaction id and is answered `transaction-failed`.
19. **What a record proves**, as each pairing's `data.proves` states it; a record fills in the values set in angle brackets:
    - `x402/exact/hedera`: The payer's own signed act carries this ATR's hash: a Hedera crypto transfer whose memo is the hash's LCP string reached consensus with SUCCESS, debiting the payer, the one account its rows debit, and paid the account it credits, recorded with the asset and amount it moved. The memo is in the public transaction record, readable by transaction id. It compares no amount, asset or time with the ATR's terms.
    - `x402/exact/hedera/transfer-executor`: Before this payment was taken, an account signed an agreement carrying this ATR's hash on `<network>`, recorded in `<transaction>`; this does not show that the account is the payer. The payment is an x402 transferExecutor payment on Hedera: the payer's executor contract moved the funds under an authorization the seller did not read, and the facilitator submitted the transaction. The hash reached the payer only in the challenge's extension, and nothing the payer signed carries it. The read of the named transaction's merged consensus record found that its one debited account is the payer recorded when the payment was presented, and that it credited the payee. It compares no amount, asset or time with the ATR's terms.
