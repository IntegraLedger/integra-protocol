**LCP profile `mpp/charge`: the ATR hash in an MPP challenge.**

1. The challenge's `opaque` map carries `legalContext` = `lcp:sha256:` + H and `legalContextUrl`, an `https` URL that serves the ATR's exact bytes.
2. The challenge `id` is the base64url (no padding) of H's 32 bytes, then `.` and the challenge's position, 0 to 31, except that a Tempo `subscription` challenge's id is exactly the base64url (no padding) of H. The resource server gives each challenge of a sale its own position, its charge challenges and its agreement challenges together, so that a sale holds at most 32 challenges and no two share an id; a response that would hold two challenges with one id is refused.
3. The client fetches the URL, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares it with H as 32 bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
4. EVM `authorization` and `permit2`: the signed nonce, or the witness `challengeHash`, is keccak256(UTF-8(JSON.stringify([id, realm]))), the id and realm framed as a JSON array of two strings, as MPP's `mppx` SDK computes it from 0.13.1. A nonce or witness that is keccak256 of the id's UTF-8 followed by the realm's is refused. Tempo, pull or push: the client signs the attribution memo MPP's SDKs write as its primary `transferWithMemo` memo, whose last 7 bytes are keccak256(UTF-8(id))[0..6] and whose bytes 5–14 are keccak256(UTF-8(realm))[0..9]. The memo binds H only through those 7 bytes: whoever assembles the ATR can construct a second ATR whose id gives the same 7 bytes.
5. The ATR's `mpp` slot holds the challenge's bound parameters (`realm`, `method`, `intent`, `request`, `expires`, `digest`, `header`, `opaque`) as issued, so the id is bound to them through H. The issued digest of a challenge is SHA-256 over the RFC 8785 form of those parameters, with `request` decoded and its carrier removed, and `opaque` decoded without its `legalContext`, `legalContextUrl` and `legalContextAgreementUrl` members, omitted when that leaves it empty. For a pairing whose quote or invoice the resource server writes after H (`nearintents`; Lightning's charge and session), the slot holds, and the issued digest covers, the challenge without the members written after H, so the challenge the server sends once they are written has the digest of the one the ATR binds. An agreement challenge, which no slot holds, is held to the digest the resource server recorded for its agreement option; its bound parameters, `expires` among them, are repeated exactly at every re-challenge, and once that `expires` has passed while no agreement payment has been taken for H and the offer has not expired, the option is composed anew and its digest recorded in place of the old.
6. EVM `transaction` and `hash` carry H in no signed or landed value. When `opaque` carries `legalContextAgreementUrl`, the client first pays that URL, whose payment carries H, and pays the challenge only after its 200 receipt.
7. **Lightning** (`method="lightning"`): the challenge's BOLT11 invoice (`methodDetails.invoice`, or `depositInvoice` for a session) has exactly one `h` field, equal to H, and no `d`; the request carries `paymentHash` equal to the invoice's. The client checks, as BOLT11 requires, that `h` is the SHA-256 of the ATR it fetched before paying.
8. **`usdc`, EVM and Gateway:** the signed nonce, or the TransferSpec salt, is usdc's derivation over the challenge `id`, so it commits to H. **Stacks:** the SIP-010 `transfer` memo is `some` of H's 32 bytes.
9. **`card` and `stripe`:** the request's `externalId`, or `methodDetails.metadata.legal_context`, is `lcp:sha256:` + H. The buyer's card or Stripe token does not carry H; the challenge and the seller's reference do.
10. **The server's order.** A resource server makes these steps in this order, and nothing before step 4 can move money:
    1. Two checks, which a stock MPP server does not make. The challenge belongs to this agreement: its id and `opaque` name H (rules 1 and 2), and its issued digest equals the one the server recorded for H (rule 5). The payment carries H: the pairing's own check of how its credential carries H, or its link, in the pairing's form, which never judges what the payment pays (its amount, asset or payee). For a credential whose payer already broadcast the payment, the server first reads the landed transaction, a read that moves nothing, and the check reads H there. For a pairing whose payer signs nothing that carries H (rules 6 and 9), the second check is the pairing's link, where it has one, and the server's own record that the agreement for this H is recorded.
    2. Validation that moves nothing, where the method's library separates it from its broadcast.
    3. The server's own record that this H's act, its agreement or its payment, is now taken, made once for each H and act. A later credential of an open channel is held to its channel and makes no such record. The record stands until the payment's instrument is shown consumed or lapsed.
    4. Only then any call that can move money: a broadcast, a one-step verification that settles, or a processor's charge, made by whichever party broadcasts.

    What a payment pays is checked only by the verification of the party asked to settle it, under its own protocol. The vectors are `mpp-server-checks.json`.
11. **An echoed `opaque` that is an object.** A credential whose echoed challenge carries `opaque` as the object of its members, as `mppx` releases before 0.6.4 write it, is read by those members, each a string: every check above reads it as it reads the base64url form. Any other value of `opaque` is refused.
12. **An MPP payment that is an x402 payment.** Where the payer's signed act is the same on both protocols, the `evm` method's `authorization` credential and the `usdc` method's EVM credential, the credential and its challenge map to x402's `exact` scheme with EIP-3009, which an x402 facilitator settles: `PaymentRequirements` `{scheme: "exact", network: "eip155:" + chainId, amount, asset: currency, payTo: recipient, maxTimeoutSeconds, extra: {name, version}}`, with `amount`, `currency` and `recipient` the request's and `chainId` its `methodDetails` (on `usdc`, `methodDetails.evm`); `PaymentPayload` `{x402Version: 2, accepted: those requirements, payload: {signature, authorization: {from, to, value, validAfter, validBefore, nonce}}}`, the credential's own members. The token's EIP-712 `name` and `version`, which x402's verification reads from `extra` and no MPP challenge carries, are the resource server's input. x402's verification reads no `maxTimeoutSeconds`, which its schema requires to be positive; the mapping writes the window the payer signed, `validBefore` less `validAfter`. Only the nonce's derivation differs (rules 4 and 8), which x402's facilitator does not check. Splits, a Permit2 witness, a Gateway burn intent and every non-EVM form have no x402 twin and are not mapped. The vectors are `mpp-x402-mapping.json`.
