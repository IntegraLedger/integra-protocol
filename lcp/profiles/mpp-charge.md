**LCP profile `mpp/charge`: the ATR hash in an MPP challenge.**

1. The challenge's `opaque` map carries `legalContext` = `lcp:sha256:` + H and `legalContextUrl`, an `https` URL that serves the ATR's exact bytes.
2. The challenge `id` is the base64url (no padding) of H's 32 bytes, then `.` and the challenge's position, 0 to 31, except that a Tempo `subscription` challenge's id is exactly the base64url (no padding) of H. The resource server gives each challenge of a sale its own position, its charge challenges and its agreement challenges together, so that a sale holds at most 32 challenges and no two share an id; a response that would hold two challenges with one id is refused.
3. The client fetches the URL, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares it with H as 32 bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
4. EVM `authorization` and `permit2`: the signed nonce, and on `permit2` the witness `challengeHash` as well as the permit's `nonce`, is keccak256(UTF-8(JSON.stringify([id, realm]))), the id and realm framed as a JSON array of two strings, as MPP's `mppx` SDK computes it from 0.13.1. A nonce or witness that is keccak256 of the id's UTF-8 followed by the realm's is refused, and so is a permit whose `nonce` is not that value (`mpp/nonce-not-challenge-hash`): the read keys the charge on the payer's Permit2 bit at it.
5. The ATR's `mpp` slot holds the challenge's bound parameters (`realm`, `method`, `intent`, `request`, `expires`, `digest`, `header`, `opaque`) as issued, so the id is bound to them through H. The issued digest of a challenge is SHA-256 over the RFC 8785 form of those parameters, with `request` decoded and its carrier removed, and `opaque` decoded without its `legalContext`, `legalContextUrl` and `legalContextAgreementUrl` members, omitted when that leaves it empty. For a pairing whose quote or invoice the resource server writes after H (`nearintents`; Lightning's charge and session), the slot holds, and the issued digest covers, the challenge without the members written after H, so the challenge the server sends once they are written has the digest of the one the ATR binds. An agreement challenge, which no slot holds, is held to the digest the resource server recorded for its agreement option; its bound parameters, `expires` among them, are repeated exactly at every re-challenge, and once that `expires` has passed while no agreement payment has been taken for H and the offer has not expired, the option is composed anew and its digest recorded in place of the old.
6. EVM `transaction` and `hash` carry H in no signed or landed value. When `opaque` carries `legalContextAgreementUrl`, the client first pays that URL, whose payment carries H, and pays the challenge only after its 200 receipt. A `hash` challenge, whose payer broadcasts the payment itself, is absent from every response the resource server sends before the agreement for its H is recorded.
7. **Lightning** (`method="lightning"`): the challenge's BOLT11 invoice (`methodDetails.invoice`, or `depositInvoice` for a session) has exactly one `h` field, equal to H, and no `d`; the request carries `paymentHash` equal to the invoice's. The client checks, as BOLT11 requires, that `h` is the SHA-256 of the ATR it fetched before paying.
8. **`usdc`, EVM and Gateway:** the signed nonce, or the TransferSpec salt, is usdc's derivation over the challenge `id`, so it commits to H. As v00 of the `usdc` draft requires, an EVM profile's `decimals` is 6 (else `mpp/request-malformed`), and its `credentialTypes` is absent or `["authorization"]`, a Gateway profile's absent or `["transfer"]` (else `mpp/credential-types`). An EVM profile's `request.currency` is the native USDC token contract Circle publishes for its `chainId`, as the draft requires servers to verify; lcp holds Circle's published table (*USDC Contract Addresses*, read on 2026-11-17), and refuses a currency that is not that chain's token, or a chain the table lacks, `mpp/usdc-not-native`. On Gateway the salt rides in the burn intent the payer signs, and the destination network's mint carries the whole TransferSpec; its read is the Gateway Minter's `AttestationUsed(token, recipient, transferSpecHash, sourceDomain, sourceDepositor, sourceSigner, value)` whose third topic is the TransferSpec's keccak-256 hash, rebuilt from the challenge with its salt recomputed, or the minter's `isTransferSpecHashUsed(hash)`; the mint's recipient, the TransferSpec's `destinationRecipient`, is the account the payment names; and its lapse is the intent's signed `maxBlockHeight` passed on the source network at that network's final mark with the hash unused on the destination's minter. Until a run shows that read, the Gateway form is at coverage `none`, its salt a link, and it follows an agreement. **Stacks:** the SIP-010 `transfer` memo is `some` of H's 32 bytes.
9. **`card` and `stripe`:** the request's `externalId`, or `methodDetails.metadata.legal_context`, is `lcp:sha256:` + H. The buyer's card or Stripe token does not carry H; the challenge and the seller's reference do.
10. **The server's order.** A resource server makes these steps in this order, and nothing before step 4 can move money:
    1. Two checks, which a stock MPP server does not make. The challenge belongs to this agreement: its id and `opaque` name H (rules 1 and 2), and its issued digest equals the one the server recorded for H (rule 5). The payment carries H: the pairing's own check of how its credential carries H, or its link, in the pairing's form, which never judges what the payment pays (its amount, asset or payee). For a credential whose payer already broadcast the payment, the server first reads the landed transaction, a read that moves nothing, and the check reads H there. For a pairing whose payer signs nothing that carries H (rules 6 and 9), the second check is the pairing's link, where it has one, and the server's own record that the agreement for this H is recorded.
    2. Validation that moves nothing, where the method's library separates it from its broadcast.
    3. The server's own record that this H's act, its agreement or its payment, is now taken, made once for each H and act. A later credential of an open channel is held to its channel and makes no such record. The record stands until the payment's instrument is shown consumed or lapsed.
    4. Only then any call that can move money: a broadcast, a one-step verification that settles, or a processor's charge, made by whichever party broadcasts.

    What a payment pays is checked only by the verification of the party asked to settle it, under its own protocol. The vectors are `mpp-server-checks.json`.
11. **An echoed `opaque` that is an object.** A credential whose echoed challenge carries `opaque` as the object of its members, as `mppx` releases before 0.6.4 write it, is read by those members, each a string: every check above reads it as it reads the base64url form. Any other value of `opaque` is refused.
12. **An MPP payment that is an x402 payment.** Where the payer's signed act is the same on both protocols, the `evm` method's `authorization` credential and the `usdc` method's EVM credential, the credential and its challenge map to x402's `exact` scheme with EIP-3009, which an x402 facilitator settles: `PaymentRequirements` `{scheme: "exact", network: "eip155:" + chainId, amount, asset: currency, payTo: recipient, maxTimeoutSeconds, extra: {name, version}}`, with `amount`, `currency` and `recipient` the request's and `chainId` its `methodDetails` (on `usdc`, `methodDetails.evm`); `PaymentPayload` `{x402Version: 2, accepted: those requirements, payload: {signature, authorization: {from, to, value, validAfter, validBefore, nonce}}}`, the credential's own members. The token's EIP-712 `name` and `version`, which x402's verification reads from `extra` and no MPP challenge carries, are the resource server's input. x402's verification reads no `maxTimeoutSeconds`, which its schema requires to be positive; the mapping writes the window the payer signed, `validBefore` less `validAfter`. Only the nonce's derivation differs (rules 4 and 8), which x402's facilitator does not check. Splits, a Permit2 witness, a Gateway burn intent and every non-EVM form have no x402 twin and are not mapped. The vectors are `mpp-x402-mapping.json`.
13. **Tempo:** a charge carries H as the memo of a split the resource server places, in the transaction the payer signs; profile `mpp/charge/tempo/split-memo`. A Tempo charge challenge offers its forms by `supportedModes` alone, and one that carries `methodDetails.zones` or sets `machineTokenEnabled` is refused. Its read takes the transaction's nonce by its nonce key at Tempo's one mark, `finalized`, and at the latest head, and searches the receive-policy guard's held transfers beside `TransferWithMemo` (that profile, rules 8 and 10 to 13).
14. **The agreement, on `evm` `authorization`, `usdc` EVM and `evm` `permit2`.** Each pairing's payment can itself be an agreement: an agreement challenge has its own id, so its own nonce or witness, apart from every charge's. The floor is 0 on `authorization`, offered as 0 only where the network's settler declares that it takes zero and otherwise 1 base unit; 1 base unit on `usdc`, whose draft asks a positive amount; and 1 base unit on `permit2`, whose primary transfer must be above zero. The settler pays the fee. Success is a receipt whose `status` is 1 and in which the token emitted `AuthorizationUsed(authorizer, nonce)`, the nonce derived from the agreement challenge and the authorizer the payer, paired with its `Transfer` (rule 15); on `permit2`, the payer's `Transfer` in a transaction whose input carries the derived value as the nonce, or the challenge's witness word, of the Permit2 call whose owner is the payer, and the payer's bit for the call's own nonce set at that block and unset before. A reverted settlement, or a successful transaction with no such use, consumes nothing and is no agreement; a cancellation consumes the nonce for that authorizer on that token for good.
15. **The read** (`read-status`), on the EVM pairings. Marks: `latest`, `safe` and `finalized`, the final one `finalized`. The position recorded at issue is the `finalized` block's number. On every pairing the read takes only an asset contract's own `Transfer`: on Arc each USDC transfer also emits a `Transfer` from the system emitter `0xfffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffe`, in 18 decimals, and a plain native send emits that log alone; it is neither paired, nor recorded as what moved, nor a second transfer. A transaction with a failed result moved nothing, and the account it names is the destination in what the payer signed: the recipient its decoded call names.
    - `authorization` and `usdc` EVM: the single-use value is the derived nonce, which each token keeps for itself. The search reads every contract's `AuthorizationUsed` with the payer and that nonce as topics, by a filter that names no address, from the position the caller gives to the latest head, in pages of at most 10,000 blocks, halved for an endpoint that refuses a page's range; a hit on a contract other than `currency` counts only where the payer's signature over the authorization its transaction carries verifies, and one whose authorization cannot be decoded holds the search at it. A cancellation is `currency`'s `AuthorizationCanceled` with that nonce. A use is paired with its `Transfer` by the token's two logs where the transaction holds one use and one transfer of that authorizer on that contract, and otherwise by decoding the call that consumed it. On a charge the act pays where that `Transfer` goes to the request's `recipient`, whatever its token and value. The value is read at the `finalized` block and at the latest head. The lapse: `authorizationState(payer, nonce)` false on `currency` at a `finalized` block whose timestamp is at or past `validBefore`, and at the latest head, with the search read whole. The payment presented is the token, `from`, `to`, `value` and `nonce`, read from the two paired logs; the window is no member. Cases that cannot arise on these pairings (`read-status` rule 28): `transaction-failed` and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*, since a reverted settlement consumes no authorization nonce; `not-found`, since each read holds its single-use value; *a read with no means to read the single-use value* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since each read holds its single-use value and reads it at a `finalized` block and at the latest block; *an act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, since every log and transaction the read meets is in a block; and *each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place*, since an EIP-3009 use moves one transfer.
    - `permit2`: the search reads every contract's `Transfer` from the payer, a hit counting only where its transaction's input carries, in a Permit2 witness call whose owner is the payer, decoded, naming the hit's token, recipient and amount, the derived value as its nonce or the challenge's witness word, the EIP-712 hash of `PaymentWitness{challengeHash, externalId}` that the presented permit carries (draft-evm-charge-00 at tempoxyz/mpp-specs 08e7dd87, lines 449-483), and the payer's bit for the call's own nonce is set at the hit's block and unset at the block before; a call under another nonce carrying the witness word is an act carrying H that consumed no value of the reference's; a cancellation is Permit2's `UnorderedNonceInvalidation` with the payer as owner; a transaction that carries the value in no such call but holds the payer's EIP-3009 use of it that the token logged, the `authorization` charge of the same challenge, holds an act of another kind, which consumes no Permit2 bit. The value is read at the `finalized` block and at the latest head. The lapse: the bit unset at a `finalized` block past `deadline`, and at the latest head. Cases that cannot arise: `transaction-failed` and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*, since a reverted settlement consumes no Permit2 bit; `not-found`, since the read holds its value; *a read with no means to read the single-use value* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since it holds its value and reads it at a `finalized` block and at the latest block; the three cases of *an act the read cannot place against the position given* and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*, as above; and *an act the option's payee signed*, *an act carrying H under another account's signature*, *a transaction for which two reasons hold* and *the settled act's answer names every foreign item*, since each gives an act carrying H that another account signed and that consumed nothing, and the search reads the payer's own transfers and cancellations, so every act it meets is the payer's.
    - `transaction` and `hash` (coverage `none`): the act is the transaction the credential carries or names. What it moved is recorded with each recipient, and it pays where one transfer goes to the request's `recipient`. One that reverted is `transaction-failed` at the final mark where the `transfer` call that is the transaction's own pays the `recipient`, and otherwise `failed`, `instrument-consumed`, since its inclusion consumed the payer's nonce; one that succeeded with no transfer to the `recipient` is `failed`, `instrument-consumed`, `other-account`. A relayed transaction that never lands signs no bound and is never `failed` by lapse; while the named transaction is not found the answer is `pending`, `not-found`, where the reader shows it holds the history. On `hash` the payment presented is the transaction the credential names. The case that cannot arise: *at coverage none, a lookup read whole beside an item the search could not decode*, since each read reads the transaction the claim names and makes no search.
