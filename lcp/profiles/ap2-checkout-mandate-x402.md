**LCP profile `ap2/checkout-mandate/x402`: an AP2 checkout paid with AP2's x402 payment credential, the ATR hash in the merchant's `checkout_jwt`.** This profile targets AP2 v0.2 (Checkout Mandates, `mandate.checkout.1`) and AP2's reference x402 flow at commit `e1ea56d` (`google-agentic-commerce/AP2`, `code/samples/python/src/roles/`).

1. H rides as `ap2/checkout-mandate` places it: the payload of the merchant-signed `checkout_jwt` carries `"legalContext":{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`, and the closed Checkout Mandate commits to that `checkout_jwt` through `checkout_hash`. Every rule of that profile holds here. The checkout's `accepted_payment_methods` names the x402 method, `{"type":"x402","address":P,"network":N}`, as AP2's merchant writes it (`merchant_agent_mcp/server.py:755-768`): P is the account the payment pays.
2. The payment credential is AP2's x402 bundle, `{"payment_mandate_chain":C,"payment_nonce":n,"eip_3009_payload":{"signature":S,"authorization":{"from","to","value","validAfter","validBefore","nonce"}}}` (`x402_credentials_provider_mcp/server.py:205-219`): an EIP-3009 `TransferWithAuthorization` the payer signs, whose `nonce` is keccak-256 of the UTF-8 text of C, the payment mandate chain (`:162-163`). AP2's payment processor refuses any other nonce (`x402_psp_mcp/server.py:147-157`). So the payment the payer signs carries no H, and nothing a public ledger records of it carries H: the pairing is at coverage `none`, with its link in the mandate. An agreement carrying H, a payment of an x402 or MPP pairing whose payment is itself a public proof, is made first, and the merchant initiates this payment only once that agreement has landed.
3. What a payment shows: the closed Checkout Mandate commits to a `checkout_jwt` carrying H; the chain's closed Payment Mandate names that checkout, its `transaction_id` being the same hash of the `checkout_jwt` as the checkout mandate's `checkout_hash` (AP2 `docs/ap2/specification.md:154-155`, "bound to a particular Checkout using the cryptographic hash of the Checkout JWT"); and the bundle's authorization is bound to that chain by its nonce. Any holder can follow the chain: the authorization's nonce to the chain, the chain to `transaction_id`, `transaction_id` to the `checkout_jwt`, and the `checkout_jwt` to H. It does not show that the account that signed the agreement is the account that signed this authorization.
4. `bound` takes `{checkout_mandate, checkout_jwt, payment}`, `payment` being the bundle as the merchant receives it. It reads H as `ap2/checkout-mandate` does, from the mandate and the `checkout_jwt` presented beside it. It then refuses, `ap2/payment-not-bound`, a bundle whose authorization's `nonce` is not keccak-256 of the UTF-8 text of its `payment_mandate_chain`, compared as 32 bytes (AP2's payment processor compares the string `nonce.hex()` writes, `x402_psp_mcp/server.py:147-157`, so a nonce written with `0x` or in upper case, which lcp admits, that processor refuses), and a bundle whose chain does not name this checkout: the chain is read as the checkout mandate is, its last hop's disclosures resolved, and exactly one object of `vct` `mandate.payment.1` must name a `transaction_id` equal to the checkout mandate's `checkout_hash`. AP2 has the merchant's processor verify that the payment credential is scoped to the checkout (`specification.md:340-341`) and its credential provider compare `transaction_id` with the hash of the `checkout_jwt` (`x402_credentials_provider_mcp/server.py:138-139`); AP2's sample processor checks the open mandate's checkout hash alone (`x402_psp_mcp/server.py:134-136`), so without this check a bundle issued for another checkout, or a chain naming none, would answer with this sale's H. A chain that is not an SD-JWT presentation of at most two hops, or whose last hop holds two closed Payment Mandates, is `ap2/payment-malformed`. It verifies no signature: the mandates' signatures are the Credential Provider's checks, as AP2 defines, and the authorization's signature is the payment processor's.
5. `reference` takes the bundle with the payment method it answers, `{"type":"x402","address":P,"network":N,"asset":T}`: AP2's method as its merchant writes it, with T, the token under whose EIP-712 domain the authorization is signed, which AP2's method does not name (AP2's sample signs under USDC on Base Sepolia, its `DEFAULT_USDC_CONTRACT`, `x402_credentials_provider_mcp/server.py:173-178`), so the merchant adds it from its own configuration of the method, as x402's option names it `asset`. N is an `eip155` CAIP-2 id, or an x402 v1 EVM network name as AP2 writes it (`base-sepolia`), which `reference` names in CAIP-2 by the chain id `@x402/evm` 2.31.0 maps it to (`EVM_NETWORK_CHAIN_ID_MAP`, exported from `@x402/evm/v1`); a method without T, or with a network that is neither, is `ap2/option-malformed`. The reference holds the keys `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009`'s reference gives for the same authorization under an option of that network, token and payee: the network, the token, `validBefore`, the digest of `from`, `to` and `value`, the token's `AuthorizationUsed` and `Transfer` logs and the search by the nonce, the authorization's nonce standing where that pairing has H; the payee P; and the authorization's members as presented, `from`, `to`, `value`, `validAfter`, `validBefore` and `nonce`, none judged against the method. So this pairing's `status` (rule 8) reads the payment as the carried pairing reads an x402 EIP-3009 payment, the authorization's nonce being its single-use value: whoever reads the payment back finds it by the token's `AuthorizationUsed(from, nonce)`.
6. The merchant forwards the bundle to its payment processor, which submits the authorization (`merchant_agent_mcp/server.py:981-995`; `x402_psp_mcp/server.py`): the payee broadcasts.
7. The ATR's `ap2` slot names the checkout's `id`, as on `ap2/checkout-mandate`.
8. `status` reads the payment as `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009`'s rules 12 to 20 read it, the authorization's own nonce standing where that pairing has H, at coverage `none`: so the act is the payer's use of that nonce whatever it carries, and a `failed` answer does not wait for the search to be read whole. The payment is no agreement, and its nonce has no agreement's value. The read kit runs its cases through this `status` on an EIP-3009 ledger. Declared inapplicable to the read kit, each with its reason:
    - `transaction-failed`, and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*: a reverted transaction consumes no authorization nonce.
    - `not-found`, and *at coverage none, a lookup read whole beside an item the search could not decode*: the read holds its single-use value and reads it at the `finalized` block and at the latest block, so it answers `instrument-unused` or `instrument-consumed` by that value, which no item the search could not decode changes.
