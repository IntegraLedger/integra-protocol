**LCP profile `x402/upto/xrpl`: x402's `upto` on the XRP Ledger, at coverage `none`, after an agreement on the same network.** x402's `upto` scheme on XRPL as its specification states it (`specs/schemes/upto/scheme_upto_xrpl.md` at x402-foundation/x402 `88b66dd4`, cited below by its lines), with three deviations: the agreement comes first (rule 9), the asset is XRP alone, as the scheme itself has it (rule 5), and nothing the payer signs carries H, so the pairing is at coverage `none` with no link (rule 7).

**The scheme.**

1. The payer opens a payment channel to the option's `payTo` with a `PaymentChannelCreate` it submits itself, its `Amount` the option's `amount`, the maximum, in drops, with a `CancelAfter` and a `SettleDelay`, and then signs one claim off the ledger over the channel's id and that maximum (`:25`, `:29-31`, `:77-80`). The channel's id is the `PayChannel` ledger entry's index: the SHA-512Half of the space key `0x0078`, the creating account's AccountID, the destination's AccountID and the create's `Sequence`, or its `TicketSequence` where it signs `Sequence` 0, as a big-endian 32-bit number (`:56`; `xrplChannelId`). The claim signs the four bytes `CLM\0` (`0x434C4D00`), the 32-byte channel id and the maximum as a big-endian unsigned 64-bit number of drops (`:251-254`; `xrplClaim`). Neither carries a network, so one account pair at one sequence number has one channel id on every network (`:55-60`): a read is given the network and reads that network's ledger alone.
2. The payment presented, in `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`, carries in its `payload` the channel's id as 64 hexadecimal characters (`channelId`), the maximum the claim signs (`maxAmount`), the claim's signature (`signature`), the channel's `PublicKey` (`publicKey`) and the channel's `Account` (`payer`) (`:152-188`). It names a channel and a payer and no transaction. `payload.settlementTransaction` is the resource server's, added at settlement, and is absent from what the client presents (`:190-199`). The payer's client submits the create before it presents the payment, so the pairing's `broadcaster` answers `payer`.
3. The payer pays the create's fee and holds the channel's owner reserve while it is open; the `payTo` account pays the settlement's fee, so the option's `extra.areFeesSponsored` is present and `false` (`:27`, `:33-37`, `:148`, `:218-224`). The payee's precondition, which no check of lcp's reads: an account at `payTo`, funded for the settlement's fee, whose key signs the settlement.
4. The resource server names the charge. Once its metered work has run, it signs a `PaymentChannelClaim` from `payTo` for the actual amount, with `tfClose`, and a settler verifies it and submits it (`:85-91`, `:317-353`); the settler holds no key and pays no fee (`:224`, `:485-488`). That claim moves money under terms already bound: no claim is read under this profile, and no claim is the sale's instrument.

**The option.**

5. The option is `upto` on an XRPL network, `xrpl:` and the network's id in its canonical form (`:43-53`). Its `asset` is `XRP`: a payment channel holds XRP alone, and the ledger refuses a create whose `Amount` is an issued currency (`:62-66`, `:497-501`); an `upto` option on an XRPL network that names another asset is refused `xrpl/currency-not-xrp`. It is refused `x402/option-malformed` where its `amount` is not a positive number of drops in canonical decimal below 10^17, its `payTo` is not a classic address whose checksum holds, its `maxTimeoutSeconds` is not a positive safe integer, its `extra` is not an object whose `areFeesSponsored` is `false`, or its `extra.minSettleDelay` or `extra.validAfter` is present, not `null` and not a non-negative integer, `minSettleDelay` being at most 4,294,967,295, the largest `SettleDelay` a create carries, a UInt32 field (ripple-binary-codec 2.11.0 `dist/enums/definitions.json` L552-558) (`:144-150`, `:236-243`). `advertise`, `bound` and `reference` refuse such an option with its code, and `read` offers none of them, before anything moves.
6. `advertise` places `extensions.legalContext`, as every x402 pairing does, and writes nothing into the option, which carries no H. The resource server places the option only once the agreement for its H is recorded (rule 9): a response sent before then carries no `upto` option on XRPL for that H.

**What the server refuses.**

7. `bound` holds the presented `accepted` to the option filter (rule 5) and the payload to its form, `payload.channelId` 64 hexadecimal characters and `payload.payer` a classic address whose checksum holds, else `x402/payload-malformed`. Nothing the payer signs carries H, neither the create nor the claim (`:523-526`), so `bound` then refuses every payment, `x402/no-signed-place`: the resource server's check of H is its own record that the agreement for this H is recorded, made before anything moves.
8. A settler's `/verify` makes the scheme's checks (`:229-315`): among them that the `PayChannel` is in a validated ledger, that its `Destination` is `payTo` and its `Account` is `payload.payer`, that `payload.maxAmount` is the option's `amount` and at most the channel's `Amount`, that the claim's signature verifies against the channel's `PublicKey`, that the channel's `Balance` is 0, and its time bounds; and at `/settle`, the bindings of the resource server's claim (`:355-410`). lcp makes none of these checks: they judge what the payment pays.

**The agreement first.**

9. The sale's agreement is an XRPL payment carrying H, on the channel's own network, recorded before the `upto` option is placed. The channel counts for the sale only where its create comes after the validated ledger index the resource server read for that network when it recorded the agreement, which it gives the read as the position the create must come after; and only where the channel's `Account` is the account that signed the agreement, which the resource server compares, the read naming the create's signer. A channel created at or before that position is never the sale, whatever follows: its read answers `failed`, `before-position`. A channel opened before the agreement, the payer's own included, stays the payer's until it closes or its `CancelAfter` passes.

**The reference.**

10. `reference` gives the network, the channel `payload.channelId` names, in upper case, the payer `payload.payer` names, and the option's payee, the `payTo` of the option it is given, the key of the read's two party tests and no search key. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not that option, compared by RFC 8785 form (`x402/accepted-not-option`). The ledger index the create must come after (rule 9) is the caller's.

**The read.** `status` reads a rippled or Clio server through the caller's reader (`server_info`, `account_tx`, `ledger_entry`), each `ledger_entry` call naming its ledger, `validated`, never the server's default, which is the current, in-progress ledger on a rippled server (XRPL's documentation, *Specifying Ledgers*); it compares no amount, asset or time with the option. It is the read the profile `mpp/session/hedera-solana-xrpl` states for the stock XRPL session's channel, the payer being the one `payload.payer` names.

11. **Marks.** One mark, `validated`, the final mark. A create the server answers in a ledger it has not validated was read at `current`, which is no mark: an act there is `pending`, `below-mark`. The position is the ledger index.
12. **Found by the channel.** The read takes the `PayChannel` at the validated ledger (`ledger_entry`), then searches the `account_tx` of the channel's `Account`, or, where the validated ledger does not show the channel, of the payer the payment names, from the ledger the create must come after to the latest ledger the server serves, at most 16 pages a call, for the `PaymentChannelCreate` whose metadata creates the channel's entry, or, where the metadata names none, whose `Account`, `Destination` and sequence number or ticket give the channel's id. The create's `Sequence` or ticket is the single-use value: only that create made the channel.
13. **The act.** That create is the act where its `Account` is the payer and its `Destination` the option's payee: `settled`. One another account opened, whose create is not the payer's act, since only that account's sequence number gives the channel's id, is `failed`, `instrument-consumed`, named with its signer; one the payer opened to another account pays no deposit to the payee and is `failed`, `instrument-consumed`; one found at or before the position is `failed`, `before-position`. A create whose `Account` or `Delegate` is the option's payee is never the act.
14. **What moved, and who signed.** What moved is the create's `Amount`, drops of XRP, recorded with the `Destination` it names as observed: a `PaymentChannelCreate` makes one deposit, so its place is the whole transaction. A later `PaymentChannelFund` raises the channel's `Amount` (`:247-250`) and is not read. The signer is the create's `Account`, or its `Delegate` where one acts; its `Signers`, a `Delegate` or an inner `Batch` transaction's parent is recorded as the authority.
15. **The payment presented.** A `settled` answer states `presented`: the channel and the payer the payment names compared with the create the read settled on, `yes` where that create's metadata made that channel's `PayChannel` entry and its `Account` is that payer, the channel's id being the ledger's own digest over the create's account, destination and sequence number or ticket. No claim is a member. The cost: the read finds the create by the channel the payer named, so `presented` is `yes` on every `settled` answer, and it shows nothing of what the claim later collects.
16. **Absence.** A channel the validated ledger shows whose create the search, read whole, does not hold was created at or before the position only if `account_tx` lists every transaction of the account, a delegated create and an inner `Batch` create among them, which no run has yet shown: until a run shows it, the read answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, the search's position held where it began, where it would answer `failed`, `before-position`. On a server whose `complete_ledgers` does not hold the range, or that refuses `account_tx` for ledgers it does not hold (`x402/exact/xrpl`, rule 14), the answer is `unreadable`, `history-gap`, never absence. A channel the validated ledger does not show whose create the payer's search, read whole, does not hold is `pending`, `not-found`: it is not yet created, its create has not yet validated, or it was opened by another account, or by the payer at or before the position, and has since closed. The costs: until that run a channel created at or before the position whose create the search does not find stays `pending`; and a channel opened at or before the position, or by another account, and closed since is answered `not-found` where one the ledger shows answers `failed`.
17. **No recover.** Nothing on the ledger carries H, so the pairing has no `recover`.

**The read kit.**

18. Declared inapplicable to the read kit: *an act read at the finality asked for, below the final mark*, since a seller names `validated` alone (rule 11); *each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place in it*, since each transaction the read takes holds one transfer, a `PaymentChannelCreate` making one deposit (rule 14); and `instrument-live`, `instrument-unused` and `transaction-failed`, since a create whose metadata creates the channel's entry has landed and succeeded (rule 12). A case is named by its reason, or by the opening words of its text in `read-status.json`. The case `not-found` is not declared: it runs through this read, a channel the validated ledger does not show whose create the payer's search, read whole, does not hold (rule 16).

**The vectors.**

19. `x402-upto-xrpl.json` holds the scheme's own option examples (`:110-124`, `:128-142`); each refusal of rules 5 and 7, a good option or payment with one member changed and the code expected; the create, by `Sequence` and by ticket, its blob encoded by ripple-binary-codec 2.11.0 and signed with @noble/curves 2.4.0's Ed25519 under keys made by XRPL's Ed25519 key derivation, and its channel id by the `PayChannel` ID format over node:crypto's SHA-512; the claim's bytes, ripple-binary-codec's `encodeForSigningClaim`, and the payer's signature over them; the reference, with an `accepted` that is not the option; and the read's answers on a constructed server, written from this profile and the profile `read-status`: the payer's create settled, `presented` `yes`, by sequence number and by ticket, and once the channel has closed; a payer that is not the create's `Account`; a channel to another destination; a create at the position; a channel the ledger shows whose create the search does not hold; a server that does not hold the range; a channel the ledger does not show; and a create in a ledger not yet validated. No published software implements the scheme (`@x402/xrpl` 2.30.0 ships `exact` alone), so no vector is a stock client's output, and none is taken from lcp's builder; a refusal's code has no outside oracle and is this profile's, over the scheme's members.

**What a record proves.** An account signed an agreement carrying H on the channel's network before the channel was opened; the create, in a validated ledger with `tesSUCCESS`, shows that the account `payload.payer` names deposited the create's `Amount` into the named channel, whose beneficiary is its `Destination`. It does not show that the account that signed the agreement opened the channel unless the record compares the two, nor what any claim later collected, nor that the amount, the deposit, the destination or the timing match the ATR's content.
