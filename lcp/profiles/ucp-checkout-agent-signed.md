**LCP profile `ucp/checkout/agent-signed`: the ATR hash in a member of the platform's signed request.** This profile targets UCP `v2026-08-25` (`Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp`, tag `v2026-08-25`, `docs/specification/signatures.md`), and is the `ucp/checkout/legal-context` profile with one rule added.

1. The checkout response carries the `legal_context` link of `ucp/checkout/legal-context`, and every rule of that profile holds.
2. The platform's request that completes the checkout carries, at the top level of its JSON body, the member `"legal_context":{"type":"sha256","value":H}`, H being the hash of the checkout's link as 32 decoded bytes, written as `0x` and 64 lowercase hex digits. The member belongs to the extension `com.integraledger.lcp.legal_context`, which the business and the platform both declare in their profiles, as UCP has vendors name their extensions.
3. The platform signs the request with HTTP Message Signatures (RFC 9421), as UCP has platforms sign their requests (`signatures.md:424-460`, `:861-866`): a request with a body carries `Content-Digest: sha-256=:B:`, B being the standard base64 of the SHA-256 of the body's raw bytes (RFC 9530; `signatures.md:410-421`), and the signature covers `content-digest`. UCP's request verification requires `content-digest` among the covered components of a request with a body, checks the header against the body's bytes, and verifies the signature with the key the platform's profile publishes (`signatures.md:704-730`). A platform that does not sign its requests uses `ucp/checkout/unsigned`.
4. What is presented to lcp is `{body, contentDigest}`: the request body as received, as text, at most 1 MiB, and the value of its `Content-Digest` field. `bound` refuses a `contentDigest` that is not exactly `sha-256=:` followed by the base64 of the SHA-256 of the body's UTF-8 bytes and `:`, the comparison UCP's verifier makes, `ucp/content-digest-mismatch`; then reads H from the body's one `legal_context` member. It verifies no signature and reads no key: the business verifies the platform's signature as UCP defines before it takes the request, and so `bound` reads H only from bytes that signature covers.
5. The business refuses a request whose H it did not issue for that checkout, or has seen claimed. The H in the request shows that the platform's key signed a request naming the ATR; it does not show that the account that signed the agreement holds that key, nor that the buyer approved the request.
6. The business's payment handler executes the payment: the payee broadcasts. Nothing here reads a card or handler payment, and nothing the payer signs on a public ledger carries H, so the pairing is at coverage `none`: an agreement carrying H, a payment of an x402 or MPP pairing whose payment is itself a public proof, is made first, and the business completes the checkout only once that agreement has landed.
7. **What a record proves**, as each pairing's `data.proves` states it; a record fills in the values set in angle brackets:
   - `ucp/checkout/agent-signed`: An account signed an agreement transaction carrying this ATR's hash on `<network>`, recorded in `<transaction>`, before this payment was taken or reported. This shows neither that the account is this payment's payer nor when this payment was taken, which nothing here reads. The checkout response carried this ATR's hash and link as its legal_context link, and the platform's request that completed the checkout carried the hash in its legal_context member, with a Content-Digest that is the SHA-256 of that request's body. The platform's RFC 9421 signature over the request, which UCP has the business verify with the key in the platform's profile, was not verified here, and nothing ties that key to the account that signed the agreement. The seller reported the payment. This does not show that amount, payee or timing match the ATR's content.
