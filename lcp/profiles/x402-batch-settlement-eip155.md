**LCP profile `x402/batch-settlement/eip155`: the ATR hash as the channel configuration's salt, or, in the scheme's refund-authorizer mode, as the deposit authorization's salt.**

This pairing serves x402's `batch-settlement` scheme on an EVM chain through x402's channel contract (`0x4020074e9dF2ce1deE5A9C1b5c3f541D02a10003`). H is the channel configuration's salt, which the channel id commits to and every signature of the channel signs; in the scheme's refund-authorizer mode, whose channel salt carries the refund authorizer, H is the salt of the opening deposit's token authorization, which the payer signs. Either way the payer's own signed act binds all of H, so the pairing is at coverage `full`. The opening deposit is the payment; every later credential of the channel is within it, or its close, and no agreement is made on it.

**The payment**

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`. H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes, as `0x` lowercase hex, and L is an `https` URL that serves those bytes.
2. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client opens a new channel whose `ChannelConfig.salt` = H, deposits into it, and echoes `extensions` unchanged. In the refund-authorizer mode (rule 7) it opens a new channel whose salt packs the refund authorizer, and signs the deposit with H as the authorization's salt. The opening's `build` gives, beside its two signing requests, `config`, the `ChannelConfig` whose `batchChannelId` under the requests' chain id is the `channelId` both sign, so a client shows the receiver and the authorizers they grant and holds the channel id to them. It also gives `authSalt`, the deposit authorization's salt (the caller's, or H in the refund-authorizer mode): the ERC-3009 deposit's nonce is `erc3009DepositNonce(channelId, authSalt)`, the Permit2 deposit's nonce `authSalt` as a uint256.
4. The server refuses an opening whose salt is not an H it issued for that request and has not seen claimed, or whose `channelConfig` does not hash to `voucher.channelId`; in the refund-authorizer mode, one whose deposit authorization's salt, or Permit2 nonce, is not such an H, or whose channel salt does not end in the refund authorizer it announced.
5. H is on chain as the salt in `ChannelCreated(channelId, config)` from `x402BatchSettlement`; the deposit authorization and every voucher sign `channelId`, which commits to H. In the refund-authorizer mode H is in the deposit call's collector data, and the token's `AuthorizationUsed` nonce, `keccak256(abi.encode(channelId, H))`, or Permit2's nonce H, is what the payer's signature consumed.
6. x402 says the salt *"Differentiates channels with identical parameters"*; H is unique per ATR, so it keeps that role. One ATR covers the whole channel; a new agreement opens a new channel. A client that draws its own salt is refused by rule 4. In the refund-authorizer mode the salt's 12 bytes of entropy keep that role, and the payer's signature over H binds the channel's one agreement at its opening.
7. The filter takes a zero price, and the opening's `build` refuses it: on the channel a deposit opens, the scheme's server requires the opening voucher to claim 0 and its facilitator requires more than 0. The deposit is an ERC-3009 `ReceiveWithAuthorization` whose nonce is `keccak256(abi.encode(channelId, authSalt))`, or a Permit2 permit whose nonce the payment's `permit2Authorization.nonce` gives. lcp writes the channel salt as H and reads H there, with the caller's `authSalt` as the authorization's salt or the permit's nonce. **The refund-authorizer mode** is an option whose `extra.voucherManager` is `"facilitator"` and whose `extra.refundAuthorizer` is an address other than the zero address, as `@x402/evm` 2.31.0's client marks it (`buildChannelConfig`, `dist/esm/chunk-AEYKXGQA.mjs:232-250`) and its server announces it (`batch-settlement/server/index.mjs:2412-2423`). In that mode the channel salt is `bytes12(entropy) ‖ bytes20(refundAuthorizer)`, the entropy's first 12 bytes or, where those are all zero, its last 12, as that client packs it (`packRefundAuthorizerSalt`, `dist/esm/chunk-7DJ4WEQ5.mjs:218-223`); lcp's `build` takes the caller's `authSalt` as the entropy, and writes H as `erc3009Authorization.salt`, so that the ERC-3009 nonce is `keccak256(abi.encode(channelId, H))`, or as the Permit2 permit's nonce. `bound` and `reference` read H there, and refuse a channel salt that does not end in the option's refund authorizer (`x402/channel-id-mismatch`). A later payment of such a channel carries no H, which only the opening's deposit authorization carries: `channel.boundWithin` refuses it (`x402/not-bound-within`), and `buildWithin` holds the channel's salt to the refund authorizer in H's place. An option whose `extra.refundAuthorizer` is present and is not an address is refused (`x402/option-malformed`). The option alone marks the mode: nothing the payer signs does, so a payment presented under an option of the other mode is read in that mode, and its server refuses what it reads there by rule 4.

**The reference**

8. `reference` of an opening gives the network, the deposit method (`eip3009` or `permit2`), the token, the channel's id and its configuration whole (payer, payer authorizer, receiver, receiver authorizer, withdrawal delay, salt), the deposit authorization's nonce, its `validBefore` or `deadline` and the deposit's amount, H, in the refund-authorizer mode the option's refund authorizer, the option's payee and the transaction a settler names, a hint. It refuses a payment that is not an opening (`x402/not-an-opening`) and one whose `accepted` is not the option it is given, compared by RFC 8785 form (`x402/accepted-not-option`), after the payment's own refusals and the option's payee's. `status` reads a reference only where its configuration hashes to its channel id, its salt is H or, in the refund-authorizer mode, ends in its refund authorizer with its nonce H's, and its network is `eip155:` and a chain id; any other reference is `unreadable`, `unparseable`.

**The read**

`status` reads through a JSON-RPC endpoint the caller supplies: receipts, transactions, `eth_call`, blocks by mark with their timestamps, and `eth_getLogs`.

9. **The single-use value** is the deposit authorization's nonce, read at the `finalized` block and at the latest block: `authorizationState(payer, nonce)` on the token, or the payer's Permit2 bit. It is a digest over the channel id (on Permit2 in the refund-authorizer mode, H itself, in the one nonce space Permit2 keeps for the payer), and the read defines no search for H: every answer that would be `failed` is `pending`, `search-incomplete`.
10. **Found by.** The channel contract's `ChannelCreated` logs, by topic, each tested by its configuration's salt, and its `Deposited` logs of the reference's channel, from the block after the position given to the latest head, in pages as `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009` reads them. The receipt of a `ChannelCreated` is read only where its configuration's salt is H or its data is not the seven words of a configuration, so that a read fetches no receipt of another channel's opening. A `Deposited` into the reference's channel is the opening where its transaction consumed the deposit authorization's nonce: the token's `AuthorizationUsed(payer, nonce)` on ERC-3009, or on Permit2 the nonce in the transaction's input with the payer's bit set at its block and unset before. Any other deposit into the channel is a later credential of it, an act of another kind. A `ChannelCreated` of another channel whose salt is H is an opening of another channel carrying H, with the first `Deposited` of that channel after it as what it moved. In the refund-authorizer mode the reference's own channel salt carries no H, and its opening is the `Deposited` that consumed the deposit authorization's nonce; another channel's opening whose salt is H still carries H, and is read so. A log of the contract the read cannot decode holds the search. A reverted transaction consumed nothing: the read finds the inline channel configuration whose last word is the salt in its input and answers a failed result naming the account its `receiver` names.
11. **Who signed, the account, and what moved.** The channel's payer signed its salt and the deposit authorization; the deposit counts where the channel's `receiver` is the option's payee. The payer, the receiver and the token are the channel's as landed: those of the channel's `ChannelCreated` in the transaction, where the deposit opened it, and otherwise those of the reference's configuration, which hashes to the channel id the landed `Deposited` names. The channel contract takes a deposit through any collector and checks only that its own balance rose by the amount, so the opening is its payer's act by the transaction's use of the deposit authorization's nonce, and any other deposit, another channel's opening whose salt is H or a later deposit into the reference's channel, is its payer's act only where the transaction holds the payer's transfer of its amount, a `Transfer` emitted by the channel's token from the channel's payer to the ERC-3009 deposit collector or to the channel contract (the two collectors the scheme names move only what the payer signed); without it the act names no signer, and is a foreign item of another signer. What moved is the deposit's amount, the first word of its `Deposited` log, to that receiver, named by the transaction and the log's place among the receipt's logs: the channel contract records a deposit as one event naming one amount. On Arc the system emitter's `Transfer` log is never read.
12. **The payment presented** is the deposit into the channel: `presented` is `yes` where the act's channel id and amount equal the presented opening's, and `no` where one differs.
13. **Lapse.** The deposit authorization's `validBefore` (ERC-3009) or `deadline` (Permit2); the nonce unconsumed at a `finalized` block past it is not answered `failed` while no search for H is defined (rule 9). **Below the position given.** Where the nonce reads consumed and the search has found no transaction that consumed it, the read reads the nonce at the position given. Consumed there, it was consumed at or before that position: the read finds the first block at which it reads consumed, halving the range from the network's first block, reads in that block the channel contract's `ChannelCreated` and `Deposited` logs whose topic 1 is the reference's channel id, and classifies their transactions as rule 10 do. One that consumed the nonce answers `failed`, `before-position`, naming it, the one `failed` this read answers; none found answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, since no search after the position given that met none shows a transaction at or before it. The cost: one read of the nonce at the position given and one for each halving, each needing an endpoint that holds that block's state (one that does not is `unreadable`, `history-gap`), and one block's logs.
14. **Marks.** `latest`, `safe` and `finalized`; final `finalized`.
15. **`recover`** takes the transaction and the option, and finds the opening: the one `ChannelCreated` log of the channel contract in the transaction whose data hashes to its id, with the payer's transfer of the deposit, the channel's `Deposited` and a `Transfer` of its amount emitted by the channel's token from the channel's payer to the ERC-3009 deposit collector (`0x4020806089470a89826cB9fB1f4059150b550004`) or, through Permit2, to the channel contract. The channel contract takes a deposit through any collector; the two the scheme names move only what the payer signed. It returns that channel's salt; under an option in the refund-authorizer mode, whose channel salt must end in the option's refund authorizer, it returns the salt, or the permit's nonce, of the transaction's one `deposit` call with that channel's configuration through one of the scheme's two deposit collectors, found at any byte offset of its input (collector data `abi.encode(validAfter, validBefore, salt, signature)` or `abi.encode(nonce, deadline, signature, permitData)`, `@x402/evm` 2.31.0 `dist/esm/chunk-W6ON4LG2.mjs:8-31`), counted only where the token logged `AuthorizationUsed(payer, keccak256(abi.encode(channelId, salt)))` in the transaction, or Permit2 set the payer's bit for that nonce at its block and not at the block before. It refuses with `evm/no-channel-created` where there is no such log, `evm/ambiguous` where there are several or several such calls, `evm/no-payer-deposit` without the payer's transfer, `evm/no-deposit-call`, `evm/no-authorization-use` and `evm/nonce-not-used` in the mode, and `evm/reverted` for a reverted transaction, each only at or below the `finalized` mark.

**What a record proves**

16. That the payer signed a token authorization for a deposit into an x402 batch-settlement channel whose id, the EIP-712 hash of the channel's configuration, commits to this ATR's hash as the configuration's salt, or, in the refund-authorizer mode, whose own salt is this ATR's hash; that the token contract or Permit2 verified that signature when the channel contract collected the deposit; that the hash is on chain as the salt in the channel's `ChannelCreated` event, or in the mode in the deposit call; and that each voucher in this channel signs a commitment to the channel, and so, outside that mode, to the hash. This does not show that the amount, payee, asset or timing match the ATR's content.

**The read kit**

17. Declared inapplicable to the read kit, each with its reason:
    - `transaction-failed`, and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*: a reverted transaction consumes no authorization nonce or Permit2 bit.
    - `not-found`: the read holds its single-use value.
    - `instrument-unused` and `instrument-consumed`: the single-use value is a digest over the channel id, or on Permit2 in the refund-authorizer mode H in the one nonce space Permit2 keeps for the payer, and the read defines no search for H (rule 9).
    - *An act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*: every log and receipt the read meets names its block.
    - *A read that did not read the value at the latest head*: the read reads the value at a `finalized` block and at the latest block.
    - *Each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place*: the channel contract records each opening as one `Deposited` naming one amount.
