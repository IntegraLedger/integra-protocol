**LCP profile `x402/exact/eip155/permit2`: the ATR hash as the Permit2 nonce.**

This pairing serves x402's `exact` scheme on an EVM chain through Permit2 and x402's exact Permit2 proxy, as lcp's client writes it. H is the Permit2 nonce the payer signs, all of H, so the pairing is at coverage `full`. The stock client draws the nonce at random and writes no H.

**The payment**

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`. H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes, as `0x` lowercase hex, and L is an `https` URL that serves those bytes.
2. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client signs the scheme's `PermitWitnessTransferFrom` with `nonce` = H as a uint256, `spender` x402's exact Permit2 proxy (`0x402085c248EeA27D92E8b30b2C58ed07f9E20001`), `deadline` the signing time plus the option's `maxTimeoutSeconds`, and the witness `{to: payTo, validAfter: 0}`, and echoes `extensions` unchanged. The option filter refuses an option whose `extra.splits` is present and not empty, since `exact` pays one payee (`x402/splits-not-carried`). `bound` reads the nonce in decimal or as `0x` and 64 hex digits, and refuses another spender (`x402/spender-not-proxy`) and a nonce in neither form (`x402/nonce-malformed`).
4. The server refuses a payment whose nonce is not an H it issued for that request and has not seen claimed.
5. H is in the settlement transaction's calldata; Permit2 records its use as bit `H & 0xff` of `nonceBitmap(owner, H >> 8)`, one bitmap per owner for every token, and no event carries it: x402's Permit2 proxies emit `Settled()` and `SettledWithPermit()`, which carry nothing, and Permit2's signature transfer emits no event of its own.
6. x402 states no Permit2 nonce derivation. H is unpredictable before issue and unique per ATR, so Permit2's single use of (owner, nonce) holds. A client that draws a random nonce is refused by rule 4.

**The agreement**

7. The agreement's nonce is the agreement's own value as a uint256: SHA-256 over the 16 ASCII bytes `lcp-agreement-v1` (hex `6c63702d61677265656d656e742d7631`) followed by H's 32 bytes (`read-status.md`, rule 1). For H = SHA-256("abc") the value is `0x424524a7e791dcf7c2e6980bc94582ae2b436d6184873563d03539e97d36a434`, the nonce `29974813443532012023066257594749077332739347346832184257714923838385434305588` (`x402-exact-eip155-permit2.json`, `AG`, computed with GNU coreutils `sha256sum` and `openssl dgst -sha256`). `build(choice, H, "agreement")` signs it; `bound` given the act `agreement` and H returns H for that nonce and refuses any other (`x402/nonce-not-agreement`), and `reference` holds the same; on the act `agreement`, `status` reads that value where this profile reads H. A reference is keyed on H and serves both acts, `status` taking the act's value from H; an agreement's own payment is given to `bound` and `reference` with the act and H, since given neither they take its nonce, the agreement's value, as H.
8. The agreement's floor is 1 base unit: no run has yet shown x402's exact Permit2 proxy taking or refusing a zero amount, so no zero is offered. The party that submits pays the fee; the buyer's one-time approval of Permit2 is the buyer's unless x402's gas-sponsoring extension covers it. It succeeded where the receipt's `status` is 1 and the transaction holds the payer's `Transfer` paired with the proxy call carrying the agreement's value, as rule 11 counts it.

**The reference**

9. `reference` gives the network, the asset, the payer (the permit's owner), the signed `deadline`, H, the members of the payment presented (the permit's `token`, `amount`, `nonce` and `deadline`, its `spender`, the owner, and the witness's `to` and `validAfter`), the option's payee, and the transaction a settler names, a hint. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not the option it is given, compared by RFC 8785 form (`x402/accepted-not-option`).

**The read**

`status` reads through a JSON-RPC endpoint the caller supplies: receipts, transactions, `eth_call` at a block, blocks by mark with their timestamps, and `eth_getLogs`; where the node no longer holds the Permit2 state of a block the read needs, an archive endpoint, and without one the answer is `unreadable`, `history-gap`. It compares no amount, asset or time with the option.

10. **The single-use value** is the payer's Permit2 bit for the nonce (on an agreement, its value), read by `nonceBitmap(payer, nonce >> 8)` at the `finalized` block and at the latest block. The nonce is H, and Permit2 keeps one bitmap per owner for every token, so the bit is H's own carrier: its two reads are the search for H, read whole (`read-status.md`, rule 26). Where both read it unset, the search is whole with no log read.
11. **Found by.** Where the bit is read set, the read finds the act that set it: every contract's `Transfer` from the payer, and Permit2's `UnorderedNonceInvalidation` with the payer as owner, from the block after the position given to the latest head, in pages as `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009` reads them. A hit counts only where its transaction's input carries the nonce in a call of an x402 Permit2 proxy, found at any byte offset (`settle`, `0x13cd3b53`, and `settleWithPermit`, `0xfa340378`, on the exact proxy; `0xff11e7b4` and `0x016c1748` on the `upto` proxy), whose words name the permit's token, amount, nonce and deadline, the owner, and the witness; where the receipt holds a `Transfer` of that token from the owner to the witness's `to` of the amount the proxy moves, the permit's whole `amount`; and where the owner's bit for the nonce is set at the receipt's block and unset at the block before, since the bitmap shows only that the nonce was used in a block. Each call is tied to its own transfer, one each. One call is taken by owner and nonce: a copy of a call in the same input is that call, and a transaction holding two calls of one owner over one nonce that differ is not read as the act, the search holding on it, as `recover` refuses it (`evm/ambiguous`). An invalidation consumes the nonce where its word and mask cover it and its owner is the payer, an act of another kind that names no account. A reverted transaction's call is a failed result that consumed nothing. A call through the `upto` proxy is an act of another kind. Another owner's proxy call or nonce invalidation over H, and the payer's over the other act's value, in a transaction the search finds, is an act carrying H that consumed nothing, named as a foreign item.
12. **The account an act names, and what moved.** The paired `Transfer`'s `to`, compared with the option's payee; what moved is that transfer, named by the transaction and its log's place among the receipt's logs. The signer of H is the permit's owner, never the transaction's sender.
13. **The payment presented** is what the payer signed, its signature apart: the permit's `token`, `amount`, `nonce` and `deadline`, its spender (the proxy the call's selector names), the owner, and the witness's `to` and `validAfter`. Where the proxy call is the transaction's own (its `to` the proxy, the call at the input's start), its decoded arguments are compared as they stand. Where it is wrapped in another call, the logs and the payer's bit show the token, the owner, `to`, `amount` and the nonce, since the exact proxy moves the permit's whole amount to the witness's `to`; so where the transaction holds exactly one `Transfer` from the payer on the token and exactly one proxy call of the payer's, a wrapped call reads `yes` where those five equal the presented permit's and `no` where one differs, and otherwise `unshown`.
14. **Lapse.** Permit2 refuses a permit once the block's time is past its `deadline` (`SignatureTransfer.sol`, `if (block.timestamp > permit.deadline) revert SignatureExpired`). The bit unset at a `finalized` block whose timestamp is past `deadline`, and at the latest block, is `failed`, `instrument-unused`; before that `pending`, `instrument-live`.
15. **Marks.** `latest`, `safe` and `finalized`; final `finalized`. The answers follow `read-status.md`.

**What a record proves**

16. That the payer signed a Permit2 witness transfer whose nonce is this ATR's hash (or, for an agreement, the agreement's value of it), with x402's exact proxy as spender and the payee in the witness; that Permit2 verified the signature when the proxy executed the transfer; and that the hash is in the settlement transaction's calldata as the Permit2 nonce, no event carrying it; not that the amount, payee, asset or timing match the ATR's content.

**The read kit**

17. Declared inapplicable to the read kit, each with its reason:
    - `transaction-failed`, and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*: a reverted transaction consumes no Permit2 bit.
    - `not-found`: the read holds its single-use value.
    - *An act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*: every log and receipt the read meets names its block.
    - *A read with no means to read the single-use value*, *search-incomplete is answered before not-found* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*: the read reads the value at a `finalized` block and at the latest block, and those reads are the search, read whole.
    - *Each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place*: the proxy moves one transfer.
    - *A search narrowed to one asset, payee or token*, *the validity passed with the value unconsumed and the search for H not read whole*, *instrument-live is answered before search-incomplete*, *a search that met an item it cannot decode*, *the answer names every item the search could not decode*, *an item the search could not decode holds a failed answer*, *the search's range left to read and an item it could not decode*, *an item the caller gave by its transaction holds no search*, *an item the caller gave that the read cannot decode, beside one* and *instrument-live is answered before an item the search could not decode*: the bit is H's own carrier, so its reads at a `finalized` block and at the latest block are the search, read whole, and meet no item the read cannot decode (rule 10).
