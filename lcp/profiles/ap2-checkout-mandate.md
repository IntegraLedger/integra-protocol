**LCP profile `ap2/checkout-mandate`: the ATR hash in the merchant's `checkout_jwt`.** This profile targets AP2 v0.2 (Checkout Mandates, `mandate.checkout.1`).

1. The payload of the merchant-signed `checkout_jwt` carries `"legalContext":{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}` and a string `id` naming the checkout. H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes, as `0x` lowercase hex, and L is an `https` URL that serves those bytes.
2. The shopping agent fetches L, hashes the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not present the checkout for approval.
3. The closed Checkout Mandate commits to that `checkout_jwt` through `checkout_hash`, as AP2 defines. Nothing is added to the mandate.
4. The merchant refuses a mandate whose `checkout_hash` is not the hash of the `checkout_jwt` it last sent for that checkout, taken from its own records and not from the presentation, or whose `checkout_jwt` carries an H it did not issue for that checkout, or has seen claimed. Compared with the `checkout_jwt` presented beside it, the mandate shows only that the two agree.
5. The ATR's `ap2` slot names the checkout's `id`, and never contains the `checkout_jwt`.
6. AP2 has two ways a payment is made under the mandate (AP2 `e1ea56d`, `docs/ap2/specification.md:203-209`). Where the merchant initiates the charge with its own payment processor, the presentation is the mandate and the `checkout_jwt` beside it, and the merchant's side executes the payment. Where the payment method pushes funds to the merchant, the merchant "will instead receive confirmation of funds sent": the payer's side has sent the payment before the merchant sees it. AP2 gives that confirmation no member of the presentation and no schema of its own; this profile carries it beside the mandate as `payment_receipt`, AP2's Payment Receipt as the payer's side gives it (`code/sdk/schemas/ap2/payment_receipt.json` at `e1ea56d`), a JSON object, carried whole and read by nothing in this pairing. A presentation with `payment_receipt` is push funding, whose payment its payer's side sent; one without it is a charge the merchant initiates. This holds of a presentation made before any charge, as the seller's server makes it before it charges: AP2 returns a Payment Receipt once the processor has accepted or rejected the payment in every flow (`specification.md:159-161`, `:212`), so a presentation made after a charge, which may carry such a receipt, and one whose receipt's `status` is `Error`, are not told apart by it. A `payment_receipt` that is not a JSON object is refused before anything else of the presentation is read. That the confirmation is AP2's Payment Receipt is this profile's reading of AP2's text, which names the confirmation without defining it.
7. **What a record proves**, as each pairing's `data.proves` states it; a record fills in the values set in angle brackets:
   - `ap2/checkout-mandate`: An account signed an agreement transaction carrying this ATR's hash on `<network>`, recorded in `<transaction>`, before this payment was taken or reported. This shows neither that the account is this payment's payer nor when this payment was taken, which nothing here reads. The closed AP2 Checkout Mandate presented carries checkout_hash, the SHA-256 of the checkout JWT presented beside it, and that JWT's payload carries this ATR's hash as its legalContext member. AP2 has the mandate signed by the user's trusted surface, or by the agent under the user's open mandate presented before it in a chain. That signature, the open mandate's signature, its delegation to the agent's key (cnf) and the chain's link were not verified here, and nothing compares the mandate's signer with the account that signed the agreement: AP2 has the Credential Provider verify the Payment Mandate, whose transaction_id is the same hash, before a payment credential issues. The payment, whether the merchant initiated it or the payer's side sent it, is known from the seller's report alone. This does not show that amount, payee or timing match the ATR's content.
