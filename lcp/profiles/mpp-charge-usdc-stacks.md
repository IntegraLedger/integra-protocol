**LCP profile `mpp/charge/usdc/stacks`: the ATR hash as the SIP-010 `transfer` memo of MPP's `usdc` Stacks charge, and the read of the payment by its origin and the nonce the origin signed.** Clauses 1–3 and 5 of `mpp/charge` apply to the challenge. The read's answers and the rules that choose them are the profile `read-status`'s; this profile states how this pairing's read finds what those rules name.

**The payment**

1. SIP-010's `transfer` takes `(memo (optional (buff 34)))`, and the `usdc` Stacks profile requires exactly the Clarity arguments `(uint amount, principal sender, principal recipient, (optional (buff 34)) memo)`. The memo is H's 32 raw bytes: the `lcp:` string is 77 bytes and does not fit.
2. The usdc specification does not fix the memo's value; this profile makes it `some` of H's 32 bytes, serialized `0x0a0200000020` ‖ H.
3. The transaction is a contract call to the profile's token contract named `transfer`, with post-condition mode Deny and anchor mode OnChainOnly, signed by the payer's Stacks wallet. Its chain id is the profile's `chainId`, and its version is that network's, as stacks-core's `validation.rs` requires: 0x00 on chain id 1 (CAIP-2's `stacks:1`, mainnet) and 0x80 on any other.
4. A transaction of 1 to 16,384 bytes is read (`MAX_STACKS_TX_BYTES`, the package's own bound on the work of decoding one). The origin signs a standard authorization, and pays its own fee, or a sponsored one, whose fee a sponsor pays: the sponsor fills its spending condition, which the origin signs in the empty form (P2PKH, a zero signer, nonce and fee, a compressed key encoding and a zero signature), and signs after the origin (`@stacks/transactions` 7.6.0 `dist/esm/authorization.js:220-225`, `:295-301`). Which the payment takes is the resource server's choice in its offer; the binding of H and the read are the same for both. A sponsor's signature changes the transaction's id, so the id of a sponsored payment is fixed only once the sponsor has signed.
5. The settler broadcasts the transaction, sponsoring it where it is sponsored, over the bytes the origin presented. It keys its record of an instrument on the network, the origin, the nonce and the SHA-512/256 of the transaction as the origin presented it, so that two signed bodies at one nonce never share a record: a second body at a used nonce is another instrument, whose read answers `instrument-consumed` naming the transaction that consumed the nonce, and a repeat is the identical bytes, which a settler may sponsor again under a new id. The form has no validity window: a Stacks transaction has no expiry, and a signed payment never broadcast stays payable for as long as the origin leaves its nonce unused.

**The agreement**

6. The payment can itself be the agreement: its floor is 1 base unit of the offer's asset, the `usdc` method asking a positive integer; the fee is the sponsor's on a sponsored authorization and the origin's on a standard one; the transaction must succeed, as rule 9 states; and a transaction whose origin is the option's payee is no agreement and pays nothing, the sponsor's signature being the fee payer's and never the test.

**The reference**

7. `reference` gives, from the presented credential and the challenge it answers: the network; `origin`, the origin's c32 address, its signer's hash160 at its hash mode's version on the transaction's network; `nonce`, the origin's nonce as a decimal string; `payee`, the challenge's `recipient`, the key of the read's two party tests and no search key, which the challenge's filter takes only as a Stacks node writes a principal, c32check in uppercase with no alias digit, so that it is the spelling the endpoint names accounts in (a recipient in any other spelling, lowercase or with `O`, `I` or `L` among them, which c32 decoding reads as another spelling of a canonical principal, is `mpp/request-malformed`); `signed`, the digest of what the origin signed (rule 15); and, on a standard authorization only, `transaction`, the transaction's id, a hint the read takes first. It names no contract and no asset. A credential whose echoed challenge is not the challenge given, member by member, its `request` and `opaque` by their decoded members, is `mpp/challenge-not-option`. The read takes H beside the reference.

**The read**

8. The read goes through a Stacks Blockchain API endpoint (`stacks-api-rest`), stacks-blockchain-api 9.9.0's routes: the transaction by its id with its events and its bytes; the origin's `last_executed_tx_nonce` at the tip or at a block height (`/extended/v1/address/<origin>/nonces`); the lookup by origin and nonce (`/extended/v1/tx/?from_address=<origin>&nonce=<n>`); the origin's transactions in ascending order; a block's transactions (`/extended/v1/tx/block_height/<h>`); the tip's Stacks and Bitcoin heights; and the Bitcoin height of the burn block of the tenure after a block's. The routes of the lookup, the nonce and the block are deprecated upstream, each naming a successor; this profile pins 9.9.0's. A 404 for a height a route does not know is `unreadable`, `history-gap`, never absence.
9. **What succeeded means.** A transaction counts only on the canonical chain and included, with status `success`, `abort_by_response` or `abort_by_post_condition`. It succeeded with status `success`. `abort_by_response` and `abort_by_post_condition` are included with a failed result, the fee paid, and consume the origin's nonce. `problematic_skipped` is not included and consumes nothing; a transaction not on the canonical chain, unmined or dropped decides nothing. None of these is a foreign item.
10. **Marks and positions.** Two marks: `block`, the transaction's block, and `bitcoin`, the final one, once at least 6 Bitcoin blocks stand on the Bitcoin block that anchors the block, the anchoring block among them. The Stacks state as of the start of tenure N is anchored at Bitcoin block N + 1, so the count starts at the Bitcoin block after the burn block of the first tenure that starts after the block's own and builds on it, and never at the block's own tenure's burn block: the block is at `bitcoin` where the tip's Bitcoin height less that burn block's height is at least 6. Stacks' documentation names no depth (*Bitcoin Finality*, *Bitcoin Reorgs*); 6 is Bitcoin's common one. A position is a Stacks block height; a transaction in a microblock no anchor block confirms (`is_unanchored`) has no block that places it.
11. **Which transaction decides.** The origin's transaction at the signed nonce, on the canonical chain and included, decides, read whole by its id with its events; and, for the act asked about, any act of the origin's the search for H (rule 13) finds. The reference's `transaction` is read first and decides only where it is that transaction; any other transaction met decides nothing. The origin is the API's sender, never the sponsor. Where the origin's last executed nonce at the tip, the latest head the endpoint serves, is below the signed nonce, or it has none, the nonce is unconsumed, there and so at the final mark. Where it is at or past the signed nonce, the lookup finds the transaction at that nonce, each of its results read whole; for nonce 0, which the route does not filter, a scan of the origin's transactions in ascending order to the first at a higher nonce. A lookup or scan answer naming another origin's transaction, or for a nonce other than 0 another nonce's, is not the route's documented answer and proves nothing. Every result at a nonce is read: two that decide at one nonce are an answer no chain gives, `unreadable`, `unparseable` at the signed nonce, and items the search could not decode at a nonce of the search (rule 13). Where neither finds it, the fallback bisects the origin's nonce by block height, from the position given, or from height 1 where none is given or the nonce at the position already reaches the signed one, to the first block where it reaches the nonce, and reads every page of that block's transactions for the origin's at the nonce. A nonce read consumed whose transaction none of these finds answers `search-incomplete`, never `not-found` or `failed`.
12. **The forms that carry H.** A transaction carries H in this pairing's form where it calls `transfer` with `(some H)` as its fourth argument, or where it is a token transfer whose 34-byte memo field is H followed by two zero bytes; it carries H in another form where a contract call holds `(some H)` or H as a 32-byte buffer in any other argument or function, which makes it an act of another kind. The account a transaction names is the recipient of the `fungible_token_asset` `transfer` events of the called contract's tokens, the call's recipient and memo arguments only locating them, or the token transfer's recipient: the payee's where one of them is the option's payee, another where none is, and none where it moved nothing. What moved is each such event's asset, amount and recipient, or the token transfer's, with `STX` as its asset, each named by its transaction and its place there, the event's index among the transaction's events as the endpoint lists them, or `payload` for the token transfer, the transaction's own payload; it is recorded as observed and compared with nothing but by its recipient, on a payment. The signer is the origin; an origin that is the option's payee makes the transaction payee-signed, on either act. A transaction included with a failed result moved nothing, so the account it names is the one the origin signed it to pay: the recipient argument of its `transfer` call, or the token transfer's recipient. The origin's transaction at the signed nonce included with a failed result is the act's own failed result, `transaction-failed`, only where it carries H in this pairing's form and, for the act `payment`, names the payee's account; any other, one that carries no H among them, consumed the nonce and is the transaction of an `instrument-consumed` answer (profile `read-status`, rule 20).
13. **The search for H.** Where the transaction at the signed nonce is not the act asked about at the finality asked for, the read searches every nonce the origin executed after the position it starts from, through the last it executed at the tip: each transaction of an origin takes its next nonce, so those are its acts. The search starts at the search position an earlier answer returned, else at the nonce after the one the origin had executed at the height of the position given, else at nonce 0; each nonce is found as rule 11 finds the signed one. It reads at most 32 nonces in one read (`MAX_SEARCH_NONCES`) and answers `search-incomplete` with the next nonce to read as its search position, and stops there too at a nonce whose transaction it does not find. An act of this kind found at another nonce is the act asked about, unless it is an other act the caller gave, by its transaction or by a reference naming its origin and nonce or its id; any other that carries H is a foreign item, with its reason. A transaction a lookup names whose answer the reader gives out of its documented form is an item the search could not decode: the answer names it, the search is held at its nonce, and the search reads on; at the signed nonce, or as the reference's own transaction, which the read names, it is `unreadable`, `unparseable`.
14. **What is not yet shown.** A search from a position given reads its first nonce from the nonce route at that position's height. That the route's `last_executed_tx_nonce` at a height is the last nonce the origin executed at or before that block is read at source (stacks-blockchain-api 9.9.0 `src/api/routes/v1/address.ts:765-772`) and not yet shown by a run, so such a search proves no absence and is never read whole: it answers no `failed` but `before-position` until a run shows the route's answer, and a range it read to its end gives no search position, the next read reading it again from its start. A search from nonce 0, or from a search position, reads no nonce at a height for its range.
15. **The payment presented.** Where the reference holds `signed`, a `settled` answer states `presented`: `yes` where the landed transaction's origin signed what the presented one's did, `no` where it did not, and `unshown` where the reader gives no bytes whose SHA-512/256 is the landed transaction's id; where it holds none, the read makes no comparison and states none. What the origin signed is compared by its digest: SHA-512/256 over the initial signature hash, the SHA-512/256 of the transaction with the origin's spending condition cleared (its nonce and fee zero, a single signature's 65 bytes zero or a multisignature's auth fields an empty list) and, on a sponsored authorization, the sponsor's spending condition in the empty form of rule 4, followed by the flag 0x04 and the origin's fee and nonce, 8 bytes each, big-endian (`@stacks/transactions` 7.6.0 `dist/esm/authorization.js:55-70`, `:178-188`; `dist/esm/transaction.js:30-34`, `:43-51`). So copies of one payment signed by other quorums, or sponsored by another sponsor, read `yes`, and the sponsor's account and fee are no member.

**Declared inapplicable to the read kit**

16. Declared inapplicable to the read kit (profile `read-status`, rule 28): `instrument-unused`, since a Stacks transaction has no expiry; and `not-found`, since the read holds the single-use value, the origin's nonce, and answers it unconsumed `pending`, `instrument-live`, and consumed with its transaction not found `pending`, `search-incomplete` (rule 11). The two are declared by their premises, by the case's words: every case that reads the value with its validity passed, since a Stacks transaction has no expiry; and *a read with no means to read the single-use value* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*, since the read holds the nonce and reads it at the latest head's height (rule 11). The search from a position rests on the nonce read at a height, not yet shown (rule 14), and answers no `failed` but `before-position` until a run shows it; with no position given it starts at nonce 0, so the kit's `failed` cases, which give none, run. This pairing's read-kit form skips those cases and no other, and makes two cases in this ledger's own spelling, where the rule that decides them treats the two alike: a transaction another account signed that consumed the value is the origin's, since only the origin's own transaction takes its nonce (rule 16 of `read-status` decides `before-position` before it asks who signed), and a second act of the payer that meets every test is at another nonce, since two at one nonce are an answer no chain gives (rule 21 asks nothing of the nonce).

**What a record proves**

17. The payer signed a Stacks transaction whose origin is the payer and which carries this ATR's hash as the `(some H)` memo of a SIP-010 `transfer`, or as a token transfer's memo; the chain verified the origin's signature, the transaction executed with status `success` on the canonical chain, and the memo is in the mined transaction on chain. Where it made a transfer, one of zero included, it paid that transfer's recipient, named in the record; the origin, never a sponsor, is the signer. This does not show that the contract is a SIP-010 token, or that the amount, the asset or the timing match the ATR's content.
