**LCP profile `x402/exact/eip155/eip3009`: the ATR hash as the EIP-3009 nonce.**

This pairing serves x402's `exact` scheme on an EVM chain through an EIP-3009 token, as lcp's client writes it. H is the nonce of the `TransferWithAuthorization` the payer signs, all of H, so the pairing is at coverage `full`, and its read finds the payment by H. The stock client (`@x402/evm`) draws the nonce at random and writes no H.

**The payment**

1. The resource server's `PaymentRequired` carries `extensions.legalContext.info` = `{"type":"sha256","value":H,"legalContextUrl":L}`. H is the SHA-256 of the ATR's exact bytes, as `0x` lowercase hex, and L is an `https` URL that serves those bytes.
2. The client fetches L, computes SHA-256 over the bytes received, and compares the result with H as 32 decoded bytes. On a mismatch it does not sign.
3. On a match, the client signs `TransferWithAuthorization` for an `exact` option with `nonce` = H, `validAfter` 0 and `validBefore` the signing time plus the option's `maxTimeoutSeconds`, under the token's EIP-712 domain (`extra.name`, `extra.version`, the chain id, the asset as verifying contract), and echoes `extensions` unchanged.
4. The option filter takes an `exact` option on an `eip155` network with the token's `extra.name` and `extra.version`. It refuses an option whose `extra.name` is `GatewayWalletBatched` or whose `extra.verifyingContract` is present and not the asset, which is Circle's batched payment, signed under the Gateway Wallet's domain and not the token's (`x402/batched-not-eip3009`), and an option whose `extra.splits` is present and not empty, since `exact` pays one payee (`x402/splits-not-carried`). It takes an amount of `"0"`: the token sets no floor, and x402's facilitator compares the authorization's value with the amount by equality.
5. The server refuses a payment whose `nonce` is not an H it issued for that request and has not seen claimed.
6. H is on chain as `topics[2]` of `AuthorizationUsed(authorizer, nonce)`, emitted by the token that consumed the authorization in the settlement transaction. A search by that topic can also return other authorizers' uses of the same value.
7. x402 calls the nonce a *"32-byte random nonce to prevent replay attacks"*. H is unpredictable before issue and unique per ATR, because each ATR carries a random UUID, so the nonce keeps its replay role. A client that does not implement this profile draws a random nonce, and rule 5 refuses its payment.

**The agreement**

8. This pairing's payment can be the agreement itself, an act of its own under the same H. The agreement's nonce is the agreement's own value: SHA-256 over the 16 ASCII bytes `lcp-agreement-v1` (hex `6c63702d61677265656d656e742d7631`) followed by H's 32 bytes, 48 bytes in all, written as H is (`read-status.md`, rule 1). So a payment and an agreement under one H never share a nonce, and neither consumes the other's. For H = SHA-256("abc") the value is `0x424524a7e791dcf7c2e6980bc94582ae2b436d6184873563d03539e97d36a434` (`x402-exact-eip155-eip3009.json`, `AG`, computed with GNU coreutils `sha256sum` and `openssl dgst -sha256`).
9. `build(choice, H, "agreement")` signs that value as the nonce. `bound` of a payment returns its nonce; given the act `agreement` and H, it returns H where the nonce is H's agreement value and refuses any other nonce (`x402/nonce-not-agreement`), and `reference` holds the same. On the act `agreement`, `status` reads the agreement's value where this profile reads H, and `AuthorizationUsed`'s nonce topic is that value. A reference is keyed on H and serves both acts, `status` taking the act's value from H, so the payment's reference asked about the agreement reads the payment as an act of another kind. An agreement's own payment is given to `bound` and `reference` with the act and H: given neither, they take its nonce, the agreement's value, as H, and `status` reads the reference as a payment under that value, whose agreement is no act anyone signed. `recover` returns the nonce the transaction used, which a holder of the ATR compares with H's agreement value.
10. The agreement's floor is 0, where the network's settler takes a zero amount; the party that submits pays the fee, and the buyer needs a key and no funds at zero. It succeeded where the receipt's `status` is 1 and the token it called emitted `AuthorizationUsed` with the buyer as authorizer and the agreement's value as nonce, paired with its `Transfer` as rule 14 pairs them.

**The reference**

11. `reference` gives the network, the asset, the payer (the authorization's `from`), the signed `validBefore`, H, the members of the payment presented (the token, `from`, `to`, `value` and `nonce`, lowercase), the option's payee (the `payTo` of the option it is given, the key of both party tests), and the transaction a settler names, a hint. It refuses a payment whose `accepted` is not that option, compared by RFC 8785 form (`x402/accepted-not-option`).

**The read**

`status` reads through a JSON-RPC endpoint the caller supplies: receipts, transactions, `eth_call`, blocks by mark with their timestamps, and `eth_getLogs`. It compares no amount, asset or time with the option.

12. **The single-use value** is the nonce (on an agreement, its value, rule 8), which the token keeps for each authorizer. The read takes `authorizationState(payer, nonce)` on the asset at the `finalized` block and at the latest block.
13. **Found by.** The search reads the logs of every contract with topics `[AuthorizationUsed, payer, nonce]`, by a filter that names no address, and the other act's nonce the same way, and the asset's `[AuthorizationCanceled, null, nonce]`, from the block after the position the caller gives (or the search position an earlier answer returned) to the latest head. It reads pages of at most 10,000 blocks, halving a page whose range the endpoint refuses; a range refused at one block is `unreadable`, `transport`, never absence. One call reads at most eight pages; a search with range left answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, with the position the next call resumes from. A use on a contract other than the asset counts only where the read verifies the payer's signature over the authorization its transaction carries, as a token verifies it (Circle's FiatTokenV2_2, `SignatureChecker`): by ecrecover to `from` in that contract's EIP-712 domain, the four-member one (its `name()` and `version()`, the chain id and the contract as verifying contract) or the one the contract states by EIP-5267's `eip712Domain()` where that names the contract and the chain, a `salt` included; or by `from`'s ERC-1271 `isValidSignature(digest, signature)` answering the magic value `0x1626ba7e` over either digest, the payer being a contract account. Each is read in the state after the use's block. A use counts for nothing only where it is shown not to be the payer's: the contract's domain is known (stated by `eip712Domain()`, or its `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR()` equal to the four-member one), the 65-byte signature, with a low `s`, recovers to another account in it, and `from` accepts it by no ERC-1271 answer (an account with no code answers none). One the read cannot decode, or cannot verify or show not to be the payer's, holds the search at that transaction (`read-status.md`, rule 19), since the payer's act on another token is the act asked about whatever asset it moved (rule 10), and absence is never inferred from it. A transaction the settler names is read along with the search.
14. **Pairing a use with its transfer.** Where the transaction holds exactly one `AuthorizationUsed` of that authorizer emitted by that contract and at most one `Transfer` from that authorizer emitted by it, the two pair, in whatever order the token logs them, whoever submitted the call and through whatever contract: EIP-3009's reference implementation and Circle's FiatToken emit `AuthorizationUsed` and then the transfer, and EIP-3009 states no order. Otherwise the read decodes the transaction's input: the contract's `transferWithAuthorization` or `receiveWithAuthorization`, in its `v, r, s` or its `bytes` signature form (selectors `0xe3ee160e`, `0xcf092995`, `0xef55bec6`, `0x88b7ab63`), found at any byte offset, whose `from` is the authorizer and whose `nonce` is the use's. Exactly one such call must be there, and the use's transfer is the contract's `Transfer` from `from` to the call's `to` of the call's `value`, not already paired with another use. Where the reader gives no transaction, or the call or the transfer is not there, the transaction holds the search (`x402-exact-eip155-eip3009.json`, `V6.pairing`). The cost: authorizations a contract holds and submits in another encoding are not split, and such a transaction holds its search.
15. **The account an act names, and what moved.** The paired `Transfer`'s `to`, compared with the option's payee; what moved is that transfer's token, `value` and `to`, named by the transaction and its place, the index of its log among the receipt's logs. On Arc each USDC transfer also emits a `Transfer` from the system emitter `0xffffFFFfFFffffffffffffffFfFFFfffFFFfFFfE` in 18 decimals (Arc's documentation, *USDC system events*): the read takes only the asset contract's own `Transfer`, and the system log is never paired, recorded or counted.
16. **Who signed.** The authorization's `from`, the authorizer, is the signer of H, never the transaction's sender, which is a settler. An act whose authorizer is the option's payee, or another account, is a foreign item and settles nothing. A cancellation, the asset's `AuthorizationCanceled(authorizer, nonce)`, is the authorizer's act of another kind that names no account, and consumes the nonce where the authorizer is the payer.
17. **A reverted transaction** consumed no nonce: the read decodes its call carrying the nonce and answers a failed result naming the account the call's `to` names.
18. **The payment presented** is what the payer signed, its signature apart: the token, `from`, `to`, `value` and `nonce`, read from the two logs rule 14 pairs, the token being the contract that emitted both. `presented` on `settled` is `yes` where the five equal the presented authorization's, `no` where one differs, and `unshown` where the read does not show one; the validity window is no member. So a call any holder of the signature wraps in a contract of its own reads `yes`.
19. **Lapse.** The token takes an authorization only while the block's time is below `validBefore` (EIP-3009, `require(now < validBefore)`). The nonce unconsumed at a `finalized` block whose timestamp is at or past `validBefore`, and at the latest block, with the search read whole, is `failed`, `instrument-unused`; before that it is `pending`, `instrument-live`. **Below the position given.** Where the nonce reads consumed and the search has found no transaction that consumed it, the read reads the nonce at the position given. Consumed there, it was consumed at or before that position: the read finds the first block at which it reads consumed, halving the range from the network's first block, reads in that block the asset's `[AuthorizationUsed, payer, nonce]` and `[AuthorizationCanceled, payer, nonce]` logs, and classifies their transactions as rules 14 to 17 do. One that consumed the nonce answers `failed`, `before-position`, naming it; none found answers `pending`, `search-incomplete`, since no search after the position given that met none shows a transaction at or before it. The cost: one read of the nonce at the position given and one for each halving, each needing an endpoint that holds that block's state (one that does not is `unreadable`, `history-gap`), and one block's logs.
20. **Marks.** `latest`, `safe` and `finalized`; the final mark is `finalized`, a state the endpoint reports and not a confirmation count. A network whose endpoints refuse a mark does not take it. The answers follow `read-status.md`: an act found and settled at the mark asked for is `settled`, and `failed` other than `before-position` comes only once the search is read whole (rule 24). The reader holds the history from the position given through its log query; a reader with no log query holds the search open and answers no `failed` that rests on it.

**What a record proves**

21. That the payer signed an EIP-3009 transfer authorization whose nonce is this ATR's hash (or, for an agreement, the agreement's value of it), that the token contract verified that signature when it executed the transfer, and that the hash is on chain as the nonce topic of its `AuthorizationUsed` event in the settlement transaction; not that the amount, payee, asset or timing match the ATR's content.

**The read kit**

22. Declared inapplicable to the read kit, each with its reason:
    - `transaction-failed`, and the cases opening *a failed result that consumed the value*: a reverted transaction consumes no authorization nonce.
    - `not-found`: the read holds its single-use value.
    - *An act the read cannot place against the position given* (its three cases) and *an item the search could not decode that gives no position*: every log and receipt the read meets names its block.
    - *A read with no means to read the single-use value* and *a read that did not read the value at the latest head*: the read reads the value at a `finalized` block and at the latest block.
    - *Each transfer of what moved is named by its transaction and its place*: a use moves one transfer.
    A transaction that consumed the payer's nonce carries an authorization the payer signed, so the read kit's case of one another account signed is made as the payer's.
